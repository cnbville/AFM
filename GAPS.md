# Gaps

Gaps found while fixing bugs or building, so they carry over between threads (Cain, 2026-11-17). Each: what's missing, why it matters, impact. Remove an entry once it's built. Nothing here is agreed until Cain says yes.

## Engine

- **Sacks and tackles for loss a team game are short** (high). After the pass rush lane (realism audit item 3's second lane, 2026-11-25; world seed 321's own teams, `match_check` with `AFM_DEFENDERS` and `AFM_BLITZ=1`, seed 1) FBS defenses get 1.93 sacks and 4.38 tackles for loss a team game (real 2.09 and 5.64; 1.78 and 4.35 after item 3) and the NFL clubs 1.74 sacks (real 2.41; 1.82 after item 3). After the four-man rush lane (2026-11-25) the NFL clubs have 1.76 (1.72 before it) and the FBS teams 1.90 on 3,000 games (1.86; cfbfastR 2.00). After who makes the tackle (2026-11-25) the NFL clubs have 1.59 and the FBS teams 1.81 on 12,000 games: with the real table of backs a base defense is on the field less, and base defenses blitz most (five or more men rush on 26.0% of the NFL clubs' dropbacks, real 25.8%; 31.8% before). Blitzers get home at PFR's pressures a blitz, and the line's pressures a team game are at real in all (edges short, tackles over), so the missing sacks are the sack a pressure makes (the four-man rush's entry and the one after it). On the NFL clubs the line, 3-4 edges included, has 1.34 sacks a team game (edges 0.70, tackles 0.64) where real linemen and edges listed as linebackers have 1.91, off-ball linebackers 0.30 (real 0.36) and backs 0.06 (0.18). At FBS by roster label the line has 1.43 (real 1.26), linebackers 0.34 (0.63, stand-up edges among them) and backs 0.14 (0.18). Tackles for loss on runs did not move: FBS linebackers 0.73 a team game and backs 0.96 (real 1.86 and 1.16), the NFL clubs' 0.78 and 1.08 (1.66 and 0.90). The bend (`BLOCK_BEND` 20) was sized with these plays missing, so read the ladders again once they are back. Found 2026-11-25 (thread "Fix freak defender stat lines").
- **The four-man rush gets half the real sacks** (high). Two thirds of dropbacks are four-man rushes. After the four-man rush lane (2026-11-25; world seed 321's NFL clubs, `match_check` with `AFM_BLITZ=1`, 2,720 games, seed 1) 15.5% of them are pressured and 3.4% sacked (real NFL 2021-25 27.2% and 6.0%; 13.6% and 3.5% before it). The tackle's vertical set and the spare man's help moved the pressure to the edges: an edge's rush is a pressure as PFR counts them 5.2% of the time (real 6.6%) and a sack 0.97 times a 100 (1.77), 0.70 sacks a team game where real ends and edges listed as linebackers have 1.27; a tackle's rush 5.2% and 0.96 (real 3.1% and 0.78). So the line's pressures are near real and its sacks are not: what turns a pressure into a sack (the next entry) holds most of the gap. The real share pressured counts more than PFR's pressures do (PFR's team rate is 22.2% of dropbacks, the book's 20.2%), so read the sacks. On the FBS teams 18.1% are pressured and 3.9% sacked (16.0% and 4.0% before). Found 2026-11-25 (thread "Fix freak defender stat lines").
- **A pressure becomes a sack two thirds as often as real** (high). After the four-man rush lane (2026-11-25; world seed 321's NFL clubs) 0.241 of the book's pressures are sacks (PFR 0.283; 0.261 before it). By PFR's count an edge's pressures turn into his sacks 0.19 times (real 0.27), a tackle's 0.18 (0.25) and a linebacker's 0.20 (0.29); linebackers, corners and safeties pressure at PFR's rate a blitz (0.175, 0.203 and 0.154; real 0.172, 0.181 and 0.159) but sack 3.48, 3.79 and 2.49 times a 100 rushes (real 4.99, 5.22 and 4.17 a 100 blitzes). The lane's edge wins come two yards behind the line and later in the down, and the passer gets the ball out: of the pressures the engine credits to an edge, 0.25 became his sack (0.33 before). What turns a pressure into a sack (the passer's escape, the throwaway, the rusher's finish, how long the coverage holds) was not traced. Same thread.
- **The missing sacks sit in nickel and dime** (high). After who makes the tackle (2026-11-25; world seed 321's NFL clubs, `match_check` with `AFM_DEFENDERS=17`, dropbacks by the backs the call put on the field against real NFL 2023-24 by listed backs): with four backs the passer is sacked on 6.2% of dropbacks (real 5.6%), with five on 4.3% (6.5%) and with six on 4.5% (8.6%). Real defenses get home more with more backs on the field, on passing downs; the engine's get home less. A base defense sends five or more men on 54% of its dropbacks (real 42%; a 3-4 base has five men on the line), a nickel on 22% (23%) and a dime on 21% (23%), so the rush counts are near real outside base and the gap is what four rushers and a coverage behind them do on passing downs: the two entries above. The FBS teams read 6.6%, 5.2% and 5.5%, with no real college count. Found 2026-11-25 (thread "Fix freak defender stat lines").
- **The best NFL sack seasons run four over real** (medium). After the four-man rush lane (2026-11-25; world seed 321's NFL clubs, `AFM_DEFENDERS=17`) the best sack season a year is 23.9 and the second 20.2 (real 19.4 and 16.5; 23.8 and 20.1 before it), from the 10th down at or a little under real. The man left over now helps where the rusher is winning, so Philadelphia's two tackles fell from 20.4 and 17.3 sacks a season to 18.3 and 12.8. The top is now the Giants' long-armed power edge (Strength 119, Lower Body Strength 125, 34.6-inch arms, 9.2 points over the typical blocker): 22.7 a season (19.2 before), a third of them through a block, where 12% of all edges' sacks are. Whether a sack through a block reads his arms too strongly (E2) or he is simply the world's freak was not traced; nothing is capped, and a deeper set gave him more (29.7 a season at 0.4 of the way to the passer). Same thread.

- **The rush call mix: linebackers blitz a fifth more and safeties a third as often as real** (medium). - **The staff's unused call draws from the game's random stream** (low). In a live game the play caller's own call is worked out on every snap before yours replaces it (`game/flow.rs`, the snap's `calling::call_offense` and the sheet, then "the play caller's call stands, or yours"), and it draws from the game's stream. So two coaches who differ only in a calling rating play different games from the same hand's calls: on 8 live games fed the same answers every pair parted (`coach_check hand`, `docs/coach-check.md`). Nothing is wrong on average, but a live game can't prove a rating is dead by hand, and a replay depends on a rating the snap never used. A stream of its own for the staff's suggestion would fix both. Found 2026-11-25 (coach ratings lane RE0).
- **Tables measured before the even run block and the block's bend** (low-medium). Audit item 1's fix (2026-11-24) changed what a defender brings off a run block: Block Shedding 0.27 of it (0.4), Hand Use 0.13 (0.2), Anchor and Play Recognition 0.1 each (0.15), and his body 0.4 (Strength 0.1). Audit item 3's (2026-11-25) bent how every block reads a rating gap (`BLOCK_BEND`: 10 points count 8.1 and 20 count 13.9, on the pass rush, the run block and the shed's pull), so a rusher's or a blocker's points far from his man's are worth less than they were. The four-man rush (2026-11-25) moved pass rush wins from the tackles to the edges (the tackle's set and the spare man's help). Who makes the tackle (2026-11-25) changed the backs on the field in every situation (five backs against two receivers on 44% of early downs where it was base, six against four receivers on a tenth where it was always, a safety as the fifth or sixth back). The tables measured on the engine still price these men the old way: the roster lean's weights (`data/roster_values.csv`), the fit scores (`data/fit_values.csv`), the coaches' call table (`data/call_values.csv`; after item 1 the NFL test league's inside runs 3.68 to 3.72 a carry and outside 5.98 to 5.87) and the analysts' models. The test leagues' totals moved little (after item 3 yards a carry 4.485 to 4.479 at the NFL and 4.865 to 4.876 at FBS, sacks 6% lower, points up 0.7 and 0.2), so the sizes are small; rebuild them once, after the defenders' lanes that follow in the same thread (linebackers and backs behind the line, who makes the tackle). Found 2026-11-24 (thread "Fix FBS rushing yards per carry"), widened 2026-11-25 (thread "Fix freak defender stat lines").

- **The clock barely runs between some in-bounds snaps** (high). In 40 engine games (NFL seed 1 and FBS seed 1, `match_check` play-by-play, 2026-11-24), 164 of 1,842 in-bounds runs (9%) were followed by the next snap 2-8 seconds later, mostly late in halves, including a team leading in the fourth quarter. Real in-bounds runs leave about 30-40 seconds outside the hurry-up, and a play itself takes 4-6. Opus's blind test and its coach review both named it the engine's plainest tell (`docs/research/sequencing.md`). Not traced yet: the runoff after a run in `game/flow.rs` (`clock_run`, `runoff`) is the first place to look.
- **Some throws land past the end line** (medium). 40 of 2,767 NFL test-league throws (1.4%) had more air yards than the field holds (yards to the goal + 10), for example a 43-yard catch with 56 air yards and a 26-yard throw from the 11. Either air yards are measured along the throw's path rather than downfield, or the deep target spot isn't held inside the end zone. Seen by the blind test and the coach review.

It belongs to section 12's topic 12, the adjustment budget, and needs its sizes from real second-half splits.

- **Safeties and linebackers end the wrong runs** (medium). After the second level (realism audit item 3, 2026-11-27; world seed 321's NFL clubs, `match_check` with `AFM_RUNDIAG=1` and `AFM_DEFENDERS=17`, 2,720 games, the first tackler on the backs' designed runs by listed group against nflverse 2024) linebackers are the first tackler on 23.3% of the runs (real 23.3%; 19.2% before), but on the wrong ones: 11.4% of the runs for a loss or no gain (real 23.3%), 31.2% of the 1-4 yard runs (23.9%), 22.7% of the 5-9 (26.2%), 10.4% of the 10-19 (15.8%) and 15.2% of the 20+ (7.2%). Safeties make 41.6% of the first tackles on the 5-9 yard runs (23.3%) and 60.8% of the 10-19 (46.8%). Causes. (a) While the runner is behind the line a linebacker fills to 2.6 yards past it and holds at 2.1 (`lb_fill`, `LB_FILL`, `LB_HOLD`), so he meets the runner a yard or two downfield and rarely in the backfield. A nearer fill (2.0, 1.5 or 1.0 yards past the line, each hold half a yard nearer than its fill) or one timed by his read (the sooner he read the run, the nearer he fills) gave linebackers 12% to 28% of the stuffs, but 39% to 45% of the first tackles on the 1-4 yard runs, and took the inside runs from 4.01 a carry to 3.46-3.78 (real 4.26). Why a nearer linebacker costs the inside runs that much is not traced yet: the next step reads where the back meets him and who is blocking him then. (b) A back who presses the hole at a free linebacker to make him commit (his hole read counting a free man past the line at a quarter or half of his weight) moved nothing (4.64 against 4.65 a carry, linebackers 23.6% against 23.8%). (c) The alley safety still meets most runs that get past the line (`deep_fit`, `FIT_ALLEY`). Needs, as a lane of its own: the linebacker's fill from his run key with the climb and the back's cut meeting it, yards a carry held at real. The trial code is in the project folder at `afm/item3-second-level/` (and `afm/item3-linebackers/` before it). Proposed, not confirmed. Found in lane SF9b (thread "Fix open-field tackles on long runs"); the climber's whiff, the shed's floor and the misread fit built 2026-11-27 (`docs/match-engine.md`, "The second level"). The catch's extra two men ("Two men at a short catch", below) rule out a longer fight here.

- **Few passers get out of the pocket, and few scramble** (high).
