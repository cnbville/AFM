Trial 6: twenty careers

The last trial before the game. All 17 fixes are built: film line C, rawer college arrivals with a first-year dip, busts after signing, dismissal severity, the transfer portal, declaring early, draft value by position and need, a 32-team league with depth charts, camp battles, the practice squad and free agency, games lost to injury, and retirement by position, body and mind. One class of 250,000 freshmen, followed to retirement. The 20 careers below were drawn at random from groups chosen to show the full range of outcomes.

Stars and the draft

Drafted: five-stars 94% in this class and 61% in a second test class (real about 63%; a class has only about 33 five-stars, so it swings), four-stars 30%, three-stars 5%. Five top-500 seniors ended up two-stars or unranked. Unranked and two-star players are about 10% of picks.

College

The first-year dip averages 0.38 for college freshmen (from about 0.1 to 0.66). 55% of five-stars start as true freshmen. About 3,800 players used the portal: up, sideways, down or no school.

Draft

88 players declared early and 66 were drafted. Round 1 had 3 QBs, 6 edge rushers and no running backs. 4 specialists were drafted, all on day 3. FCS players were about 3% of picks (real about 6%), a known gap.

Pro careers

No first-round pick and 1 of 32 second-rounders left a roster within two seasons. Careers average 10.9 seasons for first-rounders, 4.3 for 7th-rounders and 3.4 for undrafted players. Pros played for 2.8 teams on average. Median retirement: RB 28, safety 29.5, QB 32, punter 38.

Twenty careers

Each card names the group it was drawn from. Hover or tap a season in the strip for team, depth chart spot, games missed and first-year dip.

How to read the level strip: in high school it compares a player with everyone his age in the country, in college it puts every player on one national scale (FCS and D2 players after a small discount for weaker competition), and in the pros it is simply his overall. The big number is where he stood going into the season, and the green number is where he finished it.

Rare outcomes are marked as rare in the why box, with the odds where the model gave them.
