I've written the season calendar and pacing doc: [season-calendar-and-pacing.md](/mnt/project-files/design/season-calendar-and-pacing.md). It covers the whole year week by week, from W1 (the Monday after the championship game) to W52. Each week shows what's happening in the pros, college and high school, and what you decide that week.

**Pacing.** Every event gets one of four tension levels, from background news up to a full climax. The climaxes are fixed:
- The draft
- Cutdown day
- The trade deadline
- The playoffs and the championship game

On top of that, at most two regular-season games a year can become climaxes, and only when something real is at stake. Free agency and the combine are big moments but never climaxes. May to mid-July is a protected quiet stretch for development and recruiting.

**Things that can't happen fast.** Contract talks, trades, scouting reports and injury news all take days to play out. A player's potential can only move one step at each checkpoint, so nobody turns into a star overnight.

I had to pick a few defaults, and the doc lists them at the end. These two change the most, so tell me if either is wrong:
- **Your role:** you run one NFL team. High school and college are a simulated world you scout but don't coach.
- **Starting point:** a new game starts in mid-February, before the combine, not at training camp in summer like Football Manager.

High school and college still develop players in every option. The difference is whether you also coach there.

1. **NFL team only** *(recommended)*: You run one pro team and only scout high school and college, like Football Manager with one club.
2. **College too**: You can also start a career as a college coach and get hired into the NFL later.

Recommendation reason: It keeps the first version focused, and college or high school careers can be added later on the same calendar.
