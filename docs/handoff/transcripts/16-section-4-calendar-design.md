- **May to June:** rookie minicamp, OTAs (voluntary practices), and the deadline for fifth-year options (an extra year on first-round deals). Schedule release is mid-May. Mandatory minicamp is in June, then a summer break.
- **July 15:** the deadline for long-term deals with tagged players.
- **Late July to August:** training camp, three preseason games (four for the Hall of Fame Game teams), then one cut from 90 players to 53. Waivers and practice squads follow.
- **September to January:** 18 weeks, 17 games and one bye. The trade deadline is the Tuesday after week 9. Flex scheduling (moving big games to prime time) and international games are included.
- **January to February:** Black Monday (coach firings the day after the season), 14 playoff teams, the Pro Bowl Games and the Super Bowl.

Here's a proposal for a portal driven by real events.

**The idea:** a player doesn't decide on January 2. All season, an **inner lean** (stay or go) builds from what actually happens to him. The window is only when he acts on it.

**Events that move the lean:**
- **Playing time:** benched, losing the depth chart battle, or a starter returns from injury.
- **His school signs a five-star at his position** in the December early signing period. That's the real reason many players leave.
- **Coaches:** his head coach is fired, his position coach leaves, or a new scheme doesn't fit him. Each of these is felt on its own.
- **Money:** his NIL deal shrinks, the collective dries up, or another school's offer reaches him through a handler. That last one is tampering: illegal, but real.
- **Life:** a parent falls ill (move closer to home), homesickness, a breakup, academic trouble, or friends on the team leaving.
- **Draft read:** a weak NFL grade can push him toward a bigger stage to prove himself.
- **The season itself:** a team with no bowl game, or a breakout season that makes him wanted.

**How it plays out:**
- **The redshirt trick:** some players who already know they're leaving stop playing after 4 games, to keep the year.
- **Timing:** stars often have a deal lined up and enter on day 1. Fringe players wait and see. Some enter and never find a school ("portal regret").
- **Once he enters,** his school can cut his NIL. Coming back is the school's choice.
- **Personality sets how much each event weighs:** a loyal player shrugs off a benching, while an ambitious one leaves over a lost snap share. No single event decides it.

**Question:** Should you get **whispers** about a player's lean before he enters? The better connected your scouts are, the earlier and more accurate they'd be. For example: "his family is unhappy with his snaps." My pick: **yes**. It matters for your draft board when a player you're tracking moves school.

**Topic 6: The in-season week**

The proposal follows the real weekly rhythm at each level.

**NFL (a Sunday game):**
- **Monday:** film review, lifting, injury exams (MRIs). Your press conference.
- **Tuesday:** the players' day off. Coaches build the game plan. Waiver claims (claiming players other teams cut) process at 4pm ET.
- **Wednesday:** install the base plan (1st and 2nd down). The injury report starts: did not practice, limited or full.
- **Thursday:** third downs and the red zone.
- **Friday:** situations and the two-minute drill. Final game statuses: out, doubtful or questionable.
- **Saturday:** walkthrough, travel and the team hotel. Practice squad call-ups are due by 4pm ET.
- **Sunday:** the inactives list (who sits) 90 minutes before kickoff, then the game.

**What shifts the rhythm:** Thursday night and Monday night games make short or long weeks. The bye gives players 4 days off by the CBA, and teams self-scout (study their own tendencies) during it. Long trips and time zones matter, for example a West Coast team at a 1pm East Coast kickoff. The CBA caps padded practices at 14 per season. Other teams can sign your practice squad players. IR players get a 21-day window to practice before they must be activated.
