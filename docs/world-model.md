# How the world works (section 2)

The build of `docs/design/world.md`. Code: `crates/afm-engine/src/world/`. Trial report: `cargo run --release --bin world_trial -- <seed> <years>` (in `afm-tools`, debug only). The latest trial is `docs/trials/world-trial2.md`.

Everything below that Raichel has not answered is marked **proposed, not confirmed**.

## The shape of it

- `World::new` builds the whole country, then runs 30 years of hidden history before the 2026 start (decision: 30 years). Six extra spin-up years run first so rosters, coaches and prestige settle; nothing from them is kept.
- Since section 4 (2026-10-16) the world runs on a daily clock: each thing happens on its real day under that year's rules (see "Section 4: the calendar" below). Before that, every year ran in one fixed order.
- Each person has his own random stream, so a seed gives the same world on any machine. A 30-year world takes about 100 seconds in release mode.
- Game results are a stand-in until section 3: each team's strength comes from its starters, with home field and a win chance from the strength gap.

## Programs (topics 2, 6, 7)

- Real colleges from `data/real-world/colleges.csv`: 138 FBS, 127 FCS (Chicago State, with no team, is left out), 160 D2. The real junior colleges (113, NJCAA and California's CCCAA), Division III and the NAIA come from `data/real-world/colleges_lower.csv`.
- Prestige comes from history, recent results, the head coach, facilities, NIL, fans and TV, and conference (topic 7). Recruits read it relative to their division, so a strong D2 school does not out-pull an FBS one.
- 12 EA-style grades. Steady ones move about 15% of the way to their target each year (tradition, academics, facilities, atmosphere, campus life, brand exposure). Fast ones move about 80% (title chances, playing time, coach prestige, NIL, pro pipeline). Conference strength moves only on realignment (Raichel confirmed the split). Two straight titles take a C- program to about B+.
- NIL collectives have budgets that follow results and booster mood, with booster departures. A falling collective sends warnings through the inbox, the news and a falling arrow on the NIL grade, getting louder each year (Raichel's follow-up answer 4).
- Recruiting footprints grow with prestige; pipelines to a high school grow only from players who made an impact, and fade slowly (topic 4).
- FCS playoff: the SWAC and MEAC stay out, as in real life (Celebration Bowl). The Ivy League is in (it joined from 2025).

## High schools (topics 1, 2)

- Each state gets as many schools as the data says. The state's real top programs keep their names; the rest are generated in real towns from the data. Classifications go by enrollment.
- Powerhouses recruit transfers, the national ones out of state too. Transfers follow each state's real sit-out rule from the data.
- High school NIL follows each state's real rule (`hs_nil_allowed` in `hs_states.csv`; 47 of 51 allow it).
- A rare pull lets a kid from a bad home transfer to a powerhouse (Raichel confirmed: rare).

## Players' hidden background and family (topics 1, 5)

- Six background parts: money, safety, family (home stability), school resources, exposure network, academics (GPA). Plus a hidden mental-health stability level (confirmed).
- Hardship adds a little grit, a YAC tendency and mental toughness. It is never a driver.
- Risk: high potential with a bad home, or a perfect setup with low ambition, raises trouble and quitting a little.
- Families have a decider (parent, grandparent, handler, the kid himself) with their own wants. A handler can steer a kid to the wrong school. Siblings and bloodlines are a small influence.
- Scouts see a character report whose accuracy depends on the scout.

## Coaches (topic 3)

- Hidden ability (teaching by position group, scheme, game management, motivation, discipline, recruiting, network, relationships) and a public reputation that lags it.
- Reputation catches up about 30% a year. A title gives a massive boost, bigger the lower the coach's starting reputation (the Cignetti case). Bad results chip it away slowly. Assistants move at half speed.
- Hiring runs on recency bias: the last two seasons count most.
- Coaches age, improve and decline, and pass a little of their teaching to assistants (coaching trees, an influence only).
- Patience when firing grows with tenure (proposed, not confirmed: year-one coaches are almost never fired).

## Recruiting and movement (topics 4, 6, 8)

- Visibility decides who gets seen. Stars come from film and projection, with the lowest-visibility kids underrated.
- Signing day is a matching: programs offer down their boards, and each recruit (or his decider) picks by what he wants: level, brand, playing time, distance from home, academics, development, NIL.
- JUCO: kids go there for grades, late growth or no offers, and move up after 1-2 years or stop. JUCO grades improve while there.
- The portal: playing time, a coaching change, a handler's steer, moving up, dismissal or money. Tampering is quiet and never punished; it makes moving up more likely at schools with small collectives. NIL can backfire on a player.
- The draft: 257 picks plus undrafted signings, scored from the scouted rating and projection. Kickers and punters go late.

## Trial 1 additions (built 2026-10-13)

Raichel's running list from the trial 1 walk-through (`/mnt/project-files/design/world-trial1-feedback.md`).

1. **Prove-it extension.** A head coach with 5+ years, loyalty (no job-hopping, or one move with high loyalty) and real success (a title in his last 8 seasons, or 70% wins over 50+ games) who has two bad years in a row usually gets one more year, more often when there's an excuse (many players lost to the draft, a young lineup). The news reports it. Fail the prove-it year (a losing record, or well below expectation without a strong record) and he's fired.
2. **Ethnicity and hiring bias.** Players and coaches have an ethnicity. It never changes a rating. Players match who really plays at each level (state child population, how often each group plays, and the real FBS mix). AI hiring carries a hidden bias against coaches of color, strongest in the history, bigger at big jobs, fading slowly after 2026, calibrated toward TIDES counts. The player's own hires never carry it. A yearly diversity report appears in the news.
3. **Names** follow ethnicity and birth decade (`data/names/`). Rebuilt 2026-10-14 (world trial 3 finding 9): every SSA boy's name since 1940 (38,669) and every Census surname with 100+ people (167,407), plus suffixes (Jr., II, III), hyphenated surnames, players who go by initials and inner-capital spellings (DeAndre). Shares are proposed, not confirmed; see `data/names/README.md`.
4. **Pipelines.** Impact is judged inside a player's own conference (all-conference, awards, draft round). Pipelines now exist to a high school and to its area, and staff build personal ties to the schools their impact players came from; ties move with the coach. States with more FBS-level talent than their own big programs can sign spill over to nearby Group of 5 and FCS schools.
5. **Coach ladder.** Every college has 8 position coaches under the coordinators. Coaches climb from high school to position coach to coordinator to head coach, or skip steps on buzz (a high school coach with recent state titles and a dominant record gets college looks). A new head coach keeps some of the old staff. The stat-record part of buzz waits for section 3.
6. **Academics.** NCAA tiers decide who can play right away (full qualifier), who must sit year one (academic redshirt) and who must go JUCO. Strict schools have admissions bars and a few special admits a year. A recruit with risky grades is weighed by the school's academic support, and some players grow out of it. College GPA drifts, and a low GPA can cost a season.
7. **Draft value.** NFL teams draft one by one with their own needs and a position value calibrated to 2000-2025 drafts (QBs go first, kickers late). Every pick weighs the player's grade, the team's board, position value and team need. A QB's premium depends on the team needing one, except at the very top. A team with a young franchise QB holding a top-5 pick trades down to a QB-needy team when a first-round QB is there. At the top, great players at lesser positions count for less than their grade (a generational WR rarely goes first). The numbers are proposed, not confirmed.
8. **Player pages.** Height, weight, arm, hand and wingspan from the real combine per position, full combine tests, level ratings each season, the coaches he had, his offers and why he was drafted.

Fixes from trial 1's flags: foster homes no longer give money (flag 3), the NIL life event happens once (flag 4), and unsigned 3+ star recruits get a reason (flag 5).

## Coaching market (dynasty item 11.11-11.15, 2026-10-15)

Raichel approved the five items on 2026-10-15. Why: in 30-year trials one coach held a top program for 12-14 years and nobody hired him away. Every number below is proposed, not confirmed. Code: `world/staff.rs` (market, NFL staffs), `world/coach.rs` (age), `league.rs` (NFL staff slots).

- **Short lists (11.11).** A search for a college head or coordinator job ranks every coach who fits the job by how hot he is, and calls the top names first: 15 for a head job, 10 for a coordinator job. The random draw of 40 names stays, so lesser-known names still appear. Hotness is what a committee can see: reputation with recency bias, +3 for the biggest stage (scaled by the job's prestige), +2 for each conference title and +4 for each national title in his last three seasons there, and up to +3 for a program rising under him (+1 per 0.01 of prestige gained in three years, down to -1). An FBS head job also calls the 3 hottest NFL coordinators. A college search marks down a man who never coached in college by 2 points (no recruiting ties). Position coach searches still draw at random and judge unit records. Prestige still decides who can lure whom (11.13).
- **NFL staffs and hiring (11.12).** Each of the 32 NFL teams has a named head coach and two coordinators (`Team::head_coach`, `oc`, `dc`). The staff sets the team's coaching rating (head coach half, each coordinator a quarter on his side of the ball); the old random staff changes in `advance_league` now apply only to the class sim. Each season a team's record comes from its roster (0.13 win share per roster sd), its staff (0.06 per staff sd) and luck (sd 0.12), so win shares spread about like the real 0.19. The 12 (14 from 2020) best records make the playoffs; the champion and runner-up are drawn by record. Owners fire on wins: 1.6 × (0.5 − last win share) + 0.6 × the year before, less half the coach's result against his roster, past 0.1, times 3.2, times owner patience; 30% in year one, 70% in year two. A new head coach keeps each coordinator 30% of the time; a coach who stays fires a coordinator after a losing year (up to 35%). Candidates for a head job: every NFL coordinator, out-of-work former NFL head coaches and coordinators, and the 8 hottest FBS head coaches. Owners mark down a man with no NFL experience by 4 points (2 for a coordinator job). If nobody says yes, the team promotes its own coordinator.
- **How a coach weighs a move (11.13).** Before, a sitting coach moved only for a prestige gain over 0.03. Now the gain is one part of his "pull", all in prestige points. The money: 0.05 × ln(new pay / old pay), so doubling his pay is worth 0.035. Pay rises with prestige inside each level (FBS head coach $0.6M-12M, FCS $0.15-0.7M, D2 $70-200k, JUCO $50-120k, high school $50-160k; coordinators and position coaches have their own ranges), a winner has had 10% raises per title (to three), NFL head coaches earn $5-14M by owner and coordinators $1.2-4M, and the NFL paid up to twice as much in the 1990s, easing to college levels by 2024 (Claude's reading of 2024-25 pay, to check). A first head job adds 0.12 (0.18 for the most ambitious). The NFL adds up to ±0.04 by ambition (the hidden 1-20 trait the coach already had), and a gifted recruiter gives up his edge there (−0.02); an NFL coach who recruits well is drawn back to college (+0.02). A young core of starters coming back holds a head coach (−0.03). Age past 52 costs 0.003 a year and each season settled 0.0015 (to 12). Each coach now has a hidden home state (half the time where his first job was, else drawn by where players grow up): going home adds 0.03, leaving home costs 0.02. A seat getting hot adds up to 0.03. The move must clear 0.03 (the old bar), then ambition and loyalty set the chance as before. Story lines name the two biggest pulls ("a bigger program and a big raise", "the step up to the NFL", "a chance to go home to TX"), on both programs' stories and in the news.
- **Age (11.14).** Before, a coach at his learning ceiling never declined (only scheme did). Each coach now has a hidden age his sharpness starts to fade (about 59, sd 3.5, 52-68) and a pace (0.08-0.35 points a year, 15% faster each year). Recruiting fades fastest (1.2×), then motivation (1.0×), game management (0.8×), relationships (0.6×), discipline (0.5×) and teaching (0.4×). Network holds. Learning stops at 58. A coach at pace 0.2 loses about 1.6 points of head-coach value from 58 to 65.
- **Early exits (11.15).** Coaches under 60 can retire for their health (0.08% a year at 40, 0.55% at 59) or step away burned out (0.8% a year after ten seasons in the biggest head jobs, scaled by the job's prestige squared and tenure to 15 seasons, ±40% by temper; a coordinator carries 40% of the pressure). After 60 the old retirement shape stays (4% at 60, +3 points a year). Both leave a story line with the reason.
- **Check run** (seed 2026, 30 years, stand-in history, `AFM_ENGINE_FROM=2026`; the report's "Coaching market" section): FBS head coach changes 27.1 a year (real about 20-30); FBS head coaches fired 18.7; hired away by another college 4.3 and by the NFL 0.6 (real about 5-7 and 0.5, Claude's reading, not checked); NFL head coach hires 6.3 a year (real 2015-25 about 7), 4.4 from NFL coordinators and 0.6 straight from college; retirements before 60: 43.7 a year at all levels, 0.4 of them FBS head coaches (real about 0.5, Claude's reading). FBS national-title coaches stayed 15.6 seasons on average at that school (median 14; real title coaches' tenures run about 10-15, Claude's reading); the most titles by one coach was 4 (three coaches). One college head coach went to the NFL about every two years, spread over the 30 years.
- The 3-year world test's FCS draft share bound moved from 12% to 14%: that short world runs 11.5-12.7% on seeds 7-9 before this build and 12.5-13.0% after; the 30-year world gives 6.2% (real about 6%).

## Era limits, NIL and prestige (dynasty items 11.4-11.10, 2026-10-15)

Raichel approved items 11.4-11.10 in the "Dynasty fix" thread (`/mnt/project-files/afm/dynasty-fix/round1-kickers-and-item11.md`). Code: `world/ncaa.rs` (rules by era, NIL budgets, rogue boosters and the NCAA), with small hooks in `program.rs`, `staff.rs`, `season.rs` and `recruiting.rs`. **Status: built and compiling; the 30-year world check run and the trial numbers against real are not done yet** (the build moved to a fresh thread).

How it works:
- **11.4 Roster limits by era.** `ncaa::roster_limits(program, season)` is the one function every roster rule reads: (roster limit, most new signees a year), after any NCAA scholarship cut. FBS: 95 scholarships until 1991, 85 from 1992 (the 1991 NCAA convention; Sea of Blue, "A brief history of NCAA football scholarships"), 25 initial counters a year (NCAA Bylaw 15.5.6.1), until 2024; from 2025 the House settlement's 105-man roster with every player eligible for a scholarship and no yearly cap (CBS Sports, 2025). FCS: 63 equivalencies spread over at most 85 players on aid, 30 initial counters a year (Bylaw 15.5.6.2, quoted in College Sports Journal; 63 since 1994, Wikipedia FCS); from 2025 the 90 FCS programs that opted into the House settlement follow the FBS rule and the 36 that opted out keep the old one (SI, July 15, 2025; the list is in `ncaa.rs`). D2: 36 equivalencies and no cap on players on aid (scholarshipstats.com). JUCO: the NJCAA's 85 scholarships (scholarshipstats.com). The world has no walk-ons yet, so its rosters are the scholarship players.
- **11.5 No NIL before July 2021.** `Collective.budget` is now the NIL money a program can pay players in an offseason (`ncaa::nil_budget`): zero through the 2020 offseason, so the class of 2021 signed without NIL and the class of 2022 was the first with it. The booster base (`base`, `big_booster`) gives in every era: before NIL its money counts toward facilities and prestige only. NIL offers, roster pay, the "bought away" portal reason, the rising-program reach and the collective warnings all start in 2021. Before 2021 the NIL grade ranks the booster base.
- **11.6 Illegal booster money before 2021.** A few FBS programs have a hidden rogue booster with a yearly pot. On signing day he adds cash to his program's offers to a few of the best recruits in its footprint; it works through the recruit's existing money weight, so it sways needy families most. A target is paid only if he signs. Payments surface with a chance a year of 1 - exp(-paid in the last four years / $8M) (four years is the NCAA statute of limitations). A case brings real penalty types through the world's own systems: scholarships and signees cut for three seasons (`roster_limits`), no conference title and no playoff for one or two seasons (`season.rs`), and a show-cause order that ends the named coach's college job and keeps colleges from hiring him (`Coach::show_cause`). The program disassociates the booster. From July 2021 rogue boosters stop (the money goes through the collective), but payments from before can still surface for four years (Tennessee's 2023 case covered 2018-20). Real anchors: SMU (death penalty, 1987), Alabama 2002 (booster Logan Young paid about $150,000 for one recruit; 21 scholarships over three years, a two-year bowl ban), USC 2010 (30 scholarships over three years, two-year bowl ban, one-year show-cause for assistant Todd McNair), Ohio State 2011 (85 to 82 for three years, a one-year bowl ban, a five-year show-cause for Jim Tressel), Miami 2013 (9 scholarships over three years, two-year show-causes for three assistants), Ole Miss 2017 (two-year bowl ban, 13 scholarships, show-causes of eight and five years for two staff members).
- **11.7 A ceiling on NIL budgets.** No booster base gives more than $20M a year (`ncaa::COLLECTIVE_CEILING`): Ohio State's players got "around $20 million" in 2024 (AD Ross Bjork, CBS Sports, July 24, 2024), Notre Dame's collective took in $20.5M in 2023 and the Texas One Fund spent $13.3M (Sportico tax filings via CBS Sports, Dec. 30, 2024), and Opendorse's Blake Lawrence: "very few collectives actually had $20 million dollars to spend a year". Growth slows as the boosters near the ceiling and never passes it. The NIL market grows into its 2024 size (Opendorse Power 4 totals: 2021 $393M, 2022 $667M, 2023 $840M, 2024 $1.0B). From 2025 revenue sharing adds 75% of a $20.5M cap growing 4% a year for Power 4 schools (Front Office Sports: Texas and Texas Tech give football about 75%, $15.37M; CBS Sports: 4% a year), and the American's floor ($10M over three years) for the Group of 5.
- **11.8 NIL offers within real ranges.** No offer goes above the top of the real range for a starter at a major program (`rates.json`, CBS via SI 2024): QB $800K, OL $500K, DL $600K, RB, WR and LB $300K, DB $225K.
- **11.9 A fixed yardstick for prestige.** `prestige_rel` is prestige divided by the division leader's prestige when recorded history began, not by the current leader's. The spin-up years still measure against the leader. A program can now read above 1.0.
- **11.10 Recent results build slowly.** `recent = 0.75 recent + 0.25 season score`, and the season score is no longer capped at 1.0, so a title season counts more than a plain 12-1 season.

Proposed, not confirmed (Claude's picks inside the approved wording):
- D2 rosters stay at 110 (no cap on players on aid in D2); JUCO moves from 80 to the NJCAA's 85 for every JUCO, the California schools too (they give no athletic aid).
- Where no NCAA yearly cap exists (FBS and opted-in FCS from 2025, D2, JUCO), staffs still sign at most about 1.4 normal classes (39, 33, 42, 56), the world's earlier pick.
- The 25-signee limit runs through 2024 as approved; the real NCAA waived it for 2022-23 and 2023-24 (CBS Sports, May 18, 2022), keeping the 85 cap. Raichel may want the waiver.
- The NIL market ramp (each collective follows the Power 4 total), football's 75% of revenue sharing for every Power 4 school, the Group of 5 at the American's floor, and no revenue sharing in FCS and below.
- Tight end offers top out at $300K and specialists at $50K (not in the data).
- Rogue boosters: a yearly chance of 0.02 times the fan base (1.5x with a big booster) to gain one, 10% a year to stop, a pot of $50K-$300K, prices of $150K, $50K and $15K for a 5-, 4- and 3-star, at most four targets a year in the program's footprint. Detection scale $8M. Penalties: one scholarship per $15K found (3 to 30) over three seasons; a one-year postseason ban from $100K and two from $300K; the head coach named in one case in four, otherwise the recruit's position coach, for one year plus one per $100K (up to 8). FBS only.
- The yardstick (the leader's prestige when recorded history began) and the 0.25 weight on the latest season.

## Kickers, punters and roster needs (fix list topic 1, 2026-10-15)

Raichel's yes on items 1.1-1.11 (`/mnt/project-files/afm/dynasty-fix/round1-kickers-and-item11.md`). Code: `world/recruiting.rs` (class plans, boards, camps, walk-ons, portal), `positions.rs` (the long snapper), `game/roster.rs` and `game/flow.rs` (who kicks), `league.rs` (NFL long snappers).

How it works:
- **A class plan per position (1.1, 1.2).** Each staff wants the real FBS roster count at each spot, scaled to its roster limit (`real_roster`, `roster_limits`). It signs this year's hole, or half the hole two years out, whichever is bigger. Two years out, seniors, likely early NFL entrants (read against the last draft) and likely transfers count as gone. Each staff ranks recruits on a board per position.
- **Talent beats need (1.3).** At a full position a staff still offers a recruit who is clearly better than the best one left at its open spots: a quarter of a film standard deviation better at the top program, a full one at the bottom, at most two over the plan. Not for specialists.
- **Specialists (1.4-1.6).** Kickers and punters are graded on leg power, accuracy and the mental side, against their own spot. Their stars come from the kicking camps (top 25 at a spot five stars, 100 four, 250 three, 500 two), not the general ranking. A staff keeps about one kicker and one punter on scholarship; the other kickers, the second punter and the long snappers walk on. A walk-on gets no NIL and pays his way, as at Pioneer League schools.
- **The long snapper (1.7)** is a position. His rating is his snap (the hidden snapping trait). Nobody is placed there in high school; each high school's best snapper who isn't a ranked prospect can be ranked by the snapping camps, and then colleges recruit him as a snapper.
- **Who kicks (1.8, 1.9)**: see `docs/match-engine.md`. A kicker kicks and a punter punts, a big-legged kicker takes the kickoffs, and with only one of them he does both.
- **Portal (1.10).** A program whose starter left and who has no returning starter at that spot reaches out to every entrant there who beats its remaining backups, best entrants first. Item 11.16 has since replaced the 40 random programs (see the next section but one).
- **NFL (1.11).** Every NFL team carries one long snapper. Teams draft a kicker, punter or snapper only when theirs is below par or aging, with a flat discount on top of the old position value.

## Recruiting feel, relationships and the portal (dynasty items 11.1-11.3, 11.16-11.18, 2026-10-15)

Raichel's yes on item 11 (`/mnt/project-files/afm/dynasty-fix/round1-kickers-and-item11.md`). Code: `world/recruiting.rs` (signing day and the portal), with records in `world/mod.rs` (`RecruitingLog`) and `season.rs`. The match engine is untouched.

How it works:
- **11.1 A recruit's own feel for each school.** Each recruit has his own feel for each school: the campus, the people, a friend there, how a visit went. Its spread was 0.04 (in utility points); it is now 0.07. With the staff chemistry below, the total spread is about 0.076, up from 0.05. Two 5-stars now rank the same schools differently.
- **11.2 Recruits see commitments as they happen.** Signing day is still a matching, but the roster view is live. When a recruit commits, he joins that school's position room at once. The next recruit at his position sees him there, counted the way he counts himself (two years on). If a recruit flips, he leaves the room again. A recruit also re-checks the school he holds when a new offer comes: if a better player at his position committed there since, he may flip. The portal works the same way: a transfer who lands joins the room for the entrants still shopping.
- **11.3 Top recruits care more about playing early.** The weight on his position room (will he play by his second year) grows with his stars: 1.0 up to two stars, then +0.08 a star, so 1.24 for a 5-star. The same weight applies to transfers.
- **11.16 Portal players go where they'd start and where they're wanted.** The 40 random programs are gone. A program wants an entrant if he would make its two-deep next season, judged against the players it has left. A program with an open starting spot (1.10) still reaches out to anyone who beats its remaining backups. The entrant picks among the programs that want him, by the same utility and room as before. Nobody else calls. The level rules (moving up, dismissals, the level bar) are unchanged.
- **11.17 Redshirts can enter the portal.** A redshirt can now leave for playing time or after a coaching change, not only as a steered kid. His rate is 0.12 on the portal's scale, times his early-play weight (so a 5-star redshirt leaves more often). His staff judge him on practice, so "buried" uses his overall, not game play.
- **11.18 Relationships over the year.** The flat signing-day bond is gone. Each staff recruits the top of each position board all year: three targets per spot in its plan, plus three. The position coach calls and visits every target. The head coach gives his own time to the very top (up to one more than the plan at that spot). Each recruit takes five official visits (the NCAA limit) to the programs he likes best among those recruiting him, and unofficial ones to any within 250 miles. The relationship then depends on how long the staff has known him (better-seen kids are found earlier), how much head coach time he got, whether he saw the campus, the head coach's recruiting and relationships, and the position coach's recruiting. A staff member who knows his high school coach adds trust. A late offer from deep on a board has no relationship behind it. An elite head coach's edge on a top target he hosted is about +0.08 now, against +0.135 flat for every recruit before.

## Trial 7 fix-now list (2026-10-18)

Raichel chose 12 of trial 7's 18 findings to fix now (`docs/trials/world-trial7.md`); the other six wait for the systems that feed them. No trial measured these fixes; quick checks only.

- **Blue-chip walk-ons (finding 4).** A walk-on place costs a recruit his family's tuition, and, when he weighs it against a scholarship offer he has, the scholarship he gives up (`scholarship_given_up`): 1.4 for an FBS scholarship (`WALK_ON_GIVE_UP`, about what a top school's pull is worth to him), scaled by the offering school's level (0.77 for FCS, 0.42 for Division II), and nothing for a specialist, who walks on as a matter of course. It counts both ways in the matching (a walk-on place offered to a recruit holding a scholarship, and a scholarship offered to one holding a walk-on place) and in February's scramble. A recruit with no scholarship offer loses nothing by walking on. A walk-on is last in line for reps wherever he sits on the board. So a blue-chip with FBS offers signs, while a 2- or 3-star can still turn down an FCS or Division II scholarship to walk on at a big program, the usual preferred walk-on story. The first version charged 0.35 a star whatever offers he had: it stopped those preferred walk-ons too, sent that talent to FCS scholarships, and the test world's draft fell below 85% FBS.
- **The 2010 NFL year (finding 5).** 2010 had no cap but teams kept their books: the top-51 rule, the reserve and every cap move still run (`money::cap_books`). Each team spends what it spent the year before, last year's cap plus 2% a point of owner cash will above 10 (`pro::space`). Free agency needed six accrued seasons that year (`money::ufa_seasons`), so the four- and five-year players were restricted. In a small world that ran through 2010, the year had 31 signings with new teams, 8 trades, 122 waiver claims, 230 restructures, 225 cap cuts and 220 restricted tenders (real 2010: about 212 players were restricted instead of unrestricted, from memory).
- **Tags (finding 6).** A team short of room for a tag or tender it wants clears room first (`market::clear_room`, the same cuts and restructures as cap moves), then decides. A player with three or more accrued seasons and an expiring deal is tendered as an RFA under the year's rule. In the quick checks teams placed 4-23 franchise tags a year (trial 7: 0 in most years); 2021-25 ran high at 14-23, against about 6-9 real (from memory, to verify), for the next trial to measure.
- **Comp picks (finding 7).** The ledger of free agents lost and signed kept person indices, and the February compaction moved everyone, so most losses pointed at someone else. Every stored person index (the comp ledger, pending deals, offer sheets, owners' pet players) is remapped when the world compacts (`pro::remap_people`); a trade records the player's id. Only a player whose deal ran out counts as a lost free agent (a released player doesn't, as in the real formula), and last year's leftovers are dropped before the awards. In the quick checks the formula's picks rose from 0-5 a year to about 9-22 (0-32 in a long small world); the rest are still supplemental because few free agents reach the market and change teams (about 100-120 unrestricted free agents a year, against several hundred real, from memory, to verify). That is market volume, left for the next trial to measure.
- **Repeated extensions (finding 11).** A player whose extension was signed this year isn't offered another until it starts.
- **Non-quarterbacks at quarterback (finding 12).** Two causes. Staffs moved quarterbacks to other spots until a room was empty; now a player moves only while his old room keeps its two-deep (`season::reconsider_positions`). And pro scouts' projected position used to move a college player before his last college season; now it is kept aside (`PersonCal::pro_position`) and he changes position only when drafted, with a note on his page.
- **Class rankings (finding 13).** The class ranking news and its table rank FBS programs only; every program's class score is still kept.
- **Long snappers (finding 14).** The class plan counted walk-on snappers promoted at camp as walk-ons, so it kept signing more. A specialist plan now counts everyone at the position against one total and puts at most the scholarship limit of them on scholarship.
- **Player pages (finding 15).** The signing note lists every school that offered him all year, not only this stage's offers; the college stat tab starts at the first year of history.
- **Text (finding 17).** Plurals ("1 season", "1.5 sacks", "1 signee", "1 more year"), era names for franchises in every export (calendar events, pro seasons, NFL games, trades, comp picks, honors), the trade text naming the team he left, each pro once in the export, and the trial report's champions and real-data columns.
- **NFL teams off the engine (finding 18).** At cutdown and each Tuesday in season, a team below 53 signs the best street free agent (or a generated one) at its thinnest spot on a minimum deal; a team at 53 without enough healthy players for game day moves its longest injury to injured reserve (`pro::fill_roster`). It pays for the signing by restructuring, never by cutting someone else: the first version cut to make room, and in the quick check three capped-out teams cut and re-signed every week (about 1,100 cap cuts a year). A team that still can't afford the minimum plays short-handed unless it can't dress a full game-day squad. The engine needs 30 players to play a team's game. Behind most of these teams was dead money: a trade put all of a player's remaining bonus on the seller's cap at once, and in one test world that came to half the cap, so the team cut nearly everyone and still couldn't get under. Now a trade must leave the seller under the cap too, and from June 1 on a traded player's later bonus years land on next year's cap, as with a release (both real rules; `trades::kept_bonus`).
- **Speed (finding 16).** Waiver claims scan the league only on days someone was waived. Position moves, the decommitments after a firing, the street free-agent search and the recruits staffs look at are read on all cores; a recruit's system fit is read without allocating; the fit of the school a recruit holds is kept until that room changes; a search's short list sorts only its top names. Results per seed are unchanged (all 29 export files of a quick world byte for byte). A quick world (4 recorded years after the spin-up, games on the stand-in) built in 595 s instead of 830 s on 4 cores; a full trial world should gain less, as college games, the biggest step, didn't change (an estimate, not measured).

## Player tendencies (built 2026-10-19)

The design is `docs/design/tendencies.md`; the engine side is in `docs/match-engine.md`, "Player tendencies". Code: `tendencies.rs`.

- **Every player has 15 tendencies** (`Player::tend`), 1 to 20, how often he tries something, never how well. A 14-year-old's are drawn wide (sd 4.4) with leans from his body and mind of at most 3 points (a fast kid bounces runs and scrambles more, a disciplined one guesses the snap less), centred so the national class averages 10.5.
- **They change once a year** in the development step (`tendencies::develop_year`), from his own stream, so no other development draw moves. Results teach about a point a year toward what his tools support (faster with football IQ, only for a man who played). His position coaches' teaching takes a hurtful habit down: about 3 a year for a strong coach (16+) with a coachable player, about 1.5 for an average pair, nothing under 8, and a weak one lets it grow. The unit's style pulls a third as hard, more for a moulding staff; the scheme pulls a little (an attacking one-gap front pulls rushers upfield). Habits settle by 26 and barely move after. Lost speed takes a back's bouncing and a quarterback's scrambling down; a major injury leaves a dip in contact willingness that fades by 60% a year. Where coaching moved a number, the old habit is kept (`was`) and comes back under stress, late and close or tired.
- **Coaching styles.** Every coach has five settings (risk, freedom, physical, ball hunting, moulding), read from who he is: risk from ambition, freedom from his discipline (a strict coach gives little), physical from temper, moulding from relationships (a players' coach moulds less), each with a little of his own (`Coach::style`). A unit's style lands two-thirds toward the head coach from its coordinator (`Style::blend`). High school players take their school coach's style; college players their program's head coach and the side's coordinator; pros their club's.
- **AI instructions.** Before each engine game a staff gives its players the instructions its style and eye call for (`GameTeam::give_orders`): a cautious staff tells returners to kneel deeper than 1 or 4 yards and fair catch inside the 10; quarterbacks who force it get "protect the ball" from a cautious staff, checkdown men "take shots" from a bold one; a soft-hitting staff has tough quarterbacks slide; reachers get "high and tight", wide-arc rushers "keep contain" and snap guessers "no snap guessing" from strict staffs; reckless hitters "wrap up"; gamblers in coverage "stay home". "Misfit" means the habit costs him given his tools by more than the staff's eye (film study) can let go (proposed, not confirmed).
- **Plans against the opponent's habits.** Each staff reads the other team's habits from film (`calling::read_habit`, noise shrinking with film study) and uses them: a hard count against snap guessers, double moves and pump fakes against route jumpers, kicking deep against a returner who brings everything out, draws against upfield rushers.

## Trial 8 fixes (2026-10-19)

Raichel agreed to every finding of world trial 8 (`docs/trials/world-trial8.md`) and to the tendencies build's five engine bugs. No trial measured these; the checks were the tests, `match_check` and `tendency_check`. The engine side (findings 6 and 7, bugs 1-5) is in `docs/match-engine.md`, "Trial 8 fixes".

- **Injured reserve ends with the season (finding 1).** A healed player whose team had no returns left sat on reserve for years. At the new league year the reserve lists end: a healed player is back on the offseason roster, one still hurt moves to the reserve/PUP list (`ProCareer::pup`) and comes back when healed without using one of the team's returns. Injuries also heal in the offseason now, a week each Tuesday; they only healed in season, so a late-season injury lasted into the next year.
- **Tendencies spread again (finding 2).** NFL habits had narrowed to 1.1-2.8 (agreed 3.2) because three pulls closed every gap a year. A habit within 3 points of what his tools support is now his style, not a mistake (`STYLE_BAND`): results teach only the part of the gap beyond it, coaching takes a habit down only to its edge, and the staff's style and scheme pull stop there too. The next trial measures the spread.
- **Tendency averages on the middle (finding 3).** `ideal()` read every attribute against one speed norm, so fast positions sat above 10.5 (backs' bounce 13.5 at FBS). Each season the average of what the players' tools support is read per level and position (`tendencies::Centres`; for pros at the NFL year), and a player's ideal is read against it, so the average man at each position sits at 10.5 and a faster one above it.
- **Coach freedom around the middle (finding 4).** Freedom is 21 minus discipline plus the level's average head-coach discipline less 10.5 (`coach::set_freedom_norms`, read each season), so each level has strict and free staffs; top jobs go to disciplined coaches, and FBS and NFL staffs had sat at about 6.
- **FBS walk-on places filled from home (finding 5).** Traced: walk-on offers go down the same boards as scholarships, from the top, and the top of a Division I board holds recruits with scholarships, who turn a walk-on place down since trial 7's fix 4. The boards hold only the recruits a staff sees (rated ones, the top quarter of the class on film, its own commitments), so they ran out before reaching the local unrated kids who are most real walk-ons, and the places stayed empty (FBS rosters at 82 of 105). In the final period each FBS and FCS staff with planned walk-on places still open now offers them to the best unsigned unrated and 2-star seniors from its own state, by film, who meet the NCAA floor and its admissions bar, until its plan is met; programs pick in order of prestige. The walk-on cost is unchanged.
- **Comp picks traced (finding 8).** The formula (`trades::award_comp_picks`) follows the real method: the qualifying bar, values by money, snaps and honors, losses cancelled by signings of the same round, at most four a team and 32 for the league. The supplemental picks come from too few qualifying free agents: about 124-171 unrestricted free agents reach the market a year, against several hundred real (from memory, to verify), so few change teams at a qualifying wage. The NFL market now counts them (`MarketYear::cfa_noted`, those valued at the next award `cfa_valued`, and `comp_formula` and `comp_supplemental`), shown in the trial report's market table and the raw export; the next trial measures where the free agents go.
- **One snapper on scholarship (finding 9).** At camp a walk-on snapper earns a scholarship only when no snapper on the roster has one.
- **Summer signing (finding 10).** The matching still sends offers one at a time, because their order decides who signs where. What it reads is now read once and mostly on all cores: each recruit's fit with every school's scheme once a stage instead of once an offer, and per board entry the offer, the school's appeal and his ties to it, the rest of every board at once when staffs walk deep down their boards (`READ_ALL_AFTER`). On the test world's 105 signing stages the results are identical (a checksum of every stage) and the matching takes 96 seconds instead of 142, 32% less; the whole test world runs 723 seconds instead of 816 on 4 cores.
- **Memory (finding 11).** The trial tool reports memory (resident and peak, `afm_tools::memory_text`) after the history, with the game log on or off, after each future season and in the report's header, so the next trial can read the world's own size against the proposed 4 GB budget.
- **Names (from the faces work).** Race shares for 21,366 rare first names came from three-letter pieces of the name (Dashawn read 61% White). They are rebuilt from voter-file name shares (`voter_files` in `data/names/README.md`, `tools/names/voter_shares.py`): Dashawn 3% White, Antwan and Jaquan 98% and 97% Black.
- **Raw export.** Active pros carry their full player detail in `pros.jsonl`, so `match_check` can play the world's NFL rosters (`tools/rosters/export_rosters.py`).

## Build 9: sections 6 and 7, the world side (2026-10-19)

Built overnight by Cain's ask; what is built now, what waits for section 8 and what waits for a later build is in `docs/design/build9-plan.md`, topic by topic. Checked by the tests only; trial 9 measures it. Every size no real source publishes is Claude's (proposed, not confirmed).

**Section 5 leftovers and S6.3.** Practice squad elevations (2020 on), the protected four and poaching, injury settlements at cutdown, each club's power structure (GM, coach or owner with the final say), other leagues' snaps in development; a signing bonus paid once and the real tag formula (`pro/squad.rs`, `pro/office.rs`, `pro/money.rs`, `pro/market.rs`).

**Section 6 (`world/people.rs`).**
- Traits: the 8 new traits are drawn at 14 with real correlations; ambition is the family's ambition. From 18 into the early thirties each person settles at his own pace (professionalism up, temper down, about 0-5 points); a Pro Bowl or All-Pro season can feed the ego (`year_book`).
- Mood and trust: one mood and one trust in the coaches per player, rebuilt each camp (college, from the depth chart) and each NFL league year: his role against what he thinks he is worth (ego and ambition weigh it), last season's record, his pay against his market (greed), life (stability) and the room, with a dated main reason (`Person::mood_why`). Mood moves development (up to 10%), the portal lean, holdouts, trade requests and retirement.
- The room: captains each year at every NFL club and college program (voted on leadership, standing, play and ego, or picked by a strict head coach); team mood from players' moods weighted by standing, damped by happy leaders; it moves development up to 5% (`settle_room`, `morale`).
- Grudges: a pro cut or traded holds one against the team by how it hurt, his ego and temper, softened by loyalty; it fades each year and raises his price to go back (`grudge`, `ties_q`). A coach he played for pulls him in free agency.
- Support staff: every college program (strength, medical, analytics, academics) and NFL club (the first three) has named, rated heads, by budget and era. About one in eight moves on a year (a good one at a poor program more often). Strength and the player's professionalism feed physical growth, medical feeds recovery (`staff_support`, `support_ctx`).
- Off-field trouble at the NFL: real kinds by era (personal conduct, substances, PEDs, gambling, domestic cases), about 2.5% of players a year, the policies' suspensions with dates, the commissioner exempt list from 2015 and voided guarantees (`pro_trouble_week`, `rule`). College and high school trouble now has a kind.

**Section 7 (`world/pro/scouts.rs`).**
- Departments: each club has a director, national scouts (1 to 1994, 2 to 2009, 3 after) and area scouts (6-9 by era, one more or less by the owner's budget), each with judging, potential, a read for each of 8 position groups, athletic and character reads, network, work rate, conviction and a hidden bias (size, small schools, testing, tape, his conference, his region, speed). About 10% leave each year, good scouts at thrifty clubs more often; retirement from 64; promotions from inside. An expansion club hires its department the spring before its first draft (the Panthers and Jaguars in 1994, the Browns in 1998, the Texans in 2001), and the Browns' scouts went to Baltimore with the team in 1996; trial 9's first run crashed in 1995 because the new clubs had a draft but no scouts.
- Files: a club's file on a prospect is the looks it took (who, what kind, when). Its read is rebuilt from the looks: each person's lasting lean (60%) plus each look's noise (40%, narrowing with more looks); film reads hide physical traits more than live ones; small schools are harder to see; biases tilt by a third of a misread. Combine and pro-day numbers are shared measurements. The GM (with the final say) and a head coach with the power weigh more.
- The year: fall school calls by region at each scout's work rate (fewer in the West), discovery of unlisted seniors through the scout's eye and network, cross-checks of the top names by Dec 1, all-star weeks (the area scout, a national scout at the Senior Bowl), underclassmen on film once they declare, the combine (measured looks, interviews for the top 60, 45 from 2020), pro days, spring cross-checks with the GM, coordinators and the head coach on the top names, and 30 top-30 visits.
- Boards: each club's grade blends its own read with the public grade by how sure it is (an overconfident GM trusts his own more), less a character discount; a do-not-draft line for bad risk or a dismissal. AI picks come from these boards; the shared consensus grade no longer picks for them.
- Draft day: on each pick a club whose tier has run thin, or a rebuilding club, takes calls; a club within 12 picks whose top player is still there and who wants him enough (impatience and risk) buys the slot on the seller's chart with a later pick or next year's. This replaces the scripted quarterback trade. Each pick's why names the club's own grade and the public rank.
- The undrafted rush: each club's bonus pool by era (about $75,000 at the 2011 cap, scaled with the cap), offers to the best it marked, and each player's choice by his path to the 53, the money, the club that scouted him, home and his agent.
- Recruiting: every coach has an evaluation rating (`Coach::evaluation`), and a college board's error comes from its position coach and head coach, each with a lasting lean.
- Memory: files hold only looks, for the draft class only, and are cleared after the rush.

## Trial 9 fixes (2026-10-20)

World trial 9 (`docs/trials/world-trial9.md`) found ten plain bugs, and its test world an eleventh after the fixes. By Cain's ask ("fix found bugs -> build the fixes") they were fixed without a yes or no, checked by the tests only (no world was run after them). Its tuning findings wait for Raichel.

- **5-stars at junior colleges.** Once an FBS class was full its staff stopped offering anyone, so 5-stars whose families waited for February found no Division I place and Navarro took 11-20 a class. An FBS or FCS staff may now go two over its class, and two over its plan at the position, for a 5-star (`recruiting.rs`, `OVER_CLASS`), in the offers and in the final scramble for recruits whose commitments fell through.
- **The combine every year.** The invite flag was never cleared, so anyone invited once was measured at every later combine, pros included. `combine_invites` clears last year's flags, and only declared prospects test.
- **Draft-day trades.** The future pick a club gives to move up is now the latest round that covers the value, not the earliest, or one round dearer if it has dealt that pick, never more, so a late-round move no longer costs next year's first; a future pick promised earlier in the same draft isn't offered again (`scouts.rs`, `trade_on_clock`).
- **The undrafted rush runs in rounds.** Each round every club with room calls its best marked players it hasn't called (room plus four), splits what is left of its bonus pool, and each player, best first, takes the best offer by path to the 53, money, the club that scouted him, home and luck. It ends when no club calls or nobody signs (`scouts.rs`, `rush`). Before, one round of calls left every turned-down place empty.
- **No extension in the league year a deal was signed** (tags excepted), and a tag is only replaced by a deal of two years or more (`market.rs`, `extension_talks`).
- **NFL pay in the mood** is read against the market's own price for his consensus grade and service (`people.rs`, `pay_and_worth`), not a straight line from the top five's pay. A deal the rules price (the rookie scale, undrafted, reserve/future, practice squad, a restricted tender) is read against that scale until he may negotiate after three accrued seasons, as the CBA has allowed since 2011; then a star on his rookie deal feels it.
- **Camp injuries and settlements.** In camp only a vested veteran (4+ accrued seasons) hurt for six weeks or more goes straight to injured reserve. The rest reach cutdown: a club keeps a hurt player it wants on reserve, a hurt player can't make the practice squad, and the others are cut with an injury settlement (`games.rs`, `record_preseason`; `pro/mod.rs`, `cutdown`).
- **First-year cash.** No signing pays more than 38% of that year's cap in year one, bonus and pay together (real highs about 35-38%: Rodgers 2018, Prescott 2024; 41% from build 10, finding 6). Any bonus above that line becomes guaranteed base in the later years, so the deal's money and guarantee stay the same (`money.rs`, `Terms::limit_first_year`; free agency, re-signings, extensions, street signings and the veterans generated at the start of history).
- **Text.** Team possessives read "the Jaguars' camp" (`pro::possessive`), and coach news uses a singular verb for a school ("Alabama fires", "hires", "extends").
- **Python cache files** are no longer committed (`.gitignore`).

## Trial 9 findings built (build 10, 2026-10-20)

Cain said yes to all 14 findings of world trial 9 (2026-10-20 07:44: "yes implement these final fixes then we'll go to actually build"). They were checked with the code's own tests, `match_check`, a career unit test for habits and one world without the game log for memory (finding 12); no trial world. Counts below are from the tools' 3-year test world (seed 7, NFL games on the engine in 2025 only) unless named otherwise; the next trial measures the world.

1. **Dynasties.** Traced on trial 9's raw output: from 2028 Texas signed 32 5-stars straight from high school and took 86 more from Navarro, the junior college an hour from Dallas; Ohio State 50 and 47. Of the 304 5-stars who started at a junior college, 80 moved on to Texas and 43 to Ohio State. That route was trial 9's bug 1 (5-stars at junior colleges), closed in PR #60, so no other leveler was added. The next trial measures titles again.
2. **The run game.** See `docs/match-engine.md`, "Trial 9 findings (build 10)": about 5.05 yards a carry in FBS and 4.45 in the NFL expected in the world (real 5.12 and 4.50).
3. **Habits keep their spread.** The style band is 5 points (was 3), for results, coaching and the staff's style and scheme pull alike (`tendencies.rs`, `STYLE_BAND`). A unit test plays 1,500 prospects from 14 to 25 through high school, FBS and the NFL: the spread stays at 3.0-3.5 points (agreed 3.2; at 3 points 1.9-2.6). `tendency_check` measures the engine's side, which this doesn't change.
4. **Local walk-ons.** In the final signing period a local senior holding a Division II, III, NAIA or junior college place now weighs a Division I walk-on place against it, the scholarship he would give up included, and takes the walk-on place when he values it more; his small-college place opens (`recruiting.rs`). Test world: FBS rosters average 86.3 with 18.6 walk-ons a roster (three classes only; trial 9's median was 80, real near 105).
5. **Practice squad moves in season.** Each Tuesday a club with an open place on its 53 fills its thinnest spot with the best man it can get, read the way it reads a free agent (`market::worth`): its own squad player (his upside under 26, a point for knowing the playbook), another club's unprotected squad player (a point of margin needed, 56 or better), or the street's three best by the league's read, less the cost of time out of football (3 points at no sharpness). Its own player is signed up to a two-year minimum deal; anything else goes to the roster fill. Practice squads below their size sign street players they have room for the same day (`squad.rs`, `poach`, `sign_up`, `refill_squads`). Squads now hold veterans in the places the real rules open to them: from 2014 two players with up to two accrued seasons, from 2016 four, from 2020 six with any experience (`squad::veteran_places`, at the cutdown and in season). Without them every squad held only young players, a street veteran always beat them for an open place, and in the test world only 15 squad players were signed up in 14 years. Test world: 144 poached in 14 capped years (0-44 a year; trial 9: none in 25). Sign-ups stay few, 22 in those years (0-8 a year): in season the street's best are still rated above the squad players, so most holes are filled from the street (the next trial measures who fills them).
6. **First-year cash and restructures.** A signing bonus is 55% of the guarantee, at most 35% of the deal (Prescott 2024 33%, Lamar Jackson 2023 28%, Bosa 2023 29%, Watson 2022 20%); the rest of the guarantee is guaranteed base. Year one's line (bug 11) is now 41% of the cap, the real high (Prescott 2021: $75M against $182.5M), and before the league year no restructure may build a later cap hit past 26% of that year's cap (Watson 2025, $72.9M against $279.2M) (`money.rs`, `FIRST_YEAR_MAX`, `CAP_HIT_MAX`; `market.rs`, `terms_for`, `clear_room`). Test world: the highest first year is 40.7% of the cap, the next 26.5% (trial 9: 41 seasons over 30%, up to 50%).
7. **Extensions add at least two years**, except for a player past 32 (`market.rs`, `extension_talks`).
8. **Draft boards price positions the way real clubs spend round one** (`recruiting.rs`, `draft_top5`, from 2000-2025 first rounds): tackles and corners up, receivers and edge rushers down from their veteran pay. Test world, three drafts in each of two runs (96 picks a run, too few to tune on): receivers 13.5% and 12.5% of round one (real 13.4%), edge rushers 11.5% and 21.9% (16.8%), tackles 12.5% and 5.2% (12.1%), corners 12.5% and 13.5% (11.9%), quarterbacks 13.5% and 15.6% (9.4%). The next trial measures 20 drafts and the 5-star draft rate.
9. **NFL players content with their role.** A role at least as big as he expects is a lift (0.2), not nothing, and a rookie taken after round 2 or undrafted knows he is earning his place (he expects 0.3 less), as a college freshman under four stars does (`people.rs`, `pro_mood`). Test world: 377 of 2,249 NFL players happy with their role (trial 9: 10 of 2,141); NFL mood mean +0.051 (was -0.012).
10. **Trades.** Traced with a funnel (`MarketYear::trade_funnel`: calls, a player above the buyer's starter, a seller who would part with him, value at the ask, talks failed, no package): about 40 calls a year, a deal both sides value in half of them and a third of talks failing gave 10-17 trades. Clubs now call more in each window (0.45 a window, 1.5 times for a contender), and every Tuesday of the season before deadline week is a window too (0.1), as real GMs call through September and October (`trades.rs`, `Window`). Test world: 13-37 trades a year, 12-22 of them from kickoff through the deadline and 5-11 in deadline week (trial 9: 3-18 plus 2-13 at the deadline; real 15-22 players traded in season, Front Office Sports, 2020-24).
11. **Elevations.** Each game a club elevates up to two squad players, each within his season limit and the game-day limit: first for a spot short of healthy starters and rotation players, then to fill a short game-day list, then for special teams, where a squad player graded more than a point above the weakest dressed backup at a coverage spot (linebacker, safety, corner, receiver, back, tight end, edge) takes his place (`squad.rs`, `elevate`). Test world: 477 in 2025 (trial 9: 0-13 a year; real up to two a club a game).
12. **Memory.** Measured without the game log: 5.24 GB resident after the history, 5.35 GB four seasons on; budget 4 GB (proposed). See "Memory without the game log" below.
13. **Injuries.** The engine's hits now injure at real rates (`docs/match-engine.md`), and practice injures too: each Tuesday from veterans reporting to the Super Bowl, every rostered player has a chance of a practice injury (1.55% a week in camp, 0.9% in season, by his proneness, age and position, specialists a quarter), with real practice injury kinds and lengths set by his recovery and the club's medical staff (`games.rs`, `practice_injuries`; 41% of lower-body time-loss injuries happen in practice, Mack et al., Am J Sports Med 2020). In camp a vested veteran out six weeks or more goes on reserve; in season anyone out past the reserve minimum does. Test world: 218 players on reserve in 2025, about 7 a club (trial 9: 10-41 a year in all; the research's 4+ game injuries give about 5 a club from games), 61 hurt in camp and 15 of them cut with a settlement.

## Final build lane 1: foundations (2026-10-22)

Built overnight for the final build (`docs/final-build/briefs/lane1-foundations.md`). Every new stored field is `#[serde(default)]`, and an older world file fills them when it loads (`World::fill_final_build_fields`, called from `after_load`).

### Birth dates (A1)

- Every person has a date of birth: players (`Person::born`), coaches (`Coach::born`), you (`You::born`, from the setup page), your high school assistants and alumni records. Every age a screen shows counts from it on today's date (`Person::age_on`, `Coach::age_on`, `You::age_on`). The engine's whole-year `age` stays for the yearly development step: it is the age a person carries through a season.
- A school class runs from September to August birthdays: the class of 2030 is born 1 September 2011 to 31 August 2012 (`calendar/born.rs`). A boy whose engine age is `a` in the season of year `y` is born between 1 September `y - a - 1` and 31 August `y - a`.
- Birthdays follow the real births per month (CDC natality: a late-summer peak, a spring low).
- Every draw is keyed by the world's seed and the person's id, so adding birthdays moved no other draw.

### Held back and young for the class (A2)

- About 1 boy in 10 is a year older than his class (held back a grade or started school late) and about 1 in 120 a year younger. Summer birthdays are held back most (15-19%), fall birthdays least (4-6%), as academic redshirting runs in real schools (Deming and Dynarski 2008; NCES). Measured over 200,000 boys: 9.7% held back, 0.8% young.
- A held-back boy is generated a year older (his body and skills at 15 as a freshman), keeps his class, and is 18 as a senior's season starts; a fall-born held-back boy turns 19 in his senior year.
- High school steps that go by grade (freshmen dress last, spring ball, the portal of high school transfers, junior moves, line veterans) read `Person::school_age`, the age an on-time classmate has, never his real age. Reclassifying moves his class and so his grade (`Person::years_off_class` is his class against his birthday's class); a boy already a year old for his class never reclassifies down again.

### Aura and fame (F15, F16, X6)

- **Aura** is a hidden 1-20 trait (`Traits::aura`): charisma, camera presence and style. Drawn at 14 on its own key with a small link to ego (0.2) and leadership (0.15); measured correlations about 0.2 and 0.15. It grows a little: about one point between 15 and 27 on average, more with media training at a big program (prestige above 0.5, from 2000) or in a pro's first three seasons. It is not in `Trait::ALL`, so no trait list shows it. The game crate shows only a scouted word (`fame::aura_read`: "A natural in front of a camera" to "Shy of the cameras").
- **Fame** (`fame::fame`, 0-1) is what the public knows: his stage (a recruit's stars, an FBS program's prestige, the NFL), how well he plays (his season's level rating), his role, his position (quarterback 1.0, backs and receivers 0.8, guards and tackles 0.35, a long snapper 0.15), his market (a club's TV households: New York about +10%, Green Bay -15%) and his honors (Pro Bowls, All-Pro, all-state).
- **`fame::aura_appeal(person, fame)`** is what brands and audiences see: fame times Aura (0.4 at Aura 1, 0.85 at 10, 1.6 at 20), scaled to 0-1. Aura multiplies fame and never replaces it: a backup with Aura 20 stays below a star with Aura 5. **Other lanes call this** for ads, sponsors, documentaries and anything else that follows a public face.
- **`fame::brand_money`** is the year's ads, brand NIL deals and endorsements: in the NFL about $50 million at the very top (Patrick Mahomes earned about $60 million off the field in 2024), low six figures for an average starter, little for a backup, the market a third of today's in 1996. In college, brand deals only from July 2021, up to about $2.5 million; the collectives' payouts stay separate and the brand money comes on top of them on 1 July (`recruiting::nil_payouts`). High schoolers' deals are not modelled.
- **Followers** (`Person::followers`, public) move each year toward what fame and Aura hold (an elite NFL star about 5 million, a backup about 40,000, a 5-star recruit about 100,000), from nothing before 2006 to the platforms' full reach by 2016; they fall slowly when a player fades. Set at the February book (`fame::year_book`, with `people::year_book`), along with `Person::brand`.
- **No quarterback pay premium for looks or Aura (F16):** contracts read football only. Nothing in the sim reads a face.

### Linked names (L2)

- Calendar events and news desk stories keep the ids of the names in their text: `Event::refs` and `NewsItem::refs`, each a byte range of the text and a `Link` (a player by id, a coach, a college program, a high school, an NFL club, or a game by its day, level and home team). The format and the builders (`person`, `coach_link`, `school_link`, `hs_link`, `club_link`, `game_link`, `NewsItem::link`) are described at the top of `calendar/events.rs`; every lane that writes a line naming someone adds its refs there.
- Filled for every line the engine writes that names a person, game or team: games and scores, polls, awards, records, signings, commitments, offers, visits, decommitments, the portal, declarations and withdrawals, draft picks and trades, NFL signings, tags, extensions, suspensions, Pro Bowls and All-Pros, Halls of Fame, hires, firings and extensions of coaches. "On this day" lines carry the old line's links.
- The game crate cuts a line into parts (`calendar::events::segments`) and links the program or club a line is filed under even when its writer didn't name it.

### Memory compaction

The game holds the whole world in memory. In season it now fits the agreed 4 GB (measured in `docs/game.md`, "Memory (final build lane 1)"). Every change keeps the same results per seed: the values are the same bits, only how they are kept changed.

- A player's season rows (`player::SeasonRecord`) keep their optional numbers (performance, the first-year dip, the spring gains, the pro situation) as `OptF64`: 8 bytes, with "none" as a NaN that no rating ever is. A row is 136 bytes, not 176, and is written to files as before.
- Season rows, high school lines, notes, position changes and college staff rows grow by exactly one. Before, a list doubled its room when it filled, so a world of three million rows held room for twice that.
- A stat book row (`records::SeasonRecord`) keeps only the numbers that aren't zero (`records::StatSet`: a bit per stat and the values in order). A lineman's row has no passing or kicking numbers. The book went from 0.60 to 0.28 GB. It is written as the full list, so world files keep their shape.
- Last year's college season keeps only what the next year reads (its results, the rivalry series and the academy trophy) once the new season starts. Its games, rosters and stat lines went with it before.
- The new class of 14-year-olds is built straight into the people list's room, in order, not in a list of its own first.

### Decided in the final build (Claude's pick)

- Memory: kept every value's type (an 8-byte float stays 8 bytes) so no result moves, and compacted how values are held instead; the game hands freed pages back to the system after a load and after each Continue (`afm-game/src/memory.rs`).
- Held-back rates by birth month (8-19%) and young-for-class rates (0.3-2.2%): weighed NCES retention (about 10% of high schoolers have repeated a grade) against kindergarten redshirting (summer boys most); a boy is held back at most one year.
- Old world files get no held-back boys: a birthday inside each person's own class keeps every grade, roster and result the file had; held-back boys come with every class built after.
- Aura's links (ego 0.2, leadership 0.15), its growth (8% a year from 15 to 27, +10% at a big program, +15% in a pro's first three seasons) and its multiplier on fame; fame's weights by stage, role, position, market and honors; the money and follower scales above. Weighed against real off-field money (Forbes 2024), On3's NIL valuations and the follower counts of real stars and backups.

## Section 6, people: the final build (lane 6, 2026-10-22)

The world side of the people leftovers from build 9 and your own side of section 6, plus the takeover list (T1-T23, T-B), the network (N1-N7) and NFL items P2, P3, P4 and P8. The code is `world/folk/` (one module per part, listed below). The game side is `crates/afm-game/src/lane_people.rs`, the screens `app/ui/screens/people.js` and `people.css` (`docs/game.md`, "People").

**Memory.** The people around a team (bosses, boosters, reporters, the trainer, the academic advisor, the owner's family, feeder coaches) are drawn from a stable key (team, role, slot, term) whenever a screen or a review asks, and never stored: the same principal has the same name, age and temper every time, and a new one takes over when his term ends. People the world already keeps are read from it (a college's athletic director and support heads, an NFL club's GM, owner and scouting director). What is stored is small: your trust with each person you have met, the scenes waiting for you (80 at most), the takeover file, sparse ties (pairs of ids), the open court cases, compact high school staffs and dated mood reasons only for players who have one. `World::folk` is `#[serde(default)]`, so old worlds and saves open.

**The line bank (`folk/voice.rs`, `data/lines/*.txt`).** Every spoken line is put together from written parts (an opening, the point, a detail, a close), each in many versions (1,234 written in 95 parts). A speaker's voice (his traits, age, level, job, era, where he grew up) picks which versions he can say, the world's facts fill them (names, records, dates, the man you replaced), and each speaker remembers the versions he has used with you and reaches for one he hasn't. A part with nothing that fits falls back to a plain line in the code.

**Scenes (`folk/scene.rs`).** A meeting, talk, press conference or call is a scene: the person, the place, what he says and your answers as picked moves over two to five exchanges. A scene that waits for you posts a line on the calendar, so Continue stops on its morning; a meeting set for a later day stays shut until then. Any scene can be handed to your staff, who play it the way they would; one you skip is handed at the end of its day (a press availability or a call after a week). Scenes that come to you file a letter in your inbox from the person who would tell you (`folk/letters.rs`): your boss on the school's letterhead, the communications director's memo before a press conference, the trainer before a second opinion, a parent's own email. Scenes you start yourself send nothing.

**Taking over (`folk/takeover.rs`, T1-T23).** Your first two weeks are dated meetings in a real order. College: the team meeting the first evening, how the world took your hire, the introductory press conference and the athletic director's goals the next day, the holdover coordinators one by one and then the position coaches, the state of the team on film, the captains, the trainer, the academic and conduct report, who might leave, the recruiting coordinator's calls to wobbling commits, up to five boosters, the beat reporters, the tour and the plan. NFL: the owner's goals on day one, the introductory press conference, the general manager, the holdover coordinators, the roster review with the GM, the trainer, the conduct report, calls to the leaders, who might want out, the owner's family, the beat reporters, the facility and the calendar to the draft; the first team meeting waits for the offseason program in April (the CBA allows no work with players before it). High school: the principal and athletic director at 7:15 the first morning, how the town took it, the local paper, your first athletics period, the holdover staff, the old coach down the hall, the state of the team, the seniors who lead, grades and eligibility, who's hurt, who might transfer, the booster club president and up to two boosters, the parents' meeting, the feeder tour, who covers you and the plan to the season. Everything the meetings tell you goes into the takeover file with who said it and his angle; after spring ball the holdovers' claims are checked against what happened ("oversold", "undersold"). The welcome letter carries the two weeks as a table, each meeting a link.

**The coaching-change window (T-B).** At a college, your hire opens a portal window for your players starting 5 days after it (15 days from 2025, 30 in the 2022-24 windows era; none before), for your players only, and the portal leans of players tied to the old coach rise ("his head coach is gone"; `college::lean_push`, `recruiting.rs`'s `window_only`).

**Talks (`folk/talks.rs`, people topic 5).** One-on-ones (his role, his touches, his money, his behavior, life away from football, the team, his future), team talks, parents' calls and the lines you give a position coach (keep him level, push him, tell me if he's slipping). You pick a move (ask, be straight, praise, challenge, criticize, promise, warn, hand it to a coach, listen) and say it in any tone (calm, warm, firm, fiery); before you say it your staff tells you what they expect and how sure they are, by the reading coach's relationships, yours and how long you've known him. The answer is written lines with tells your read catches. A move that fits his personality and the moment moves trust and mood the right way; a wrong one backfires. Promises are logged with a date and checked on it; breaking one made in public costs double. People time is a budget: 16 hours a week out of season, 10 in it, refilled on Monday.

**Mood (people topic 3).** The parts the world already kept (role, winning, money, coach, life, the room) stay in `people.rs`; talks, promises, friends, outside noise and discipline add dated reasons that fade by about a quarter a month, so a player's page can say why he feels the way he does.

**Ties (`folk/ties.rs`, topic 2).** Close friends (up to six), formed each fall camp from time together, more in the same position room, class or high school; leaders and loyal players make more and deeper ties, a big ego or temper fewer; they fade by a third a year apart. Mentors: a veteran at the same position with leadership and professionalism takes a young player under his wing, and a bad veteran teaches bad habits the same way. Family: brothers in the world and coaches' sons. Friends on the roster lift a mood and losing one dips it; a friend leaving through the portal pushes the lean; a friend on a team pulls in free agency.

**Team rules and discipline (`folk/rules.rs`, topic 8, P2, P8).** Your rulebook (curfew, being late, class, study hall, weight, dress, phones, social media, drug tests, conduct), each with how strictly you enforce it. Violations come from professionalism most, then temper and ego, mood, age and the room; a long strict list catches more and wears on veterans, a loose one lets small things grow into off-field trouble. You hear of a violation only as well as your staff sees it, and you answer within each level's real limits (the CBA's Article 42 fines and four-week cap; no fines in college; a high school's code). Consistency is tracked: the star held to the walk-on's rule, or not. A leadership council can take cases. AI teams' rules come from their head coach. League discipline runs on the real scale: NFL fines and suspensions for hits by era, college targeting ejections from 2013, high school ejections. Rookie rituals (P8): banned, within limits or the veterans' call, with the bills and stories that come with it.

**Courts and appeals (`folk/courts.rs`, topic 7).** Trouble runs its level's real process with dates: the arrest, the charge, court dates and the outcome, then the league or association, then the club or school. An NFL suspension goes to the hearing officer, a team suspension past four weeks to an arbitrator, an on-field fine to the appeals officers; each appeal takes weeks. One incident is one case (update 2, lane 1): before, a college or high school player's closed case opened again the next Wednesday while his season's trouble flag stood, and a dismissal for team rules could reopen an old arrest from his notes.

**Bosses (`folk/boss.rs`, topic 12).** Every boss is a person: the athletic director and president at a college, the owner and his team president at a club, the principal, athletic director and superintendent at a high school. His trust moves with results against his goals, how trouble, money and the media are handled and how you treat him. Goals are set in meetings on each level's calendar (the first meeting, before each season, a midseason check-in, a review after the last game); you can argue a bar down or take a higher one for time and money, and ask for staff money, a facility project, recruiting or NIL money, roster spots or an extension. He tells you in words, never a score. His trust feeds your job security (`review_push`) and a big booster's patience (`booster_push`).

**Press (`folk/press.rs`, topic 9).** The introductory press conference, a Monday availability and a postgame podium at a college or club (an NFL coach who skips is fined), the local paper's call at a high school. Reporters ask from the week's facts (the last game, the quarterback battle, injuries, the rival, the hot seat, a player in trouble, recruiting). Public criticism of a player counts double; reporters you treat well treat you softer, ones you freeze out dig, and one with sources inside the building prints what he hears.

**Staff (`folk/staffing.rs`, topics 10 and 11).** Every assistant has a deal (NFL two or three years; college coordinators multi-year with buyouts where the program pays, the rest year to year; a high school stipend on a teaching contract). Jobs the world doesn't keep as coaches (NFL position coaches, quality control, analysts, graduate assistants, every AI high school's staff) are people drawn from the job and its term. You hire and fire: candidates start with who you know, then coaches out of work, then assistants a rung down; an interview is a talk (a confident talker interviews better than he coaches); an offer reads money, the step up, ties and your name, his team can counter, and the level's permission rules apply (a club can block a lateral move, not a promotion; a college deal carries a buyout; a high school needs a teaching opening). Firing costs the rest of his deal, and the room reacts by what it thought of him. Your coordinators and department heads bring you proposals.

**Lives (`folk/life.rs`, topics 6 and 13, P3, P4).** Pros' and your own players' life events at real rates by age (births, weddings, a parent's illness, deaths, divorce, money trouble, community work): a birth in season can cost a game, a funeral a few days. Coaches' families are drawn from the coach (spouse, children, their ages); their health is read from age and stress (the hot seat, losing): heart trouble, exhaustion, a medical leave with an interim, and a serious event pushes toward retiring. Second opinions (P3): a player's camp can ask for an outside specialist; you can agree, refuse or bring him back early, and a rushed return carries an 8% a week chance of reinjury. Star power (P4): a star with standing asks for things (touches, a teammate, a scheme) and the room watches how you answer.

**Network (`folk/network.rs`, N1-N7).** The coaches you know and how warm each tie is; reaching out by text, call or lunch, which costs people time, and neediness people notice; a ledger of favours asked and done; the real calendar's trips (the AFCA convention in January, the Senior Bowl, the combine, spring clinics); warm ties that open doors (a name for your staff, the grapevine on jobs, a word for a player, a school's interest); the coaching all-star games; speaking at clinics.

Typical pairs sit at 0.45, where nothing changes. A cold pair busts up to about 20% more option routes, throws up to 0.05 s later and about 5% wider; a fully warm pair busts about 25% fewer and throws on time. - **Specialists on the draft board** (`PositionAligner::for_draft`, front office's find: a punter went 10th in a test world). Every club's board, the draft's public order and your own board lined positions up so that the same standing among his peers gave the same grade, which put the best kicker, punter and long snapper of a class in the top 40: your board had a kicker 9th and a punter 15th in the 30-year world, and its history drafts took 5.9 kickers and 4.6 punters a year, one kicker at pick 32 and a punter at 49 (real 2000-2025, nflverse: 2.15 kickers, 1.96 punters and 0.35 long snappers a year, one kicker in round one, no punter before pick 70). Now each specialist position is read off its own class in order: its best at the class's grade at 45% of the draft's picks (kickers), 50% (punters) or 70% (long snappers), each next one a quarter of the draft lower, the rest past the draft; a read between two of his class falls between their grades, so one great kicker doesn't push the rest out (the 2026 class's best kicker graded 87 against 81 for the next), and a read above the best rises to at most the class's grade at 18% of the draft (kickers), 27% (punters) or 70% (long snappers). Draft day carries no flat discount for them any more (the December read keeps its 2 and 5 points, so the public's boards still rank them late); a club still takes one only with a need, and never two at one specialist position in a draft (one club took two kickers in a test draft). The shares are of a full-size draft (`recruiting::full_draft`, the picks over the world's scale): a small test world's 13 picks stand for a whole draft, and against them its kicker went 5th and a punter and a long snapper sat in its board's top 5. Measured on two 15-year worlds on the stand-in (10 drafts each): 1.6 and 1.8 kickers a year (earliest picks 140 and 97, medians 187 and 160), 1.9 punters in both (earliest 118 and 108, medians 160 and 151) and 0.1 long snappers (one, at pick 170); starting the ladder earlier (30% and 35%) gave 2.7 kickers and 3.3 punters. The world trial's section 7 prints these lines.
- **Memory**: the seat's looks are the only new store of any size (a few bytes a look, pruned of players out of football each new football year, kept for My players); every read is rebuilt from them when a screen asks.

### Decided in the final build (Claude's pick)

## Final build lane 5: world fixes (2026-10-22)

### Eligibility by era and the long roads (S11, A3)
- `world/eligibility.rs` holds every college player's clock. Through 2025-26: four seasons inside five years from his first full-time enrolment. A season he never played in is a redshirt.
- **Five years to play five (S11).** Real: the Division I Cabinet adopted it on June 23-24, 2026, for 2026-27. From the 2026 season FBS and FCS players get five seasons in five years. Redshirts, the four-game rule and hardship waivers end there; every year on a roster counts. Division II, the NAIA and junior colleges keep the old rules. It is in the rule book from 2026, no longer a possible change.
- **Medical hardship.** An injury that ends his season after no more than three games or 30% of his team's, whichever is more, gives the season back. Two seasons lost to injury (a hardship or a hurt redshirt year) earn a sixth year when the clock runs out with a season unplayed.
- **The 2020 COVID year.** Nobody's 2020 season counted and every clock gained a year. A senior whose extra year is all he has left decides at the declaration deadline whether to use it: starters 60%, rotation 45%, backups 25%, the rest 12% (Claude's pick inside the AP's count of about 8 a FBS team). A 2020 senior back in 2021 didn't count against the scholarship limits that year (the NCAA's exemption).
- **Missions.** A Latter-day Saint (keyed from his home state's share: Utah 42%, Idaho 19%, Wyoming 9%, under 2% in most states; about a third of Pacific Islander families) may serve two years: as a signee before enrolling (the school holds his place) or after his first college year. 40% of members with a place served before October 2012, 45% after the age dropped to 18, 30% in the NIL era; before 2012 most went after a season, after it most at 18. Top recruits serve less. An unsigned member near the top of his state can serve and walk on when he gets home.
- **Military service.** About 3% of the unsigned graduates near the top of their state enlist; a quarter of those (Claude's pick) mean to play again and walk on after a four-year term, at 22 or 23.
- **Away from football** is its own stage: no games and no coaching, a year older each fall; the clock stops while he is away. His page says where he is and which school holds his place. On his return a held place becomes a signing; otherwise he walks on at the best program in his home state where he would make the back half of the two-deep.
- **Moving up from below.** A Division II standout can go straight to FBS, and NAIA and Division III standouts can move up to FCS or Division II (`portal_open`; Division III to Division I needs no waiver). JUCO, prep school and FCS-to-FBS were built before.
- Counted each year in the calendar log: medical hardships, sixth years, COVID returns and declines, missions, enlistments, returns, far moves up.

### Real towns (final build)
- Every generated high school sits in a real town (`data/real-world/hs_towns.csv`, built by `data/real-world/scripts/hs_towns.py` from the 2010 Census by ZIP code). Each state gets its towns biggest first until its programs have a place each, with a tenth to spare, so hamlets without a school stay out. A city takes about one school per 35,000 people, at most 40.

- Schools in one county share a recruiting area (area pipelines and nearby non-district games).

### Town weather (U-e)

## Poll and award voters (final build)

The polls and the Heisman are voted by people, not read from a formula (`world/voters.rs`, `world/polls.rs`; section 6 topic 9).

- **AP poll:** 62 sportswriters and broadcasters, each with a home state, an outlet (real papers, sites and networks) and seven habits stored as bytes: how much last week's ballot holds him, brand bias, résumé against eye test, margin, homer lean, attention and doubt about Group of Five teams. About four leave a year and are replaced. Names are drawn from the voter's id when shown.
- **How a ballot is made:** one shared read of the season per release: record, who you beat and lost to (a loss weighs more against a weak team, at home, by a lot, and late in the year), a margin and schedule power rating, yards a play for and against (the eye test), the preseason view and the brand. Each voter weighs it his way, then holds toward last week's ballot after this week's results moved each team. A team that won the head-to-head moves above a close team it beat unless it has more losses. Points 25 to 1; ties broken by first-place votes.
- **Preseason:** last season's final AP rank, what the roster brings back and the brand.
- **Homers:** own state and conference count a little; half as much from 2007, when AP ballots went public.
- **Coaches Poll:** 65 FBS head coaches, conferences in proportion, about two in three kept each year. Coaches watch less, lean harder on last week, and rank their own team and conference higher. If a coach is fired, his program's new head coach takes the ballot. A final Coaches Poll comes out after the bowls; its ballots are the public ones.
- **Harris poll (2005-2013):** 114 voters, the BCS's human poll in those years.
- **BCS standings (1998-2013):** from 2004, the average of the human poll's share (AP through 2004, Harris after), the coaches' share and the computers' share (six computers, highest and lowest dropped). Before 2004: poll ranks, computer ranks, schedule rank / 25, losses and (2001-2003) a quality-win deduction. The six computers are rebuilt from their published methods without margin: Colley, a Sagarin-style Elo-chess, Massey wins-only, Anderson & Hester, Billingsley and Wolfe (every division).
- **Playoff committee (2014 on):** 13 members (five sitting ADs, two of the world's retired head coaches, a former first- or second-round pick, a former writer, four administrators), three-year terms, the first ones staggered. A member leaves the room for his own school (an AD's school, a coach's last head job, a player's college). It ranks by the protocol: lists of 30, then rounds of six ranked with three placed each, until 25. Members read the whole season with every week alike, strength of schedule, game control and conference titles. Close pairs are settled by head-to-head and common opponents. There is no preseason anchor. Weekly rankings on Tuesdays from the real first date. Selection day reads the committee's final ranking (BCS era: the BCS standings; before 1998: the AP poll), then the rest by power rating.
- **Heisman:** 870 media voters (145 in each of six regions), the world's own former winners and one fan ballot (from 2007), ballots 3-2-1. Voters lean to their region's players, to quarterbacks and backs, to winning teams, to wins over ranked teams and to August hype (last season's numbers, a returning finalist). A former winner has a higher bar. Finalists: the top three and anyone else with 250 points, up to six. The news gives the points and first-place votes. The vote's top ten is kept with the season.
- **Checks the tests forced (final build):** a recused committee member's school ranks by its share of the members who could vote, and a round places a team once three in every thirteen of those members back it. In the first weeks a voter holds his last ballot more (half of the weight in week one, fading by 0.7 a week), moves a team by places (about 8 for a full swing of the week's evidence) rather than starting over, and judges an opponent by its preseason view until it has played a few games. The margin and schedule rating is damped (half a step each of 30 rounds), every read is capped at three standard deviations, and a margin against a team from a lower division counts 18 points less. A preseason No. 1 that loses close in week one stays in the top ten.
- **Polls-only era:** the final AP poll's No. 1 is the champion; a different No. 1 in the coaches' final poll is posted as a split title.
- **Speed and memory:** a release is a few thousand small sums. Only each voter's latest ballot is kept (about 10 KB for every panel); polls keep their top 25 with points and first-place votes.
- **Old world files:** the panels are built on load.

## Your front office in the world (final build, lane 10)

`world/front_office/` holds your club's standing choices (`w.front_office`, saved with the world, every field defaulting so older worlds open). - **Contracts and free agency** (`nfl.rs`): your offer is scored by `market::score` against the player's ideal (`market::ideal`, or the one the market set for him); re-signing and extensions clear the same bar as the AI's talks (85%, lower at the tag deadline, higher for a money-minded player with no tie to the club). - **Roster** (`roster.rs`): the cutdown takes your plan first and the staff's `cut_split` for the rest (`seat_cutdown`); the squad's protections are yours (`protected`); waiver claims for your club are yours, held a day on the wire (`seat_claim`, `held_on_wire`).

These change the AI's world too, toward the real rules.
- **Randomness:** every draw is keyed by the career and the player (`front_office::key`), on its own stream: the desk takes nothing from the world's streams. The restructure fix above does change the AI's restructured deals (their injury guarantee), so a seed's world can differ slightly from before the build.

**Earned nicknames (A14 items 4-20, `nick.rs`).** A name comes from a specific, rare moment in the play log that fits the man (a 400-yard passing day, a three-sack game, a fourth-quarter comeback). ## Money and the other leagues (final build, lane 4)

### Club finances at every level (later extras topic 1, X1)

`world/finance/` keeps books for every NFL club, college athletic department and high school (`w.finance`, saved with the world; every field defaults, and `finance::after_load` opens the books of an older world on load). Each book is a fixed array of yearly lines in dollars (`Book<N>`, f32): the budget set when the money year opens, and what came in and went out so far. Games book on their days; everything else books on the first of each month by its real spread over the year (`month_share`). The NFL and college keep every closed year; a high school keeps this year and the last. The real figures behind every line, with their sources, are in `finance/data.rs`; anything no source publishes is marked "proposed, not confirmed" there.

- **NFL** (`finance/nfl.rs`, the money year opens with the league year):
  - **Revenue:** equal national money (TV and NFL Properties), then the club's local money from its market, stadium and fans: tickets, suites and club seats, concessions and parking, sponsors, naming rights, local radio, seat licenses, other stadium events, and the visiting gate pool.
  - **Costs:** players' cash and benefits, coaches and football staff, scouting, medical, travel, player amenities, stadium operations, the business side, and stadium debt.
  - **Attendance is a market** (`demand`, `gate`). Each game draws from the fans' mood, the club's pull, the opponent, the record, the weather, the kickoff and the price, and it sells out when demand passes the seats.
  - **Season tickets** (A17's money side) renew from the fans' mood and the price change. A waiting list fills what is given up, and grows when demand runs past the seats.
  - **Blackouts:** from 1973 to 2014 a home game that doesn't sell out is blacked out locally, and the news says so.
  - **Stadiums:** they follow their real history to 2026. After that the world runs its own. In November a club whose stadium is past its prime asks for public money; a yes starts a new stadium or a rebuild. A string of no's opens the road to moving: at the March meeting 24 of 32 owners must agree, the club pays the relocation fee, plays out its lease, and moves when the new stadium opens (the Raiders' road, 2017-2020).
  - **Values and owners:** Forbes-style values come from revenue times the era's multiple, with more for a big brand and a new stadium. About one club in forty is sold each year (the 2000-2025 record) or handed to the owner's family, at a price from the value. The Packers publish their books each July.
  - **The owner's words:** the coach hears about money in words, never meters (`owner_words`). Your seat sees the published numbers and the football budgets it spends.
- **The NFLPA's team report cards** (P1, `report_cards`): players grade their club on eleven parts of the survey (families, food, nutritionist, weight room, locker room, training room, training staff, strength staff, travel, the head coach, the owner) from what they live with. The grades are public in 2023 and 2024; from 2025, after an arbitrator's ruling, only the club's own players and the agents see them. Free agents and their agents read a club's card in the market's choice (`card_pull`, about 0.94-1.06). A bad card pushes an owner who cares about his name: money goes to food, travel and the family room at once, and a poor building starts a new training facility.
- **College** (`finance/college.rs`, the money year runs July to June):
  - **Revenue:** conference and playoff money, tickets with the priority-point seat donations, gifts, NCAA money, media rights and licensing, basketball and the other sports, and student fees with the university's support.
  - **Costs:** football's coaches, staff, recruiting and travel, scholarships, revenue sharing from July 2025 and the House back damages, facility debt, guarantee games, bowl trips and the other sports.
  - **Each game's gate** follows demand: fans, recent years, the opponent, the rivalry, homecoming, the record, the weather and the kickoff, capped by the seats.
  - **Guarantee games** pay the visitors.
  - **Spring renewals** carry the seat donation, and a bad season dents both.
  - **Facility projects:** each July the ADs decide on them (an indoor field, a football building, a weight room or locker room), two or three years out. The facilities grade moves when one opens. As a head coach you can ask your AD once a year (`ask_facility`); he reads the department's money, the donors and your standing.
  - **Media deals:** each conference's deal stands as it really did in 2026 (the SEC's to 2034, the Big Ten's to 2030, the Big 12's to 2031, the ACC's grant of rights to 2036). A deal that runs out is sold again for what its members are worth to the networks now (`media_value`: fans, name, wins and the home state's market).
  - **Realignment by media money** (after 2026, `realign`): a conference adds a school that raises every member's share, a school goes where it is paid more after its exit fee, and a conference that falls under eight football members fills from below. Moves are announced in the summer and take effect the next season.
- **High school** (`finance/hs.rs`): every school's football budget on a real school's lines.
  - **Lines:** the district's allocation and the coaches' stipends, the gate, the state association's playoff share, pay-to-play fees where the state allows them (common in Ohio, Michigan and California, rare in Texas and the South), and the booster club.
  - **At your own school:** the fundraisers you run fill the booster club's account (six kinds, each once a year, two at a time). The booster club itself, its president and its year are the front office's school desk. Once the running costs are paid, the district's equipment budget is yours to spend, and you can ask the district for more once a year.
- **Coaches' buyouts** land on the employer's books (`finance::buyout`).

The market sets them from what the public can see: each team's strength as the world reads it, home field and the season's scoring. It learns each team's form from its games against the line (`Form`). Upsets are judged by the line, and each head coach keeps a record against the spread (`w.finance.ats`, shown on coach pages). ### UCF's stadium and the stadium data (A4)

`program_from_row` keeps each school's real seats; the level's cap now applies only to what its crowds are read from (`crowd_seats`). ### Conference moves (1a, 1b)

- **1a:** a move is posted on 1 July, the day it takes effect ("officially leaves the Mountain West for the Pac-12 today", `college::post_move`, `officially`). ### Front office findings (from lane 10)

- **Free agents' asks:** an ask never passes the record deal at his position (`money::record_apy`: the top five's average times 1.08), moved for his age as the price is. Past his prime a player's price falls about 4% a year for three years, then 9% a year, down to 40% (Cousins at 35 near 88% of the top five, Brees and Rivers near 70% at 38-39, a 40-year-old about half). The quarterbacks' top-five share is now read against the cap they're paid under (0.2 in 2025), not the share each deal had when it was signed.
- **Trade values by plan:** a club counts the seasons it will use a player. A rebuilding club gets a third of a veteran's surplus from the year he turns 27, so it doesn't spend picks on one and sells its own cheaply. A contender pays 1.15 times for help this season. What a player costs over his worth counts in full either way.
- **Portal NIL by market:** a transfer's offer from a collective is its yearly budget split the way it splits it on 1 July: by the role he would have there, his position and his stars, against the roster's payroll weight without this window's entrants (`portal_nil`). How he played against the level's starters moves it 4% a point (0.6 to 1.6 times). A starting quarterback who played well draws seven figures at a big program; a backup lineman a few thousand.

### The other leagues as full leagues (X7)

`world/pro/other.rs` (`w.others`) runs every other pro league in its real years: the CFL, arena football (1987-2008 and 2010-2019), the World League and NFL Europe, the XFL of 2001, 2020 and 2023, the AAF (2019), the USFL of 1983-85 and 2022-23, and the UFL of 2009-12 and from 2024.

- **The season:** each league plays its real teams by year (`teams_in`), and the divisions as they were (Winnipeg East or West by the year). A league opens camp 21 days before its season, plays weekly on an even schedule, holds its real playoffs and title game (the Grey Cup, the ArenaBowl, the World Bowl, the UFL title game), then closes. Seasons that really stopped stop where they did: the AAF after week 8 in 2019, the XFL after week 5 in 2020, the UFL after week 4 in 2012. The CFL's 2020 season isn't played, and 2021 has 14 games. Every game is on the engine under the league's own rule book (`docs/match-engine.md`, "The other leagues' rule books"). A playoff game plays its overtime out.
- **Rosters:**
  - **Who plays:** players from the street (unsigned pros), then generated players for the rest.
  - **Spring rosters:** a spring team's players go back to the street at the season's end. CFL and arena players stay under contract.
  - **Camp cuts:** each team cuts to its league's roster size by position.
  - **Playbook and line:** a player knows the playbook by his seasons with the team and his football mind, as in the NFL. A spring team, met at a three-week camp, knows less. A line's chemistry counts the linemen who started there last season, as in the NFL, so a new team's line has just met.
- **The CFL's ratio:**
  - **Nationals:** the Canadian ("national") players come from U Sports programs and from American colleges (three in ten) and are known by id (`Others::nationals`).
  - **Fill:** under the CFL each team fills its nationals first, by position share (most on the line, fewest at corner and quarterback).
  - **Starters:** the coaches start at least seven: 7 to 2013, 7 of 21 dressed from 2014 (`ratio`, `ratio_starters`, which swaps the cheapest bench national in for the weakest import).
  - **The draft:** on 1 May the CFL drafts its nationals (`Others::draft`, school by school).
  - **Hometowns:** a national's hometown is a town in his school's province.
- **Coaches:**
  - **Hiring:** every head coach and coordinator seat is filled before camp through the world's coaching market (`carousel`, `staff::fill_others`): out-of-work coaches, NFL and college assistants who want a head job, and the league's own.
  - **Staff:** each staff draws position coaches at the league's pay.
  - **Moving on:** coaches come back to the NFL and college with the stint on their record (Marc Trestman's road). A folded franchise lets its staff go.
  - **Pay and pull:** pay is proposed, not confirmed. A CFL head coach makes about $550,000 in 2026 money, a spring league's about $400,000 and arena football's about $110,000. As jobs they rate like a strong FCS job (the CFL), a little below (the spring leagues) and a small college (arena).
- **Players' records:** each season is a line on the player's page (`OtherSeason`: games, starts, passing, rushing, receiving, tackles, sacks, interceptions, kicks and pay). The lines feed the NFL clubs' pro scouting.
- **People:** the folk modules know the other leagues' clubs (`folk::Team::Other`): an owner and GM, a reporter, the trainers, the club's seasons and fixtures, so a coach there has a boss, a staff and a life like anyone else's. A coach's page shows the job, the league and the club.
- **Memory:** rosters are people indices, remapped at the yearly compaction (`Others::remap`). Nationals and the draft are player ids. A season keeps its table, its games' scores and its champion.

**Your career there** (first post-release update, 2026-10-24): a club is a seat (`Seat::other`, `calendar::Level::Other`, `Job::Other`). Its head coach and coordinator jobs are on your job market (`Interest::other`) and in the career setup. Your club's game each week is built in the morning as the day plays it (`other::seat_setup`, `seat_fixtures`, `seat_opponents`): `play_week` plays your game from that setup when you don't play it live, keeps it with its film (`keep_seat_game`) and keeps your coming opponents' tapes (`tape_watch`). A CFL or spring job doesn't move the family (`family_words`).

## Section 6b, training: the final build (lane 11, 2026-10-23)

Training is built whole from `docs/design/training.md` for every player in the world, AI teams and yours by the same step (`crates/afm-engine/src/training/`).

### How growth lands (topics 12 and 15)
- **The year is decided, then landed by the week.** The yearly checkpoint still runs `develop_season`, but with the world's training on (`WorldOptions::yearly_growth` off) it stores the year's growth as a small `Train` record (34 bytes a player): what each of seven families (technique, mental, strength, speed and movement, conditioning, football IQ, the body) owes, how much has landed, the week's load, the practice grade and the plan's position. `week::monday` lands each AI player's share every Monday from the daily clock; `seat::week` lands your team's from your own week. `close_year` lands anything left at the next checkpoint, so nothing is lost when a season ends early.
- **The landing matches the yearly step.** `grow_share` lands a share with the step's own finishing curve (the gap to the ceiling, the finishing step, the yearly cap), so a year landed in 40-odd shares lands where one step would (the proof: 2.63 a year either way, 0.03 apart).
- **A week's work moves the share.** `work::week_work` counts each period's minutes by family (individual periods teach technique, team periods football IQ, the weight room the body, a walkthrough the mind at almost no load). A family's share is moved by the week's minutes against the staff's neutral week, saturating at 15% either way (`PLAN_REACH`, choice 2): the hardest realistic plan gives about 1% more a year, an all-weight-room plan about 8% less, and nobody grows past his ceiling.
- **The main focus** takes the bigger share of a player's individual periods inside his family; how much sticks rises with coachability. A habit focus coaches one tendency toward a target at the tendency layer's coaching speed (a strong teacher about 3 points a year, an average one about 1.2).

### The week, the block and the rules (topics 1-4)

## The storyline engine (update 2, lane 1, 2026-10-24)

The world's events become stories that last (S1-S11 in `afm/post-release-2/list.md`; the design is the Claude Doc "The storyline engine"). The code is `world/storylines/`; the screens are in `docs/game.md`, "Update 2".

- **A story** (`Story`) is one kind from the catalogue (`catalogue.rs`: 117 kinds in 14 families, each with its question, stakes, tone, quiet days, endings with how good each is, the ratings it tests and the real story it is drawn from), a cast in parts (lead, rival, decider, boss, parent, suitor), what is really going on apart from what the public believes and how sure it is, four audiences' sides (the locker room, fans, the media, boosters or ownership), heat 0-100 day by day, a phase (seed, spark, building, peak, resolved, aftermath, fizzled), beats with their sources, links to other stories, an ending and a legacy line.
- **Detectors only notice** (`detect/`: daily, Mondays and after games). They read the world's own state and never bend an outcome. A private story (a seed) goes public only through a person: a reporter with a source, a player's post, a parent on the radio, a rival's remark, your own words.
- **Heat.** A story opens at a quarter of its stakes times its amplifier: (0.55 + stakes/110) × (0.7 + 0.6 × the cast's fame) × (0.7 + 0.6 × the market's size), ×1.45 in the summer dead zone (June 1 to July 20). Beats add heat through the same amplifier. Every day heat fades: ×0.93 for a public story, ×0.95 for a private one, ×0.965 for one that has its audience's attention. Each audience attends to only so many stories a day (10 nationally, 3 per team, a cap per level and per high school state), so a firing pushes a kicker's slump off the front page.
- **Voices** (`tell.rs`) tell it from the line banks (`data/lines/stories/storylines.txt`): beat writers, national columns, radio, fans, the cast, rival coaches and your staff's memos, with their history ("for the third week running"). A near story gets a voice on 2.5% of days; one with the audience's attention on 4% plus heat/380. Lines rest per key before they come back, a story never tells the same line twice, and a person doesn't hear a line twice in a season across his stories (a coach's hot seat, buyout watch and vote of confidence share the job-security lines), nor does a story that comes back the same season (a decommitted recruit, a second flare-up at quarterback): a voice with nothing fresh to say stays quiet, and a fact that would repeat word for word gives way to the bank's line. Talk keeps a story warm but is no news: only news (it went public, a fact, the day it is forced, its answer, your move or a denial; a no-comment is no news) holds off the end, so a story with no news for its kind's quiet days fizzles, unless a due date keeps it alive.
- **Recruits' stories end with the recruit.** Each Monday the open recruiting stories are checked against their recruit, past what the daily watch follows: one who signed or enrolled anywhere ends them the way it went (stayed home or left, won or lost, held or flipped), and one who left football lets them fade. Before this a recruit who signed after his class rolled over could carry "Will he stay home?" into his junior year of college.
- **One event, one story.** A late pick's rise is told as the late-round gem, not also as a breakout; each quarterback story a start answers ends in its own words (won the job, kept it, split the snaps, took over from the freshman, back under center). A building years in the making is news once a year while the work goes on (as real projects are: the groundbreaking, the steel, the money) and ends with its opening. A recruit's commitment, signing or flip ends all his stories at once: the first tells the fact and the others end in their own words (he is staying home, Georgia won the fight for him; a war lost to a third school says neither side got him), so the news says it once. A quarterback's first start is told once (not again after a benching, nor as a tenth grader who started in ninth grade), a revenge game and a coach's return once a season (the second meeting with a division rival is no new story), a rivalry run's ending gives the new count ("seven in a row now"), and a second arrest is told as one. A senior has one last home game a season and a player one rise (a breakout, a freshman star, a walk-on star; a walk-on's is told as the walk-on star's), and a top uncommitted recruit's story lives until signing day instead of fading in a quiet month and starting over; a stand-in who starts a second time this season does so "again", and a second death in a player's family is "another". A coach "has never beaten" a rival only when his team lost every meeting of his tenure (two a season with an NFL division foe), and the line gives the run: "the Texans have lost 6 straight to the Jaguars".
- **Each level's words.** Lines tagged for a level are used only there: an NFL club is a team or a franchise, never a program; its fans fill the lots, not the student section; boosters, recruiting pitches and the portal are amateur words; a high school coach's fall comes through the principal and the school board, not a press conference, and his team plays on Friday nights (a game line names the game's own day: Thursday night, Monday night). A first-year starter is a freshman in college, a rookie in the pros and a freshman or sophomore in high school, and a kind's name reads the same way ("A rookie under center", "A proud franchise in decline").
- **Names outlast people.** A story keeps its players' names when they leave the world (everyone who left football is dropped each spring), so its question and cast still name a retired player. A team's story led by its head coach (he can't beat a rival) names him as its lead, not only as the man who decides.
- **Effects go through people** (`effects.rs`): moods, the locker room, a recruit's read of a program (`Program::story_push` in the recruiting fit), the boss's review of his coach (`effects::review_push` in the college and high school reviews), the fans and the cast's own choices.
- **Detail follows distance.** Stories around you (your team, your rivals and conference, your recruits and former players) keep every beat; far ones keep their cast, heat and key beats. Ended stories stay two seasons; one that reached 85 heat stays for good, and so do yours. At most 7,000 stories are open at once (the far, cold ones fizzle first past it) and a person leads at most three at a time. After an ending, the same kind rests for its subject for twice its quiet days (30 to 400).
- **The store has its own random stream**, so a seed tells the same stories, and the detectors walk the season's stat lines and snaps in player order, so a loaded save tells the same stories as the world it came from.

### Your side (S5-S10)

- **Moves** (`moves.rs`): back the lead, back the rival, keep it open, no comment, in private, deny, own it, fire back, praise, draw a line, leak, promise, challenge. Each is a beat in your own words and moves the sides, the cast's moods and the truth's chance to come out; the page shows what your staff expects each to do as chips (heat first, three at most).
- **Your part.** You lead the stories about you and decide your team's; in a recruit's story your team is a suitor (the board reads "recruiting him"), because the recruit decides. The other team in a rivalry or a recruiting war makes you the other side.
- **Meetings.** A public story you lead, decide, court or are the rival in brings a meeting once it runs at 30 heat or more: at most three a week, 14 days apart per story, and only when something new happened since the last one. At 55 heat, when you lead or decide, it stops Continue (one stop a day). Left alone, your staff answers for you with a no comment. A line you used on a story this week isn't offered again, and saying it twice does nothing; said again in a later week, the beat reads "again" ("Coach Smith, again: ...").
- **What people think** reads in the story's own terms: the insiders' picks for a recruit, the mock drafts for a prospect, who starts for a battle, whether he stays for a seat, and how sure they sound.
- **What handling it pays** (`credit.rs`, S10). A public story you are in ends with a score from its ending for your side (0.7) and how you handled it (0.45). The schedule builder plays each season's contracts first (a kickoff game in the opening weeks), then fills the rest as before; a program that already hosts an FCS school under contract doesn't buy a second. Contracts go a year after their season. Older saves book their contracts when they load. At most a few thousand contracts, about 32 bytes each.
- **What a game pays.** The money in a game is now read one way (`deals::money`) by the books and the page alike: the contract's guarantee or payout, else (a game the builder filled late) the going rate a bigger program pays a smaller one it hosts. A Group of 5 host now pays about two thirds of what a Power 4 host pays (`finance::data::guarantee`), and a neutral-site game pays both sides (before, it paid nothing).
- **The high school district cycle** (`hs_year::cycle_start`). States realign every two years (even years, as Texas's UIL does each February): classifications are redrawn and districts drawn only in a cycle's first year, from the cycle's own seed, so a district holds for both years, and in the second year each district game's home and away swap. The page shows next season's district games in the first year.
- **Last season, game by game.** Last college season now keeps its games (about a megabyte) and its polls (`World::prev_polls`) through the new season, and the last NFL season stays whole (`World::nfl_prev`, small), so last season's page opens for every college and NFL team, ranks, lines and crowds included.
- **Your seasons kept** (`World::kept_schedules`). On the day the football year turns, your team's season is kept as its page showed it (about 5 KB), up to 40 seasons, so your own career's schedules open in full. Any other season shows its line from the team's book.
- **What the page reads.** College: the games, their kickoffs, lines, crowds and weather, the AP poll or the playoff committee's rankings the day before the game, the conference standings and every team's record by week. NFL: the weeks and slots (time and window), records by week, the playoff games and seeds, the stadiums by their years, and the weather at the stadium at kickoff (`pro::games::venue`). High school: the state's weeks and results (records by week, `HsSeasonState::records_by_week`), the district standings, the weather at 7 PM in the town, and the school's JV and freshman games. Networks by level, era and window come from `world/broadcast.rs`.
- **The year board** (V13) stores nothing. It reads the training rule book's blocks and limits for your level and state, the calendar's dates (`YearDates`) and recruiting periods, the season page, your recruiting files and your staff's planned week. Two small readers were added for it: `season_page::rank_now` (your rank today: the latest AP poll or committee ranking at college, the class poll going into your next game at high school) and `World::hs_class_day` (the day the high school's new class joins).
- **Found on the way, not changed.** The college polls' `last_final` (where each team finished last season, for the preseason poll) looks for last season's final poll in `college_prev`, whose polls were already let go, so it has never found one; the Heisman's look at last season's stat lines has the same gap. Both are left as they are (changing them would move the polls and the award), now that last season's polls are kept in `prev_polls` they could read it.
