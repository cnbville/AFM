# How the world works (section 2)

The build of `docs/design/world.md`. Code: `crates/afm-engine/src/world/`. Trial report: `cargo run --release --bin world_trial -- <seed> <years>` (in `afm-tools`, debug only). The latest trial is `docs/trials/world-trial2.md`.

Everything below that Raichel has not answered is marked **proposed, not confirmed**.

## The shape of it

- `World::new` builds the whole country, then runs 30 years of hidden history before the 2026 start (decision: 30 years). Six extra spin-up years run first so rosters, coaches and prestige settle; nothing from them is kept.
- Since section 4 (2026-10-16) the world runs on a daily clock: each thing happens on its real day under that year's rules (see "Section 4: the calendar" below). Before that, every year ran in one fixed order.
- Each person has his own random stream, so a seed gives the same world on any machine. A 30-year world takes about 100 seconds in release mode.
- Game results are a stand-in until section 3: each team's strength comes from its starters, with home field and a win chance from the strength gap.

## Programs (topics 2, 6, 7)

- Real colleges from `data/real-world/colleges.csv`: 138 FBS, 127 FCS (Chicago State, with no team, is left out), 160 D2. JUCO programs are placeholders ("TX Junior College 1") until the real list is researched (waits on realism question 1).
- Prestige comes from history, recent results, the head coach, facilities, NIL, fans and TV, and conference (topic 7). Recruits read it relative to their division, so a strong D2 school does not out-pull an FBS one.
- 12 EA-style grades. Steady ones move about 15% of the way to their target each year (tradition, academics, facilities, atmosphere, campus life, brand exposure). Fast ones move about 80% (title chances, playing time, coach prestige, NIL, pro pipeline). Conference strength moves only on realignment (Raichel confirmed the split). Two straight titles take a C- program to about B+.
- NIL collectives have budgets that follow results and booster mood, with booster departures. A falling collective sends warnings through the inbox, the news and a falling arrow on the NIL grade, getting louder each year (Raichel's follow-up answer 4).
- Recruiting footprints grow with prestige; pipelines to a high school grow only from players who made an impact, and fade slowly (topic 4).
- FCS playoff: the SWAC and MEAC stay out, as in real life (Celebration Bowl). The Ivy League is in (it joined from 2025).

## High schools (topics 1, 2)

- Each state gets as many schools as the data says. The state's real top programs keep their names; the rest are generated in real towns from the data. Classifications go by enrollment.
- Powerhouses recruit transfers, the national ones out of state too. Transfers follow each state's real sit-out rule from the data.
- High school NIL follows each state's real rule (`hs_nil_allowed` in `hs_states.csv`; 47 of 51 allow it).
- A rare pull lets a kid from a bad home transfer to a powerhouse (Raichel confirmed: rare).

## Players' hidden background and family (topics 1, 5)

- Six background parts: money, safety, family (home stability), school resources, exposure network, academics (GPA). Plus a hidden mental-health stability level (confirmed).
- Hardship adds a little grit, a YAC tendency and mental toughness. It is never a driver.
- Risk: high potential with a bad home, or a perfect setup with low ambition, raises trouble and quitting a little.
- Families have a decider (parent, grandparent, handler, the kid himself) with their own wants. A handler can steer a kid to the wrong school. Siblings and bloodlines are a small influence.
- Scouts see a character report whose accuracy depends on the scout.

## Coaches (topic 3)

- Hidden ability (teaching by position group, scheme, game management, motivation, discipline, recruiting, network, relationships) and a public reputation that lags it.
- Reputation catches up about 30% a year. A title gives a massive boost, bigger the lower the coach's starting reputation (the Cignetti case). Bad results chip it away slowly. Assistants move at half speed.
- Hiring runs on recency bias: the last two seasons count most.
- Coaches age, improve and decline, and pass a little of their teaching to assistants (coaching trees, an influence only).
- Patience when firing grows with tenure (proposed, not confirmed: year-one coaches are almost never fired).

## Recruiting and movement (topics 4, 6, 8)

- Visibility decides who gets seen. Stars come from film and projection, with the lowest-visibility kids underrated.
- Signing day is a matching: programs offer down their boards, and each recruit (or his decider) picks by what he wants: level, brand, playing time, distance from home, academics, development, NIL.
- JUCO: kids go there for grades, late growth or no offers, and move up after 1-2 years or stop. JUCO grades improve while there.
- The portal: playing time, a coaching change, a handler's steer, moving up, dismissal or money. Tampering is quiet and never punished; it makes moving up more likely at schools with small collectives. NIL can backfire on a player.
- The draft: 257 picks plus undrafted signings, scored from the scouted rating and projection. Kickers and punters go late.

## Trial 1 additions (built 2026-10-13)

Raichel's running list from the trial 1 walk-through (`/mnt/project-files/design/world-trial1-feedback.md`).

1. **Prove-it extension.** A head coach with 5+ years, loyalty (no job-hopping, or one move with high loyalty) and real success (a title in his last 8 seasons, or 70% wins over 50+ games) who has two bad years in a row usually gets one more year, more often when there's an excuse (many players lost to the draft, a young lineup). The news reports it. Fail the prove-it year (a losing record, or well below expectation without a strong record) and he's fired.
2. **Ethnicity and hiring bias.** Players and coaches have an ethnicity. It never changes a rating. Players match who really plays at each level (state child population, how often each group plays, and the real FBS mix). AI hiring carries a hidden bias against coaches of color, strongest in the history, bigger at big jobs, fading slowly after 2026, calibrated toward TIDES counts. The player's own hires never carry it. A yearly diversity report appears in the news.
3. **Names** follow ethnicity and birth decade (`data/names/`). Rebuilt 2026-10-14 (world trial 3 finding 9): every SSA boy's name since 1940 (38,669) and every Census surname with 100+ people (167,407), plus suffixes (Jr., II, III), hyphenated surnames, players who go by initials and inner-capital spellings (DeAndre). Shares are proposed, not confirmed; see `data/names/README.md`.
4. **Pipelines.** Impact is judged inside a player's own conference (all-conference, awards, draft round). Pipelines now exist to a high school and to its area, and staff build personal ties to the schools their impact players came from; ties move with the coach. States with more FBS-level talent than their own big programs can sign spill over to nearby Group of 5 and FCS schools.
5. **Coach ladder.** Every college has 8 position coaches under the coordinators. Coaches climb from high school to position coach to coordinator to head coach, or skip steps on buzz (a high school coach with recent state titles and a dominant record gets college looks). A new head coach keeps some of the old staff. The stat-record part of buzz waits for section 3.
6. **Academics.** NCAA tiers decide who can play right away (full qualifier), who must sit year one (academic redshirt) and who must go JUCO. Strict schools have admissions bars and a few special admits a year. A recruit with risky grades is weighed by the school's academic support, and some players grow out of it. College GPA drifts, and a low GPA can cost a season.
7. **Draft value.** NFL teams draft one by one with their own needs and a position value calibrated to 2000-2025 drafts (QBs go first, kickers late). Every pick weighs the player's grade, the team's board, position value and team need. A QB's premium depends on the team needing one, except at the very top. A team with a young franchise QB holding a top-5 pick trades down to a QB-needy team when a first-round QB is there. At the top, great players at lesser positions count for less than their grade (a generational WR rarely goes first). The numbers are proposed, not confirmed.
8. **Player pages.** Height, weight, arm, hand and wingspan from the real combine per position, full combine tests, level ratings each season, the coaches he had, his offers and why he was drafted.

Fixes from trial 1's flags: foster homes no longer give money (flag 3), the NIL life event happens once (flag 4), and unsigned 3+ star recruits get a reason (flag 5).

## Coaching market (dynasty item 11.11-11.15, 2026-10-15)

Raichel approved the five items on 2026-10-15. Why: in 30-year trials one coach held a top program for 12-14 years and nobody hired him away. Every number below is proposed, not confirmed. Code: `world/staff.rs` (market, NFL staffs), `world/coach.rs` (age), `league.rs` (NFL staff slots).

- **Short lists (11.11).** A search for a college head or coordinator job ranks every coach who fits the job by how hot he is, and calls the top names first: 15 for a head job, 10 for a coordinator job. The random draw of 40 names stays, so lesser-known names still appear. Hotness is what a committee can see: reputation with recency bias, +3 for the biggest stage (scaled by the job's prestige), +2 for each conference title and +4 for each national title in his last three seasons there, and up to +3 for a program rising under him (+1 per 0.01 of prestige gained in three years, down to -1). An FBS head job also calls the 3 hottest NFL coordinators. A college search marks down a man who never coached in college by 2 points (no recruiting ties). Position coach searches still draw at random and judge unit records. Prestige still decides who can lure whom (11.13).
- **NFL staffs and hiring (11.12).** Each of the 32 NFL teams has a named head coach and two coordinators (`Team::head_coach`, `oc`, `dc`). The staff sets the team's coaching rating (head coach half, each coordinator a quarter on his side of the ball); the old random staff changes in `advance_league` now apply only to the class sim. Each season a team's record comes from its roster (0.13 win share per roster sd), its staff (0.06 per staff sd) and luck (sd 0.12), so win shares spread about like the real 0.19. The 12 (14 from 2020) best records make the playoffs; the champion and runner-up are drawn by record. Owners fire on wins: 1.6 × (0.5 − last win share) + 0.6 × the year before, less half the coach's result against his roster, past 0.1, times 3.2, times owner patience; 30% in year one, 70% in year two. A new head coach keeps each coordinator 30% of the time; a coach who stays fires a coordinator after a losing year (up to 35%). Candidates for a head job: every NFL coordinator, out-of-work former NFL head coaches and coordinators, and the 8 hottest FBS head coaches. Owners mark down a man with no NFL experience by 4 points (2 for a coordinator job). If nobody says yes, the team promotes its own coordinator.
- **How a coach weighs a move (11.13).** Before, a sitting coach moved only for a prestige gain over 0.03. Now the gain is one part of his "pull", all in prestige points. The money: 0.05 × ln(new pay / old pay), so doubling his pay is worth 0.035. Pay rises with prestige inside each level (FBS head coach $0.6M-12M, FCS $0.15-0.7M, D2 $70-200k, JUCO $50-120k, high school $50-160k; coordinators and position coaches have their own ranges), a winner has had 10% raises per title (to three), NFL head coaches earn $5-14M by owner and coordinators $1.2-4M, and the NFL paid up to twice as much in the 1990s, easing to college levels by 2024 (Claude's reading of 2024-25 pay, to check). A first head job adds 0.12 (0.18 for the most ambitious). The NFL adds up to ±0.04 by ambition (the hidden 1-20 trait the coach already had), and a gifted recruiter gives up his edge there (−0.02); an NFL coach who recruits well is drawn back to college (+0.02). A young core of starters coming back holds a head coach (−0.03). Age past 52 costs 0.003 a year and each season settled 0.0015 (to 12). Each coach now has a hidden home state (half the time where his first job was, else drawn by where players grow up): going home adds 0.03, leaving home costs 0.02. A seat getting hot adds up to 0.03. The move must clear 0.03 (the old bar), then ambition and loyalty set the chance as before. Story lines name the two biggest pulls ("a bigger program and a big raise", "the step up to the NFL", "a chance to go home to TX"), on both programs' stories and in the news.
- **Age (11.14).** Before, a coach at his learning ceiling never declined (only scheme did). Each coach now has a hidden age his sharpness starts to fade (about 59, sd 3.5, 52-68) and a pace (0.08-0.35 points a year, 15% faster each year). Recruiting fades fastest (1.2×), then motivation (1.0×), game management (0.8×), relationships (0.6×), discipline (0.5×) and teaching (0.4×). Network holds. Learning stops at 58. A coach at pace 0.2 loses about 1.6 points of head-coach value from 58 to 65.
- **Early exits (11.15).** Coaches under 60 can retire for their health (0.08% a year at 40, 0.55% at 59) or step away burned out (0.8% a year after ten seasons in the biggest head jobs, scaled by the job's prestige squared and tenure to 15 seasons, ±40% by temper; a coordinator carries 40% of the pressure). After 60 the old retirement shape stays (4% at 60, +3 points a year). Both leave a story line with the reason.
- **Check run** (seed 2026, 30 years, stand-in history, `AFM_ENGINE_FROM=2026`; the report's "Coaching market" section): FBS head coach changes 27.1 a year (real about 20-30); FBS head coaches fired 18.7; hired away by another college 4.3 and by the NFL 0.6 (real about 5-7 and 0.5, Claude's reading, not checked); NFL head coach hires 6.3 a year (real 2015-25 about 7), 4.4 from NFL coordinators and 0.6 straight from college; retirements before 60: 43.7 a year at all levels, 0.4 of them FBS head coaches (real about 0.5, Claude's reading). FBS national-title coaches stayed 15.6 seasons on average at that school (median 14; real title coaches' tenures run about 10-15, Claude's reading); the most titles by one coach was 4 (three coaches). One college head coach went to the NFL about every two years, spread over the 30 years.
- The 3-year world test's FCS draft share bound moved from 12% to 14%: that short world runs 11.5-12.7% on seeds 7-9 before this build and 12.5-13.0% after; the 30-year world gives 6.2% (real about 6%).

## Era limits, NIL and prestige (dynasty items 11.4-11.10, 2026-10-15)

Raichel approved items 11.4-11.10 in the "Dynasty fix" thread (`/mnt/project-files/afm/dynasty-fix/round1-kickers-and-item11.md`). Code: `world/ncaa.rs` (rules by era, NIL budgets, rogue boosters and the NCAA), with small hooks in `program.rs`, `staff.rs`, `season.rs` and `recruiting.rs`. **Status: built and compiling; the 30-year world check run and the trial numbers against real are not done yet** (the build moved to a fresh thread).

How it works:
- **11.4 Roster limits by era.** `ncaa::roster_limits(program, season)` is the one function every roster rule reads: (roster limit, most new signees a year), after any NCAA scholarship cut. FBS: 95 scholarships until 1991, 85 from 1992 (the 1991 NCAA convention; Sea of Blue, "A brief history of NCAA football scholarships"), 25 initial counters a year (NCAA Bylaw 15.5.6.1), until 2024; from 2025 the House settlement's 105-man roster with every player eligible for a scholarship and no yearly cap (CBS Sports, 2025). FCS: 63 equivalencies spread over at most 85 players on aid, 30 initial counters a year (Bylaw 15.5.6.2, quoted in College Sports Journal; 63 since 1994, Wikipedia FCS); from 2025 the 90 FCS programs that opted into the House settlement follow the FBS rule and the 36 that opted out keep the old one (SI, July 15, 2025; the list is in `ncaa.rs`). D2: 36 equivalencies and no cap on players on aid (scholarshipstats.com). JUCO: the NJCAA's 85 scholarships (scholarshipstats.com). The world has no walk-ons yet, so its rosters are the scholarship players.
- **11.5 No NIL before July 2021.** `Collective.budget` is now the NIL money a program can pay players in an offseason (`ncaa::nil_budget`): zero through the 2020 offseason, so the class of 2021 signed without NIL and the class of 2022 was the first with it. The booster base (`base`, `big_booster`) gives in every era: before NIL its money counts toward facilities and prestige only. NIL offers, roster pay, the "bought away" portal reason, the rising-program reach and the collective warnings all start in 2021. Before 2021 the NIL grade ranks the booster base.
- **11.6 Illegal booster money before 2021.** A few FBS programs have a hidden rogue booster with a yearly pot. On signing day he adds cash to his program's offers to a few of the best recruits in its footprint; it works through the recruit's existing money weight, so it sways needy families most. A target is paid only if he signs. Payments surface with a chance a year of 1 - exp(-paid in the last four years / $8M) (four years is the NCAA statute of limitations). A case brings real penalty types through the world's own systems: scholarships and signees cut for three seasons (`roster_limits`), no conference title and no playoff for one or two seasons (`season.rs`), and a show-cause order that ends the named coach's college job and keeps colleges from hiring him (`Coach::show_cause`). The program disassociates the booster. From July 2021 rogue boosters stop (the money goes through the collective), but payments from before can still surface for four years (Tennessee's 2023 case covered 2018-20). Real anchors: SMU (death penalty, 1987), Alabama 2002 (booster Logan Young paid about $150,000 for one recruit; 21 scholarships over three years, a two-year bowl ban), USC 2010 (30 scholarships over three years, two-year bowl ban, one-year show-cause for assistant Todd McNair), Ohio State 2011 (85 to 82 for three years, a one-year bowl ban, a five-year show-cause for Jim Tressel), Miami 2013 (9 scholarships over three years, two-year show-causes for three assistants), Ole Miss 2017 (two-year bowl ban, 13 scholarships, show-causes of eight and five years for two staff members).
- **11.7 A ceiling on NIL budgets.** No booster base gives more than $20M a year (`ncaa::COLLECTIVE_CEILING`): Ohio State's players got "around $20 million" in 2024 (AD Ross Bjork, CBS Sports, July 24, 2024), Notre Dame's collective took in $20.5M in 2023 and the Texas One Fund spent $13.3M (Sportico tax filings via CBS Sports, Dec. 30, 2024), and Opendorse's Blake Lawrence: "very few collectives actually had $20 million dollars to spend a year". Growth slows as the boosters near the ceiling and never passes it. The NIL market grows into its 2024 size (Opendorse Power 4 totals: 2021 $393M, 2022 $667M, 2023 $840M, 2024 $1.0B). From 2025 revenue sharing adds 75% of a $20.5M cap growing 4% a year for Power 4 schools (Front Office Sports: Texas and Texas Tech give football about 75%, $15.37M; CBS Sports: 4% a year), and the American's floor ($10M over three years) for the Group of 5.
- **11.8 NIL offers within real ranges.** No offer goes above the top of the real range for a starter at a major program (`rates.json`, CBS via SI 2024): QB $800K, OL $500K, DL $600K, RB, WR and LB $300K, DB $225K.
- **11.9 A fixed yardstick for prestige.** `prestige_rel` is prestige divided by the division leader's prestige when recorded history began, not by the current leader's. The spin-up years still measure against the leader. A program can now read above 1.0.
- **11.10 Recent results build slowly.** `recent = 0.75 recent + 0.25 season score`, and the season score is no longer capped at 1.0, so a title season counts more than a plain 12-1 season.

Proposed, not confirmed (Claude's picks inside the approved wording):
- D2 rosters stay at 110 (no cap on players on aid in D2); JUCO moves from 80 to the NJCAA's 85 for every JUCO, the California schools too (they give no athletic aid).
- Where no NCAA yearly cap exists (FBS and opted-in FCS from 2025, D2, JUCO), staffs still sign at most about 1.4 normal classes (39, 33, 42, 56), the world's earlier pick.
- The 25-signee limit runs through 2024 as approved; the real NCAA waived it for 2022-23 and 2023-24 (CBS Sports, May 18, 2022), keeping the 85 cap. Raichel may want the waiver.
- The NIL market ramp (each collective follows the Power 4 total), football's 75% of revenue sharing for every Power 4 school, the Group of 5 at the American's floor, and no revenue sharing in FCS and below.
- Tight end offers top out at $300K and specialists at $50K (not in the data).
- Rogue boosters: a yearly chance of 0.02 times the fan base (1.5x with a big booster) to gain one, 10% a year to stop, a pot of $50K-$300K, prices of $150K, $50K and $15K for a 5-, 4- and 3-star, at most four targets a year in the program's footprint. Detection scale $8M. Penalties: one scholarship per $15K found (3 to 30) over three seasons; a one-year postseason ban from $100K and two from $300K; the head coach named in one case in four, otherwise the recruit's position coach, for one year plus one per $100K (up to 8). FBS only.
- The yardstick (the leader's prestige when recorded history began) and the 0.25 weight on the latest season.

## Kickers, punters and roster needs (fix list topic 1, 2026-10-15)

Raichel's yes on items 1.1-1.11 (`/mnt/project-files/afm/dynasty-fix/round1-kickers-and-item11.md`). Code: `world/recruiting.rs` (class plans, boards, camps, walk-ons, portal), `positions.rs` (the long snapper), `game/roster.rs` and `game/flow.rs` (who kicks), `league.rs` (NFL long snappers).

How it works:
- **A class plan per position (1.1, 1.2).** Each staff wants the real FBS roster count at each spot, scaled to its roster limit (`real_roster`, `roster_limits`). It signs this year's hole, or half the hole two years out, whichever is bigger. Two years out, seniors, likely early NFL entrants (read against the last draft) and likely transfers count as gone. Each staff ranks recruits on a board per position.
- **Talent beats need (1.3).** At a full position a staff still offers a recruit who is clearly better than the best one left at its open spots: a quarter of a film standard deviation better at the top program, a full one at the bottom, at most two over the plan. Not for specialists.
- **Specialists (1.4-1.6).** Kickers and punters are graded on leg power, accuracy and the mental side, against their own spot. Their stars come from the kicking camps (top 25 at a spot five stars, 100 four, 250 three, 500 two), not the general ranking. A staff keeps about one kicker and one punter on scholarship; the other kickers, the second punter and the long snappers walk on. A walk-on gets no NIL and pays his way, as at Pioneer League schools.
- **The long snapper (1.7)** is a position. His rating is his snap (the hidden snapping trait). Nobody is placed there in high school; each high school's best snapper who isn't a ranked prospect can be ranked by the snapping camps, and then colleges recruit him as a snapper.
- **Who kicks (1.8, 1.9)**: see `docs/match-engine.md`. A kicker kicks and a punter punts, a big-legged kicker takes the kickoffs, and with only one of them he does both.
- **Portal (1.10).** A program whose starter left and who has no returning starter at that spot reaches out to every entrant there who beats its remaining backups, best entrants first. Item 11.16 has since replaced the 40 random programs (see the next section but one).
- **NFL (1.11).** Every NFL team carries one long snapper. Teams draft a kicker, punter or snapper only when theirs is below par or aging, with a flat discount on top of the old position value.

## Recruiting feel, relationships and the portal (dynasty items 11.1-11.3, 11.16-11.18, 2026-10-15)

Raichel's yes on item 11 (`/mnt/project-files/afm/dynasty-fix/round1-kickers-and-item11.md`). Code: `world/recruiting.rs` (signing day and the portal), with records in `world/mod.rs` (`RecruitingLog`) and `season.rs`. The match engine is untouched.

How it works:
- **11.1 A recruit's own feel for each school.** Each recruit has his own feel for each school: the campus, the people, a friend there, how a visit went. Its spread was 0.04 (in utility points); it is now 0.07. With the staff chemistry below, the total spread is about 0.076, up from 0.05. Two 5-stars now rank the same schools differently.
- **11.2 Recruits see commitments as they happen.** Signing day is still a matching, but the roster view is live. When a recruit commits, he joins that school's position room at once. The next recruit at his position sees him there, counted the way he counts himself (two years on). If a recruit flips, he leaves the room again. A recruit also re-checks the school he holds when a new offer comes: if a better player at his position committed there since, he may flip. The portal works the same way: a transfer who lands joins the room for the entrants still shopping.
- **11.3 Top recruits care more about playing early.** The weight on his position room (will he play by his second year) grows with his stars: 1.0 up to two stars, then +0.08 a star, so 1.24 for a 5-star. The same weight applies to transfers.
- **11.16 Portal players go where they'd start and where they're wanted.** The 40 random programs are gone. A program wants an entrant if he would make its two-deep next season, judged against the players it has left. A program with an open starting spot (1.10) still reaches out to anyone who beats its remaining backups. The entrant picks among the programs that want him, by the same utility and room as before. Nobody else calls. The level rules (moving up, dismissals, the level bar) are unchanged.
- **11.17 Redshirts can enter the portal.** A redshirt can now leave for playing time or after a coaching change, not only as a steered kid. His rate is 0.12 on the portal's scale, times his early-play weight (so a 5-star redshirt leaves more often). His staff judge him on practice, so "buried" uses his overall, not game play.
- **11.18 Relationships over the year.** The flat signing-day bond is gone. Each staff recruits the top of each position board all year: three targets per spot in its plan, plus three. The position coach calls and visits every target. The head coach gives his own time to the very top (up to one more than the plan at that spot). Each recruit takes five official visits (the NCAA limit) to the programs he likes best among those recruiting him, and unofficial ones to any within 250 miles. The relationship then depends on how long the staff has known him (better-seen kids are found earlier), how much head coach time he got, whether he saw the campus, the head coach's recruiting and relationships, and the position coach's recruiting. A staff member who knows his high school coach adds trust. A late offer from deep on a board has no relationship behind it. An elite head coach's edge on a top target he hosted is about +0.08 now, against +0.135 flat for every recruit before.

## Trial 7 fix-now list (2026-10-18)

Raichel chose 12 of trial 7's 18 findings to fix now (`docs/trials/world-trial7.md`); the other six wait for the systems that feed them. No trial measured these fixes; quick checks only.

- **Blue-chip walk-ons (finding 4).** A walk-on place costs a recruit his family's tuition, and, when he weighs it against a scholarship offer he has, the scholarship he gives up (`scholarship_given_up`): 1.4 for an FBS scholarship (`WALK_ON_GIVE_UP`, about what a top school's pull is worth to him), scaled by the offering school's level (0.77 for FCS, 0.42 for Division II), and nothing for a specialist, who walks on as a matter of course. It counts both ways in the matching (a walk-on place offered to a recruit holding a scholarship, and a scholarship offered to one holding a walk-on place) and in February's scramble. A recruit with no scholarship offer loses nothing by walking on. A walk-on is last in line for reps wherever he sits on the board. So a blue-chip with FBS offers signs, while a 2- or 3-star can still turn down an FCS or Division II scholarship to walk on at a big program, the usual preferred walk-on story. The first version charged 0.35 a star whatever offers he had: it stopped those preferred walk-ons too, sent that talent to FCS scholarships, and the test world's draft fell below 85% FBS.
- **The 2010 NFL year (finding 5).** 2010 had no cap but teams kept their books: the top-51 rule, the reserve and every cap move still run (`money::cap_books`). Each team spends what it spent the year before, last year's cap plus 2% a point of owner cash will above 10 (`pro::space`). Free agency needed six accrued seasons that year (`money::ufa_seasons`), so the four- and five-year players were restricted. In a small world that ran through 2010, the year had 31 signings with new teams, 8 trades, 122 waiver claims, 230 restructures, 225 cap cuts and 220 restricted tenders (real 2010: about 212 players were restricted instead of unrestricted, from memory).
- **Tags (finding 6).** A team short of room for a tag or tender it wants clears room first (`market::clear_room`, the same cuts and restructures as cap moves), then decides. A player with three or more accrued seasons and an expiring deal is tendered as an RFA under the year's rule. In the quick checks teams placed 4-23 franchise tags a year (trial 7: 0 in most years); 2021-25 ran high at 14-23, against about 6-9 real (from memory, to verify), for the next trial to measure.
- **Comp picks (finding 7).** The ledger of free agents lost and signed kept person indices, and the February compaction moved everyone, so most losses pointed at someone else. Every stored person index (the comp ledger, pending deals, offer sheets, owners' pet players) is remapped when the world compacts (`pro::remap_people`); a trade records the player's id. Only a player whose deal ran out counts as a lost free agent (a released player doesn't, as in the real formula), and last year's leftovers are dropped before the awards. In the quick checks the formula's picks rose from 0-5 a year to about 9-22 (0-32 in a long small world); the rest are still supplemental because few free agents reach the market and change teams (about 100-120 unrestricted free agents a year, against several hundred real, from memory, to verify). That is market volume, left for the next trial to measure.
- **Repeated extensions (finding 11).** A player whose extension was signed this year isn't offered another until it starts.
- **Non-quarterbacks at quarterback (finding 12).** Two causes. Staffs moved quarterbacks to other spots until a room was empty; now a player moves only while his old room keeps its two-deep (`season::reconsider_positions`). And pro scouts' projected position used to move a college player before his last college season; now it is kept aside (`PersonCal::pro_position`) and he changes position only when drafted, with a note on his page.
- **Class rankings (finding 13).** The class ranking news and its table rank FBS programs only; every program's class score is still kept.
- **Long snappers (finding 14).** The class plan counted walk-on snappers promoted at camp as walk-ons, so it kept signing more. A specialist plan now counts everyone at the position against one total and puts at most the scholarship limit of them on scholarship.
- **Player pages (finding 15).** The signing note lists every school that offered him all year, not only this stage's offers; the college stat tab starts at the first year of history.
- **Text (finding 17).** Plurals ("1 season", "1.5 sacks", "1 signee", "1 more year"), era names for franchises in every export (calendar events, pro seasons, NFL games, trades, comp picks, honors), the trade text naming the team he left, each pro once in the export, and the trial report's champions and real-data columns.
- **NFL teams off the engine (finding 18).** At cutdown and each Tuesday in season, a team below 53 signs the best street free agent (or a generated one) at its thinnest spot on a minimum deal; a team at 53 without enough healthy players for game day moves its longest injury to injured reserve (`pro::fill_roster`). It pays for the signing by restructuring, never by cutting someone else: the first version cut to make room, and in the quick check three capped-out teams cut and re-signed every week (about 1,100 cap cuts a year). A team that still can't afford the minimum plays short-handed unless it can't dress a full game-day squad. The engine needs 30 players to play a team's game. Behind most of these teams was dead money: a trade put all of a player's remaining bonus on the seller's cap at once, and in one test world that came to half the cap, so the team cut nearly everyone and still couldn't get under. Now a trade must leave the seller under the cap too, and from June 1 on a traded player's later bonus years land on next year's cap, as with a release (both real rules; `trades::kept_bonus`).
- **Speed (finding 16).** Waiver claims scan the league only on days someone was waived. Position moves, the decommitments after a firing, the street free-agent search and the recruits staffs look at are read on all cores; a recruit's system fit is read without allocating; the fit of the school a recruit holds is kept until that room changes; a search's short list sorts only its top names. Results per seed are unchanged (all 29 export files of a quick world byte for byte). A quick world (4 recorded years after the spin-up, games on the stand-in) built in 595 s instead of 830 s on 4 cores; a full trial world should gain less, as college games, the biggest step, didn't change (an estimate, not measured).

## Player tendencies (built 2026-10-19)

The design is `docs/design/tendencies.md`; the engine side is in `docs/match-engine.md`, "Player tendencies". Code: `tendencies.rs`.

- **Every player has 15 tendencies** (`Player::tend`), 1 to 20, how often he tries something, never how well. A 14-year-old's are drawn wide (sd 4.4) with leans from his body and mind of at most 3 points (a fast kid bounces runs and scrambles more, a disciplined one guesses the snap less), centred so the national class averages 10.5.
- **They change once a year** in the development step (`tendencies::develop_year`), from his own stream, so no other development draw moves. Results teach about a point a year toward what his tools support (faster with football IQ, only for a man who played). His position coaches' teaching takes a hurtful habit down: about 3 a year for a strong coach (16+) with a coachable player, about 1.5 for an average pair, nothing under 8, and a weak one lets it grow. The unit's style pulls a third as hard, more for a moulding staff; the scheme pulls a little (an attacking one-gap front pulls rushers upfield). Habits settle by 26 and barely move after. Lost speed takes a back's bouncing and a quarterback's scrambling down; a major injury leaves a dip in contact willingness that fades by 60% a year. Where coaching moved a number, the old habit is kept (`was`) and comes back under stress, late and close or tired.
- **Coaching styles.** Every coach has five settings (risk, freedom, physical, ball hunting, moulding), read from who he is: risk from ambition, freedom from his discipline (a strict coach gives little), physical from temper, moulding from relationships (a players' coach moulds less), each with a little of his own (`Coach::style`). A unit's style lands two-thirds toward the head coach from its coordinator (`Style::blend`). High school players take their school coach's style; college players their program's head coach and the side's coordinator; pros their club's.
- **AI instructions.** Before each engine game a staff gives its players the instructions its style and eye call for (`GameTeam::give_orders`): a cautious staff tells returners to kneel deeper than 1 or 4 yards and fair catch inside the 10; quarterbacks who force it get "protect the ball" from a cautious staff, checkdown men "take shots" from a bold one; a soft-hitting staff has tough quarterbacks slide; reachers get "high and tight", wide-arc rushers "keep contain" and snap guessers "no snap guessing" from strict staffs; reckless hitters "wrap up"; gamblers in coverage "stay home". "Misfit" means the habit costs him given his tools by more than the staff's eye (film study) can let go (proposed, not confirmed).
- **Plans against the opponent's habits.** Each staff reads the other team's habits from film (`calling::read_habit`, noise shrinking with film study) and uses them: a hard count against snap guessers, double moves and pump fakes against route jumpers, kicking deep against a returner who brings everything out, draws against upfield rushers.

## Trial 8 fixes (2026-10-19)

Raichel agreed to every finding of world trial 8 (`docs/trials/world-trial8.md`) and to the tendencies build's five engine bugs. No trial measured these; the checks were the tests, `match_check` and `tendency_check`. The engine side (findings 6 and 7, bugs 1-5) is in `docs/match-engine.md`, "Trial 8 fixes".

- **Injured reserve ends with the season (finding 1).** A healed player whose team had no returns left sat on reserve for years. At the new league year the reserve lists end: a healed player is back on the offseason roster, one still hurt moves to the reserve/PUP list (`ProCareer::pup`) and comes back when healed without using one of the team's returns. Injuries also heal in the offseason now, a week each Tuesday; they only healed in season, so a late-season injury lasted into the next year.
- **Tendencies spread again (finding 2).** NFL habits had narrowed to 1.1-2.8 (agreed 3.2) because three pulls closed every gap a year. A habit within 3 points of what his tools support is now his style, not a mistake (`STYLE_BAND`): results teach only the part of the gap beyond it, coaching takes a habit down only to its edge, and the staff's style and scheme pull stop there too. The next trial measures the spread.
- **Tendency averages on the middle (finding 3).** `ideal()` read every attribute against one speed norm, so fast positions sat above 10.5 (backs' bounce 13.5 at FBS). Each season the average of what the players' tools support is read per level and position (`tendencies::Centres`; for pros at the NFL year), and a player's ideal is read against it, so the average man at each position sits at 10.5 and a faster one above it.
- **Coach freedom around the middle (finding 4).** Freedom is 21 minus discipline plus the level's average head-coach discipline less 10.5 (`coach::set_freedom_norms`, read each season), so each level has strict and free staffs; top jobs go to disciplined coaches, and FBS and NFL staffs had sat at about 6.
- **FBS walk-on places filled from home (finding 5).** Traced: walk-on offers go down the same boards as scholarships, from the top, and the top of a Division I board holds recruits with scholarships, who turn a walk-on place down since trial 7's fix 4. The boards hold only the recruits a staff sees (rated ones, the top quarter of the class on film, its own commitments), so they ran out before reaching the local unrated kids who are most real walk-ons, and the places stayed empty (FBS rosters at 82 of 105). In the final period each FBS and FCS staff with planned walk-on places still open now offers them to the best unsigned unrated and 2-star seniors from its own state, by film, who meet the NCAA floor and its admissions bar, until its plan is met; programs pick in order of prestige. The walk-on cost is unchanged.
- **Comp picks traced (finding 8).** The formula (`trades::award_comp_picks`) follows the real method: the qualifying bar, values by money, snaps and honors, losses cancelled by signings of the same round, at most four a team and 32 for the league. The supplemental picks come from too few qualifying free agents: about 124-171 unrestricted free agents reach the market a year, against several hundred real (from memory, to verify), so few change teams at a qualifying wage. The NFL market now counts them (`MarketYear::cfa_noted`, those valued at the next award `cfa_valued`, and `comp_formula` and `comp_supplemental`), shown in the trial report's market table and the raw export; the next trial measures where the free agents go.
- **One snapper on scholarship (finding 9).** At camp a walk-on snapper earns a scholarship only when no snapper on the roster has one.
- **Summer signing (finding 10).** The matching still sends offers one at a time, because their order decides who signs where. What it reads is now read once and mostly on all cores: each recruit's fit with every school's scheme once a stage instead of once an offer, and per board entry the offer, the school's appeal and his ties to it, the rest of every board at once when staffs walk deep down their boards (`READ_ALL_AFTER`). On the test world's 105 signing stages the results are identical (a checksum of every stage) and the matching takes 96 seconds instead of 142, 32% less; the whole test world runs 723 seconds instead of 816 on 4 cores.
- **Memory (finding 11).** The trial tool reports memory (resident and peak, `afm_tools::memory_text`) after the history, with the game log on or off, after each future season and in the report's header, so the next trial can read the world's own size against the proposed 4 GB budget.
- **Names (from the faces work).** Race shares for 21,366 rare first names came from three-letter pieces of the name (Dashawn read 61% White). They are rebuilt from voter-file name shares (`voter_files` in `data/names/README.md`, `tools/names/voter_shares.py`): Dashawn 3% White, Antwan and Jaquan 98% and 97% Black.
- **Raw export.** Active pros carry their full player detail in `pros.jsonl`, so `match_check` can play the world's NFL rosters (`tools/rosters/export_rosters.py`).

## Build 9: sections 6 and 7, the world side (2026-10-19)

Built overnight by Cain's ask; what is built now, what waits for section 8 and what waits for a later build is in `docs/design/build9-plan.md`, topic by topic. Checked by the tests only; trial 9 measures it. Every size no real source publishes is Claude's (proposed, not confirmed).

**Section 6 (`world/people.rs`).**
- Traits: the 8 new traits are drawn at 14 with real correlations; ambition is the family's ambition. From 18 into the early thirties each person settles at his own pace (professionalism up, temper down, about 0-5 points); a Pro Bowl or All-Pro season can feed the ego (`year_book`).
- Mood and trust: one mood and one trust in the coaches per player, rebuilt each camp (college, from the depth chart) and each NFL league year: his role against what he thinks he is worth (ego and ambition weigh it), last season's record, his pay against his market (greed), life (stability) and the room, with a dated main reason (`Person::mood_why`). Mood moves development (up to 10%), the portal lean, holdouts, trade requests and retirement.
- The room: captains each year at every NFL club and college program (voted on leadership, standing, play and ego, or picked by a strict head coach); team mood from players' moods weighted by standing, damped by happy leaders; it moves development up to 5% (`settle_room`, `morale`).
- Grudges: a pro cut or traded holds one against the team by how it hurt, his ego and temper, softened by loyalty; it fades each year and raises his price to go back (`grudge`, `ties_q`). A coach he played for pulls him in free agency.
- Support staff: every college program (strength, medical, analytics, academics) and NFL club (the first three) has named, rated heads, by budget and era. About one in eight moves on a year (a good one at a poor program more often). Strength and the player's professionalism feed physical growth, medical feeds recovery (`staff_support`, `support_ctx`).
- Off-field trouble at the NFL: real kinds by era (personal conduct, substances, PEDs, gambling, domestic cases), about 2.5% of players a year, the policies' suspensions with dates, the commissioner exempt list from 2015 and voided guarantees (`pro_trouble_week`, `rule`). College and high school trouble now has a kind.

**Section 7 (`world/pro/scouts.rs`).**
- Departments: each club has a director, national scouts (1 to 1994, 2 to 2009, 3 after) and area scouts (6-9 by era, one more or less by the owner's budget), each with judging, potential, a read for each of 8 position groups, athletic and character reads, network, work rate, conviction and a hidden bias (size, small schools, testing, tape, his conference, his region, speed). About 10% leave each year, good scouts at thrifty clubs more often; retirement from 64; promotions from inside. An expansion club hires its department the spring before its first draft (the Panthers and Jaguars in 1994, the Browns in 1998, the Texans in 2001), and the Browns' scouts went to Baltimore with the team in 1996; trial 9's first run crashed in 1995 because the new clubs had a draft but no scouts.
- Files: a club's file on a prospect is the looks it took (who, what kind, when). Its read is rebuilt from the looks: each person's lasting lean (60%) plus each look's noise (40%, narrowing with more looks); film reads hide physical traits more than live ones; small schools are harder to see; biases tilt by a third of a misread. Combine and pro-day numbers are shared measurements. The GM (with the final say) and a head coach with the power weigh more.
- The year: fall school calls by region at each scout's work rate (fewer in the West), discovery of unlisted seniors through the scout's eye and network, cross-checks of the top names by Dec 1, all-star weeks (the area scout, a national scout at the Senior Bowl), underclassmen on film once they declare, the combine (measured looks, interviews for the top 60, 45 from 2020), pro days, spring cross-checks with the GM, coordinators and the head coach on the top names, and 30 top-30 visits.
- Boards: each club's grade blends its own read with the public grade by how sure it is (an overconfident GM trusts his own more), less a character discount; a do-not-draft line for bad risk or a dismissal. AI picks come from these boards; the shared consensus grade no longer picks for them.
- Draft day: on each pick a club whose tier has run thin, or a rebuilding club, takes calls; a club within 12 picks whose top player is still there and who wants him enough (impatience and risk) buys the slot on the seller's chart with a later pick or next year's. This replaces the scripted quarterback trade. Each pick's why names the club's own grade and the public rank.
- The undrafted rush: each club's bonus pool by era (about $75,000 at the 2011 cap, scaled with the cap), offers to the best it marked, and each player's choice by his path to the 53, the money, the club that scouted him, home and his agent.
- Recruiting: every coach has an evaluation rating (`Coach::evaluation`), and a college board's error comes from its position coach and head coach, each with a lasting lean.
- Memory: files hold only looks, for the draft class only, and are cleared after the rush.
