# The World Reader app (lane R5)

The reader's Mac app and its browser twin: the shell, the library, search, filters, the year slider, hover cards, "Copy for Claude", the hidden-truths switch, Compare, and the Overview, Tools, Your career and player pages. Spec: `afm/world-reader/spec.md` sections 1, 5, 6 and 7 in the project folder; the look is the mock-up canvas https://claude.ai/artifact/NtrNC4UTjVcqCaFKnsrjPr. The book itself (lane R4) is described in `docs/world-reader.md`.

## Where it lives
- `crates/afm-reader-app`: the commands, `Reader::call(cmd, args)`, as the game's `Game::call`. A debug tool: it links afm-engine with the `debug` feature and R4's `afm-reader` (the book's builder, its `more()` function and its text pages). The game never links it.
  - `book.rs`: the book (SQLite) as the app reads it. It reads the book's own schema, so a table a later lane adds shows up without a change here: tables and columns, rows paged with filters and the year slider, search, links between tables, hover cards from `defs`.
  - `pages.rs`: every page as blocks (`hero`, `tiles`, `list`, `table`, `rows`, `kv`, `chart`, `pair`, `replay`, `links`, `note`). Overview, the topic pages, Tools, Your career, any table, and every entity's page with the player page as the template.
  - `compare.rs`: two worlds, two years, two groups, a player against real players like him.
  - `play.rs`: replaying a logged game snap by snap from the book, and playing any two programs now on the engine from the world file.
  - `text.rs`: "Copy for Claude". A page R4 prints itself (overview, the topic pages, checks, odd things, known issues, knobs, coverage, any table, a person, coach, program, school, club or log record) copies as `afm_read`'s own text with the line that prints it again; a row's page copies as the app's text (every id a name, packed records unpacked) with the `afm_read` line for its raw record; Compare and the play tools as the app's text.
  - `library.rs`: books on the worlds page (`cnbville/afm-worlds`, assets `name.afmbook` or parts `.001`, `.002`), books on this Mac, and the game's world files and saves. A world file or save without a book gets one built on the Mac with R4's `make::build` (and its world log beside it, `name.afmlog`, when there is one) into `books/<name>.afmbook`, with the builder's steps on the progress bar; opening such a file starts the build.
  - `demo.rs`: a stand-in book with made-up numbers, for the tests and for looking at the screens.
  - `bin/afm_reader_web.rs`: the reader in a browser (`cargo run --release --bin afm_reader_web -- 7710 reader/ui`); `afm_reader_web demo <path>` writes the stand-in book.
- `reader/ui`: the screens (one page, plain JS, Archivo as in the game). `reader/src-tauri`: the Tauri 2 app (package `afm-world-reader`, apart from R4's `afm-reader` crate), outside the workspace like the game's. `.github/workflows/reader-app.yml` builds the universal `.dmg`; on `main` it goes on a release `reader-<run>` (never marked latest, so the game keeps that link) and on the public downloads page as `reader-latest`, where the installed reader looks for its updates.
- The reader's folder: `~/Library/Application Support/AFM Reader` on a Mac (`~/.afm-reader` elsewhere, `AFM_READER_HOME` overrides): `books/` and `settings.json` (the switch and the last book). It reads the game's folder (`AFM_HOME` as the game does) for its worlds and saves.

## People and Recruiting (lane R6)
- `areas.rs`: a topic page with panels (People, Recruiting) lists its inventory areas, each with its verdict, its checks as tiles and its panels; `area/<code>` opens one area whole. Panels draw in `reader/ui/charts.js`: bars against real, a histogram against a curve, lines with the real band shaded, a matrix, a funnel, a state map and tables whose rows open pages. The filters bar and the year slider cut a panel's rows where it has the column; a line over ages or rating bands keeps its axis whole.
- `class/<year>` and `state/<XX>`: the panels filed under that page, cut to one class or state, with its own verdict line; a class or state in any panel links there.
- `player.rs`: the player page's own blocks on R5's template. "In plain words" (born, his age on the world's date and whether he is old or young for his class; his body; stars, camp stars, walk-on, junior college, GPA). "Season by season": every season with its year, age, level, team, position, role, depth, rating at the start and end, the season's form, level rating, coaching, his stat line for that year (high school lines, the college stat book, pro seasons) and what happened (events, a step up, a move, the first-year dip, spring, missed games); with hidden truths on, his potential and the world log's why for that step. "His story": his career notes as a timeline, each line under the year its age gives.

## What the app reads from a book
R4's book (`docs/world-reader.md`) is the contract; the app also reads the older stand-in names (`meta`, `issues`, `<x>_id` links) so its own tests keep a small book.
- `book_meta` (key, value): `seed`, `world_commit`, `first_season`, `last_season`, `log_streams` (the logs read), `missing` (a list of plain lines, one badge each, the full line on hover), `world_file`.
- `checks`: `id`, `page`, `areas` (the first places it), `title`, `world`, `real`, `unit`, `mark` (near, off, far off, no data, no real), `tolerance`, `real_source`, `real_years`, `says` and `detail` (the why), and on the hover card `how`, `needs`, `sql` and `issues`.
- `odd` (`level`, `kind`, `page`, `what`, `says`, `facts`, `entity_id`), `known_issues`, `knobs`, `coverage` (`page`, `tables`: a topic page lists its areas with their status and the tables behind it), `real_figures`, `log_whys`, `consistency`, `catalog`, `defs`, `search` (`tbl` and `id` open a page).
- Rows: `id`, a person's `player__id`, or `_row` (child tables, log rows, clubs) picks one. A child table (`people__player__history`) shows on its parent's page through `_parent`; log rows through `id`. Columns link by the book's names: `head_coach`, `coach`, `oc`, `dc` to coaches; `college`, `program`, `job__employer__College`, a college game's `home` and `away` to programs; `hs` to high schools; `pro__team` and `job__employer__Nfl` to the NFL clubs; `home_state` and `state` to states; `id` in the log and person tables to people. A roster or captains list holds places in the people store (`_row`) and shows as names.
- Values: `date` and `born` are days since 1 January 1970 and show as dates; years show without thousands marks; the 67 ratings, their ceilings and the 15 tendencies show as named grids; a list of notes as a list; `rng__` columns are left out. A world log row's packed record (`_more`) unpacks on its own page through R4's `more()` ("Everything the world log kept": nested names joined, ids as names, lists of records as lines), and each log row in a table links to it ("Record"). A development step (`log_growth`) keeps almost everything packed, so its rows also show its level, role, overall and true potential before and after, and its potential drive with the drive's seven parts (luck, coaching, work, playing time, situation, coachability, setback) as columns.
- Filters apply to a table only when it has the column (`level`, `division`, `conference`, `position`, `stars`, `state`; `year` or `season` for the years), never to the book's own lists. The year slider shows the world as it stood: a snapshot table's year (`as_of`), season rows up to that year, people active then (`first_year` to `last_year`).
- Hidden truths: columns marked `debug` in `defs`, or named for them (`potential`, `ceiling`, `x_*`, the hidden traits consistency, temperament, recovery, injury proneness and longevity, `bloom`, `visibility`, `sportsmanship`, a coach's `ability__*`), and tables named `hidden` or `debug`. With the switch off they never leave the reader.

## Measured (browser)
- R4's test book (a 0.2% world, 2 years of history, its world log, 148 MB, 369 tables): overview and topic pages in 1-6 ms, a player page in 15 ms, a program page with its 37 linked tables in 50 ms.
- The stand-in book: every page under 70 ms.
- Building a book in the app from R4's test world with its log: 21 s, 351 tables, 895,638 records, 0.15 GB, peak 0.4 GB of memory (this container, 4 cores). A log record's page reads in 15 ms; sorting all 14,865 growth steps by a packed column takes 1.5 s.

## Picks (proposed, not confirmed)
- The marks' thresholds are the Debug Lab's: near within 5% of real, off within 15%, far off beyond (the canvas's own note).
- The app keeps the last book open between runs, and the hidden-truths switch starts on (the reader is a debug tool).
- Compare years and groups show each numeric column's mean and its 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles; a player against real players like him uses the same position and a draft slot within 8 picks.
- Playing two teams now uses an average road trip's crowd (0.65) and the world as it stands at its last save; it loads the world once (about a minute and 4 GB on the M1) and keeps it while the app runs.
- Growth rows show the drive's parts and the before and after as columns (the rest stays on the record's page); entity pages keep the app's text on "Copy for Claude" because R4's person page prints the raw record.
- The reader's own version runs 0.1.<build>, apart from the game's 0.2.<build>, and it shares the game's update signing key.
