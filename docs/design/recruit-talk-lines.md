# Recruiting talk lines (the 20k run)

Written 2026-11-12, topped up 2026-11-13, for Cain ("I want 20k lines, specifically for the recruiting personal talks"; he raised the API limit to $60 and said go). This is the line bank for the recruiting one-on-ones: a call, a home visit and a transfer's portal call. It is data only. The engine reads it through `world/folk/voice.rs` like every other bank. The talk design (F1, `afm/talk-facts/proposal.md` in the project folder) and the build lanes after it decide when each key is asked.

## Who says a line

A part says who speaks it. A recruiting talk has two sides:

- **The family side** (a high school recruit's call or home visit): `Speaker::parent`, tagged by who decides (`parent`, `grandparent`, `handler`, `himself`), how a parent is around his football (`loud`, `online`, `absent`), region and era.
- **The transfer side** (a college player in the portal): `Speaker::player`, tagged `college`, `transfer`, a stage, a position, a personality, region and era.

Most `recruit.*` parts use the union speaker (`parts.py`, `SPEAKERS['recruit']`: both sides' tags). A line tagged for one side is said only by that side; an untagged line is said by both, so its words fit a parent at a living-room visit and a transfer on the phone alike. The `portal.*`, `transfer.*` parts are transfer only. If the F1 build makes the high school recruit a player speaker, `himself` becomes `hs`; that is a rename in this one file.

Rules every line was held to (`briefs/recruittalks.md`, `check.py`): it asserts the one fact its key is named for and nothing else stored about him; it never names a school, a coach, a number or a day; it never says a visit happened unless the key is a visit; a family-side line shows the family's voice and never claims the shape of the family unless the key is that fact; the word "college" in a tag means the transfer side only; there are no `hs` or `nfl` tags.

## New facts the build needs to ask for the right key

None of these exist as a stored fact the dialogue code reads today. Each is a small read of something the world already knows; the F1 design owns the exact form.

- **Which aspect he cares about, and how strong yours is** (to pick `recruit.aspect.{aspect}.{good|cool|weak}`). The grade categories (12 aspects, `GradeCat`) hold both; the line bank covers seven that read as talk.
- **Your record at his position** (`recruit.record.*`): drafted alumni at the position and who is ahead of him on the depth chart. The key set (ask, strong, mixed, weak) needs a three-way read.
- **Interest tier** (`recruit.open.*`, `recruit.close.*`): warm, polite or guarded for the opening, warm, flat or cool for the close.
- **Call or visit** (the `.call.` and `.visit.` parts).
- **Your years at the school** (`recruit.you.long`, `recruit.you.new`) and **your team's last record** (`recruit.team.*`; the `{last_record}` hole is in the facts list but no line states it).
- **The visit result** (`recruit.visit.liked`, `.meh`).
- **A rival school's name, and miles to home** (`recruit.other.*`). The lines never name the school or give a number; a later bank pass can add `{rival}` and `{miles}` holes once the dialogue code fills them.
- **A transfer's old school** (`transfer.why.*`, `transfer.role.*`). Same: no name today.
- **Commitments** (`recruit.committed.*`): he committed to someone else or to you.

## The one-side-only rule

An untagged line is said by both sides, a parent at a home visit and a transfer on the phone. In the parts about his own life (`recruit.bg.*`, `recruit.committed.*`, `recruit.other.*`, `recruit.want.*`, `recruit.visit.*`) a first-person line that tells his own story ("I said yes to somebody else", "my grades haven't been where they should be") is true of only one side. The first run's checker passed a lot of them; an independent read found it was the biggest single fault. The rule now, in the brief and in `check.py` (`OWN_LIFE`, `FIRST`): in those parts an untagged line may not say I, my or me; a first-person line carries the tag of the side that can say it (`himself` or `college`), and a line about the boy ("my son", "the kid") carries a decider's tag.

- Independent reads (a separate reader with the part's meaning and the speaker beside each line, not the run's checker):

  - Not done: a full read of the first run's 12,253 old lines. From the sample, about 7% of them are weak in these ways. It costs reading, not API money; Cain's call.
