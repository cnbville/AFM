## Section 2: standing (Cain 2026-11-07 19:31 UTC: on 6 "i really like the idea of 6, so much that i feel you're not selling it enough. It could give an entire layer to the game, an internal economy. Fuck i like that but it's a delicate feature, like it should be useful to a championship team, a playoff team or a bottom tier D1 team."; on the others "yes to the rest")

- GR17. The ladder: four levels with each person, Stranger, Known, Trusted, Home. At college it is with high school coaches, trainers, counselors and families; at high school, with each college staff; at the NFL, with each college's coaches, trainer and strength coach, through your scouts.
- GR18. A why on every standing ("Trusted: four visits this year, his Reyes started for you, you helped place Simmons at Elon").
- GR19. What standing changes (GR16's open half): everyone answers; standing sets how much, how true, how early and who hears first. Stranger: the public answer a day or two later. Known: his real list of prospects, the same day. Trusted: the truth about a kid (his work, his family, the injury he plays through) and tape before you ask. Home: he calls you first, before the kid is public.
- GR20. Busy coaches answer in order: a powerhouse coach answers by standing, then by program level, so a stranger hears last and gets the short version; a small-school coach answers everyone fast and pushes his kids on you.
- GR21. The chain reaction (Cain's idea): a high school whose kids do well in college and the NFL climbs (more staffs visit, more exposure, better kids come: move-ins, and transfers where state rules allow); a school that stops producing slowly falls back. New in the world: today prestige is fixed and kids change schools only on your pitch.
- GR22. Placing kids builds standing: help place a coach's kids, even ones you don't take. Cain wants it grown into its own layer, an internal economy, useful to a championship team, a playoff team and a bottom-tier D1 team alike. Its design is asked separately (`placement.md`); nothing in it is agreed until he answers.
- GR23. What builds it: being there (visits, calls, his clinic, your camp), your word coming true, help, how his kids fared with you, and your program's level, weighed by your man's skill with people and the coach's own nature.
- GR24. How it fades: slowly with time away and rivals' visits; sharply with a broken promise (an offer pulled, a kid buried after you said he'd play); your man leaving takes his own ties with him.
- GR25. Warnings: before a level drops you hear it, with the reason and the fix ("Columbus is slipping: no visit since March, Georgia was there twice").
- GR26. Moving up is a moment: each new level comes in that person's own words, at his hour ("From now on you hear about my guys first"), and the morning map shades the area.
- GR27. Standing lives with people: an area's level is its people together, weighted by how much talent each one controls.
- GR28. How long it takes: Known in a few visits, Trusted in a year or two of keeping your word, Home in three to five years, usually after a kid of his did well with you.
- GR29. Yours and the program's: your own ties go with you when you change jobs; the program's ties stay behind, and your successor starts with them.

Turnaround times follow real life (the six-part day's "everything at its real hour"); numbers no source publishes are proposed, not confirmed: see `sections.md`, "a day on the ground".

## Round 5, the race (Cain 2026-11-15 19:02 UTC: "yes"; design `race.md`, college)

- GR64. Within a level, a busy coach's nature decides who hears first: a loyal coach calls the staff he's closest to, a self-serving one the biggest program (GR21). This refines GR20, which put the bigger program first within a level. Example: Georgia State and Auburn are both Home with the loyal Columbus coach, and Georgia State has done more for him, so it hears about his new end on Monday and Auburn on Tuesday.
- GR65. The race (taken as design in the round 5 reply Cain said yes to): every rival staff works the same ground by the same rules, and its recruiters are people with a style, their own ground, a history with you and a career. You see the race in signs (sign-in sheets, the polo in the stands, a kid's visits and posts, the order the news went, what people tell you by their standing, the services' predictions), never in numbers. Both sides can invade, defend, poach a man for his ground, outwork, flip a commit and run the other down, and a claim that proves false costs whoever made it. Money outside the rules exists as rumors and a case if found, and Ground's own work never buys a signature. The AI plays with the same rule book, money, people and favors, knows only what its people know, and its build is checked on GR61's trial worlds.

## Round 6, finding and paying off (Cain 2026-11-15 19:07 UTC: "agree"; design `finds.md`, college)

- GR66. A kid becomes your find when you act first, with an offer while the world still rates him low (his stars and his other offers), not when you hear first. A find you lose to a rival stays your find. Example: Georgia State offers the Columbus end after its June camp as a 2-star with no other offer; Auburn flips him a year later; when he makes all-SEC he's still Georgia State's find, credited to the D-line coach who heard first and to you as the one who decided.
- GR67. Finding and paying off (taken as design in the round 6 reply Cain agreed to): leads come only from work and the crowded public paper, each on a lead card with its blanks, and filling a blank costs hours and always fills it. A find blooms in steps when the world catches up (a fourth star or an offer from a level up; a starting job by year two or all-conference; drafted or All-American), and the lower the world rated him when you acted, the more it pays. A bloom pays the person who told you (his memory and his word), your man (his record, mood and name), your name as a finder (noteworthy, never driving), the news when it's big, and your eye. Setbacks are felt: the oversold kid, the bust, the flip, the grades, the miss from your own ground, the coach who stops calling. The ground report comes each August with the verdict. Your staff's board fills from your ground plus the public view, replacing stars and footprint. Bloom steps and payoff sizes are proposals tuned in GR61's trial worlds.

## Round 7, your staff's ground (Cain 2026-11-15 19:37 UTC: "yes to all"; design `staff.md`, college)

- GR68. Your staff's ground (taken as design in the round 7 reply Cain said yes to): each on-field assistant works areas on the map (GR1) plus his position group, where the area man finds kids and builds the people and the position coach judges them and sells the role. Areas are set at the February meeting from your recruiting coordinator's proposal and can move any Monday (GR47): home ground is fast and new ground slow, a big area can take two men, an empty one shows as misses in the ground report, and a young coach who rides along with a veteran builds ground of his own. Off-field staff stay on campus by each year's rule book. Each man's ground is his people and standing, his ties, his record and finds, his style, his week (road days, hours and tiredness) and his mood. Hiring trades teaching, scheme and ground: a candidate's ground shows only in signs fogged by your network, his own ties and standing come with him while his old program keeps its own, and a high school coach can be hired for his area within the built limits (C17). Losing a man: signs and a warning first, then a counter scene (a raise, a title or a promise, tracked like hiring promises). When he goes, his ties go with him and his commits waver (a warning and one scene each), and some follow him. Your men climb, a man who leaves happy joins your coaching tree, one who leaves angry runs you down, and word travels through their ties both ways. The AI's staffs work by the same rules. Sizes are proposals, tuned in GR61's trial worlds and in play.
- GR69. The college assistant seat (SK76, taken as design in the same reply): your area and your position group are yours, and the rest is your head coach's. His Monday plan gives you road days and film, and you plan your own route; the week's key scenes come to you and the rest play on your own ratings. You bring him up to two kids a week on the built offer chain from the other side, he signs or says wait by his own read, the open spots and his trust in your eye, and a disagreement is a scene in his office. A kid he signs is your find. One he says wait on isn't (GR66), but your head coach, the high school coach you told and the people around them remember, and your head coach's trust grows as the world catches up. He's a person with one memory of you: he sets your area and position goals at the February meeting, you pick your own ground goal and may try the hunt, and his August verdict counts when he reviews his staff. Your record and teaching bring offers, your ground goes with you, a new head coach who knows you keeps you more readily, your head coach can counter to keep you, and your first offseason is guided by your program's recruiting coordinator.
