## What it does

**Georgia State, a new career (Jan 1 start).**
- Jan 1 and 2: the takeover meetings. Then the portal window, your roster's two weeks. Ground stays quiet.
- The morning after the window closes, 5 AM: your recruiting coordinator is at your door. "Portal's done. Let me show you our ground." The map: Birmingham glows (you coached there), the program is Known on Atlanta's south side, Georgia's red is everywhere else. Your job for January: meet your ground before signing day.
- Late January is a contact period, so you go yourself. Three schools he picked: one where they know you, one where they know the program, one he's heard about. The south side coach hands you his list and mentions a junior safety nobody's offered. You watch his tape that night. Your first find, before signing day.
- The Monday after signing day: the February meeting. Your coordinator lays out three goals. Needs: two tackles and a corner. Your AD's word after signing day: a top-6 class in the Sun Belt. A ground goal: open Columbus, your D-line coach's hometown. You add the hunt: find one the world missed.
- All spring the Monday letter has a line for each goal ("Tackles: one committed, one leaning Auburn").
- The week before the first game: the verdict at the map. One tackle, no corner (Auburn flipped him on the Fourth), the class sixth in the conference, Columbus Known. The safety you found in January is a three-star now. Your AD says you're on track. Your D-line coach is a name in Columbus.

## The February meeting

- **When.** The Monday after signing day.
- **How it plays.** A scene at the map with your staff. Your coordinator shows the class you just signed, next year's holes, your boss's word, and the ground as it stands. He suggests three goals. You change any of them.
- **Your director** is your recruiting coordinator (at a program without one, your most senior assistant).

## The goals and how each is judged

- **Your needs:** commitments at those spots on verdict day, from kids your staff (or you, where you've looked) grade as fits.
- **Your boss's:** his recruiting goal, already built: a top-3 or top-6 class in the conference, by class score. Read on verdict day as a pace from your commitments, like his midseason wins pace.
- **Your ground goal:** open an area (Stranger to Known), take one (become its leader) or hold one (stay its leader).
- **The hunt (optional):** a find of yours who's committed to you or rising with the services.
- Partial progress always shows ("one of two tackles").
- Your coordinator's suggestions fit your program and your ground. You can pick a bolder one, like taking a rival's Home area instead of opening an empty one.
- You can swap a goal at any Monday meeting until the end of March. After that they hold to the verdict, since by April the road is booked.

## The race through spring

- The Monday letter has one line per goal, and the map outlines your goal areas.
- A warning comes when a goal slips, in the same voice as GR25 ("The corner's visit to Auburn went well. We're second.").
- The hunt shows only once you have a find to chase.

## The verdict

- **When.** The week before your first game, late August.
- **How it plays.** A scene at the map with your coordinator: each goal met or missed, and why, from the world itself ("Auburn's man was in Columbus four times in May. We were there twice."). Then your boss's word, then the local paper's take.
- **What it moves.** A little each, and never a firing on its own (Cain's rule that influences are noteworthy, rarely driving): your boss's trust, as with any of his pace reads; your staff's mood; your name with the coaches on your ground. Your standing in the goal area moved with the work itself, so the verdict doesn't count it twice. The class itself is still judged at signing day by your boss's review (built).
- **Then** the season starts, and Ground keeps running under it: Friday nights, December home visits, early signing.

## Your first offseason

- **Jan 1 start:** the takeover and the portal first (built). Ground opens the morning after the window closes (agreed), with the walkthrough and January's one job: meet your ground. The contact period lets a head coach go himself, so you play those visits, and one of them gives you a name nobody else has.
- **Feb 9 start** (the built second start): Ground opens on day one, at the February meeting, with the walkthrough first.
- **The guide is a person:** your coordinator, in scenes. He suggests and explains, and never decides for you. You can send him off at any time.
- **Tools open as you need them,** in the first offseason only: the map on day one, the leads board with your first lead, the rivals page when a rival first shows up, the week planner at the February meeting.
- **The first find isn't staged.** The coach's list always has names (rule 1), and your coordinator sends you where your standing is best, so you hear something first. Whether that kid turns out good is up to the world.
