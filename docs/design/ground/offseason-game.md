## 1. What Ground is

From signing day to the first game, the season is over and the game goes quiet. Ground is the game for those months, played every day like the season is. You aren't playing games. You're working your ground: the schools, the coaches and the people around the kids, the roads between them, and the rivals who want the same kids.

What you win is next year. At college, a class you built. In the NFL, a board that beats the other 31. At high school, kids you got seen and a team from your own halls. Over the years you win a name: the coach who finds players.

What makes it a game and not a menu:
- A goal each offseason, and a verdict at the end of it.
- Something to do every week, with a choice and a result you can feel.
- People who remember you.
- Rivals who want the same kids and can beat you to them.
- Discovery that comes from work, never from dice.
- Rewards now (a name, a call back), at the end (the class, the verdict) and years later (the find who blooms).

## 2. The offseason as a campaign

**It opens at the February meeting,** the Monday after signing day. Your director lays out three goals, and you can change any of them:
- **Your needs:** next year's holes (the Needs map, built). "Two tackles and a corner."
- **Your boss's expectation:** the athletic director, the owner or the principal (the bosses, built). "A top-80 class, and keep Atlanta's south side."
- **One ground goal you pick:** take an area, defend one, or open a new one. "Open Columbus."
- You can add a hunt: "Find one the world missed."

**All spring you see the race against them,** on the map and in the Monday letter.

**It ends with the verdict,** the week before the first game: each goal met or missed, and why. It moves your boss's trust, your staff's belief in you and your name with the coaches on your ground. Then the season starts, and Ground keeps running under it (Friday nights, December home visits).

At each seat:
- **College (Georgia State, year one):** two tackles and a corner, a top-80 class, open Columbus. August's verdict: one tackle committed, the corner lost to Auburn in July, Columbus up from Stranger to Known. Your boss is fine with it. Your D-line coach is a name in Columbus now.
- **NFL:** a starting corner (the draft does that, built), one Day 3 starter off your own ground, and a spring list from your Southeast scout that beats the services. The verdict lands at cutdown.
- **High school:** get your three juniors offered, fill the line from your own halls, win over the track coach. The verdict is August's tryouts.

## 3. Your first offseason

A career starts on Jan 1 with the takeover (#266). In the first season, Ground waits until the January portal window has closed, so the takeover and the portal come first (Cain, Nov 13: "otherwise it'll be too much at once"). The steps below start the morning after the window closes; round 1 (`campaign.md`) has the details.

- **Day one.** Your director walks you through the map. Your old ground glows: where you coached before and your hometown (GR7). The program's ground is half lit. Rivals' colors fill the rest. He gives you January's two jobs: keep your December signees, and meet your ground.
- **The first days: the phone.** Early January is a dead period, so you call: your signees and their coaches, the portal (its window is open), and the best coaches on your ground. Each call is a scene.
- **Mid-January: the road.** The contact period opens, and a new head coach goes on the road himself. You visit a school in your best area. The coach knows your name, hands you his list and mentions a kid nobody's offered.
- **Late January: your first find.** You fill one blank: call the coach back, watch the tape, or get the kid to junior day.
- **Signing day, then the February meeting** and your three goals.
- **Spring.** The map opens as your standing grows. Each tool arrives when you first need it: the leads board with your first lead, the rivals page the first time one shows up in your area.

The other seats:
- **NFL.** You're hired after Black Monday. All-star week is your first set piece: your scouts' ground is there, and one of them points you to a small-school kid in practice. You meet each area scout and hear his list.
- **High school.** You take over a school in January. The 6 AM weight room shows you who's committed. The basketball coach's 6'5" forward is bored, and a family moving in calls about their son.

The aim: by signing day, a new player has someone who's starting to trust them, a name nobody else has, and a rival who's noticed. Your director is the guide, as a person in your building, not tooltips.

## 4. The week

The six-part day (#224) already gives every day its hours. Ground fills them.
- **5 AM, the morning map** (GR15): what changed overnight. Standing up or down, new names, a rival in your area.
- **Monday 8 AM, the meeting at the map:** your director's draft of the week (W1). Who goes where, which events, what you host. You change any of it. The choice is real: men, days and money are short, travel eats days, and returns shrink when you keep hitting the same area in one week.
- **The road, Tuesday to Friday:** your men's days. Each school visit is a scene you can play or let run (section 5).
- **7 to 10 PM, the night calls** (GR13): each man calls in with his day, in his own voice. A call that matters stops the day at its minute (GR14).
- **The weekend:** what you host (junior days, your spring game, camps, official visits), the phone, and film to confirm a lead.
- **Every week ends with something:** a name, a filled blank, a standing tick, a commitment, or a warning (GR25).

You can play every scene, or let your director run the week and only take the calls that matter. Both work.

**A week in May at Georgia State, played:**
- Monday: you send your D-line coach to Columbus for two days and your backs coach to south Atlanta.
- Tuesday night: your D-line coach calls in. The Columbus coach told him about a sophomore end who grew three inches. Nobody else has heard.
- Wednesday: you call the Columbus coach yourself. He's guarded (you're only Known), so you're straight with him: his senior corner isn't for you, and you'll help place him at Mercer. He warms.
- Thursday: Auburn's area man is at the same school. The coach tells you himself when he calls back, because you were straight with him.
- Friday, 10 PM: the coach calls. The end's dad wants to see your campus Saturday.
- Saturday: the kid and his dad on an unofficial visit. You show them the D-line room and the strength coach.
- Sunday: his tape confirms the get-off.
- Monday, 5 AM: the lead card says "Auburn: Thursday. You: Tuesday." Columbus is close to Trusted.

## 5. Being there: the scenes

This is the heart of it. Ground today says what a visit gets you. It has to be something you play: a place, the people in it, what you say (live talks), what you notice (your eye), and what you walk away with (rule 1 still holds: it always buys something).

College:
- **A school visit.** 7:40 AM, the front office. The sign-in sheet shows Auburn came Tuesday. In the coach's office, his old college's pennant tells you his ties. He gives you his list; how much he tells and how true goes by your standing (GR19). In the weight room, a sophomore you've never heard of is squatting more than the seniors. The counselor has his transcript.
- **Friday night** (fall). The stands or the press box. Both rosters. A Georgia polo two rows down. The kid's dad by the fence.
- **Your clinic** (spring). 80 high school coaches in your meeting room. You talk scheme, and the ones who run it warm fastest. The talk afterwards is where the lists come out.
- **Junior day.** A recruit and his family on a tour: the locker room, the photo in your jersey, his mom's questions about the degree (talk facts keep every answer true).
- **Your camp** (June). Stations, laser times, the coach who brought five kids watching how you treat them, and the walk-up nobody listed running a 4.5.
- **Official visit weekend.** Friday arrival, Saturday tours and the roster talk, dinner, his player host.
- **The phone** (all year, and all of July). The call to a coach who's slipping. The kid about to announce on the Fourth.
- **The head coach's spring at FBS.** You can't go on the spring road (the 2008 rule), so your spring is your campus and your phone: the clinic, practices open to high school coaches, junior days, the spring game. At FCS and below you go yourself, so the small-school seat plays the road hands-on.

NFL:
- **A pro day.** The stopwatch, the strength coach's word on character, the trainer's word on the knee, the agent working the room.
- **The spring meeting** (May). Each area scout presents his list of rising seniors. You push back on his grades. The names he has that the services don't are his.
- **A school call** (fall). How far in your man gets: practice, the coaches' honest word, the training room.
- **Joint practices and cutdown** (August). Another club's bubble players up close, and the call from your pro scout's friend about who's getting cut.

High school:
- **The 6 AM weight room.** Who shows up. Who's grown.
- **A track meet** you help at. The track coach's 400 runner who's never played football.
- **The college coach at your door** (April and May). Whom you show him and what you say. Your word is your credibility (built).
- **The camp trip.** Your kids in the school van to the camps of staffs you know.
- **August tryouts.** The kids your building gave you show up.

A scene takes about a minute if you talk your way through it, and one line if you skip it. Live talks (built) carry the talking. Talk facts (built) keep every line true to what that person knows.

## 7. The people

- **Standing** is agreed (GR17-GR29): the ladder from Stranger to Home, a why on every level, warnings, moving up as a moment, your ties against the program's.
- **Characters** (never designed): each person's eye, straightness, agenda, what he wants from you and his ties; a face and a voice; a memory of you; jobs that change, taking ties with them.
- **The favor economy** (`placement.md`): its six questions have been open since Nov 7. They belong in this round.
- **One memory per person.** What he thinks of you lives in one place, so promises, talk facts and live talks all read the same thing (today they'd each keep their own).

## 8. The race

- Rival recruiters as named people, with their own ground, a history with you and a style.
- You see them: the sign-in sheet, the polo in the stands, a kid's visit list, and the order the news went on every lead (rule 2).
- Your moves and theirs: invade, defend, poach, outwork, and the AI answering (old part 6).
- July flips, and August's preseason lists turning the race public.
- The AI plays Ground by the same rules, with looks and standing. Today it doesn't (round 2's D11).

## 9. Finding and paying off

- Leads come from work: the coach's list, the camp walk-up, the track meet, the move-in, the other sport, the trainer's client, the box score outlier (crowded, since everyone sees it).
- A lead has blanks. Filling one costs hours and always fills it (old part 4). Film confirms.
- Finds bloom when the world catches up (old part 9).
- **Things go wrong,** and you feel it: the coach who stops calling after you pull his kid's offer (GR24), Auburn flipping your corner on the Fourth, your area scout poached with his ties, the kid who was smaller than his coach said, your boss's class goal missed.
- The verdict each August, the ground report each year, and the long game: pipelines, your name as a finder, the jobs your ground gets you.

## 12. Where the current design falls short as a game

1. **No goal and no finish line.** Ground describes a world to work, not a game to win. Nothing says what this offseason is for or how it went. Without that, February to August is a sandbox: more to do, still no reason to do it. Fix: section 2.
2. **The first month a new player plays has no Ground in it.** Careers start on Jan 1; Ground's year one opens on Feb 2. January is when a real new coach does his hardest ground work: every school in his state, saving the class. Fix: section 3.
3. **Nothing teaches it.** A new player would open a grey map with a dozen systems at once. Fix: section 3.
4. **At FBS, the head coach watches the best months.** The 2008 rule keeps you off the spring road, and the design says so and stops there. April and May turn into spectating at the top seat. Fix: your campus and your phone as scenes (section 5), and lower levels play the road hands-on.
5. **Results, not scenes.** "A school visit always gets the coach's list" is what you get, not what you play. Being there (old section 6) was never designed, and it's where the immersion lives. Fix: section 5.
6. **July and August are a line each.** Real July is commitment season: announcements, flips, the phones, the convention. August is the race going public. Fix: section 6.
7. **Months, not weeks.** A game needs a beat every week; the design's year is monthly. Fix: the week-by-week round.
8. **Rivals are moves, not people.** You never meet their recruiters, and you don't feel the race day to day. Fix: section 8.
9. **Nothing goes wrong.** Standing fades, but there are no setbacks you feel: the flip, the coach who stops calling, the oversold kid, the poached scout, the missed goal. Without loss there's no tension. Fix: section 9 and the verdict.
10. **No time budget for the player.** The offseason is 30 weeks. Played hands-on it must be rich; delegated it must be quick; neither should feel like a chore. Fix: section 4 and the skip on every scene.
11. **The NFL's May to August and the high school's outward ground are thin.** The NFL has the combine, free agency and the draft (built), then two lines. High school has its building, but not getting kids seen: one kid to a camp, hosting college staffs, whom to show, the reel (SK75). Fix: the scenes and months rounds, at all three seats.
12. **It predates what's been built since.** The six-part day, live talks, talk facts, promises, the inbox and the AI staff are Ground's engine, and none is in the design. Left apart, they'd grow overlapping pieces: four ways of tracking what a coach thinks of you, two weekly planners, three weekly reports. Fix: section 13 and one memory per person.
13. **The people aren't characters yet.** The design lists them, but only the high school head coaches are full people today (trainers and 7v7 directors are contact types). The people round was never designed. Fix: section 7.
14. **Under the hood, finding still runs on stars and dice.** Your staff's board is gated by stars and footprint, and a film pop is the strongest finder (round 2's D1-D26). Ground's build has to replace those, not sit beside them. That's for the build threads, not a question for Cain.

## 15. Decisions

### Ground as the offseason game

My proposal:
- The offseason plays as a campaign: three goals at the February meeting (your needs, your boss's, one ground goal you pick), the race against them all spring, and a verdict the week before the first game. Real staffs work to a class plan and their athletic director's expectations, and the verdict gives February to August an ending.
- The first offseason starts at the January takeover, guided by your director, so a new player has a first find before signing day. January is when a real new coach does his hardest ground work: the phone first, then the road.
- Being there is played as scenes: a school visit, Friday night, your clinic, junior day, your camp, an official visit, the phone, a pro day, the spring meeting, the 6 AM weight room. You talk your way through on live talks, or skip and take the result.
- The rest is designed in this order: the campaign and the first offseason; the week and the scenes; the months week by week; the people (with the favor economy's six questions); the race; finding and paying off; your staff's ground; the map and the screens.

4 questions:
1. Play the offseason as a campaign, with goals set in February and a verdict before the first game?
2. Start a new player's first offseason at the January takeover, guided to a first find before signing day?
3. Play being there as scenes on live talks, each skippable with its result?
4. Design the rest in that order, the game first and the systems after?

My pick: yes to all four.
