## What it does

**Georgia State, the last week of April.** The spring contact period is on. You're an FBS head coach, so the rules keep you off the road (they have since 2008). Your road is your staff.

- **Monday, 5 AM.** The map: Columbus went up overnight, close to Known (your campaign's ground goal). Auburn's man was seen in south Atlanta on Friday.
- **Monday, 8 AM, at the map.** Your coordinator has the week drafted: six men out, 24 of the 90 days your staff has left this spring. Three places want a man and you have two left to send: Columbus (your goal, your D-line coach's hometown), Miami (a flight, you're a Stranger there, a rumored 4.4 corner) and Birmingham (Trusted, your old job, and the coach says he has a name he'll give only to you). You send the D-line coach to Columbus for Tuesday and Wednesday and your receivers coach to Miami on the week's one flight, and Birmingham waits a week.
- **Monday, 2 PM, your phone.** You call the Columbus coach yourself. He's guarded, since you're barely Known. You're straight with him: his senior corner isn't for you, but you'll help place him at Mercer. He warms.
- **Tuesday, 8:40 PM, the night calls.** Your D-line coach: the Columbus coach told him about a sophomore end who grew three inches. Nobody else has heard. You ask what he saw of the kid himself. "Only in the weight room. Big hands, moves well for his size." Your backs coach: a Georgia staffer offered the south Atlanta junior back today. You send him back Thursday, one more day off the 90.
- **Thursday.** Auburn's area man is at the same Columbus school. The coach tells you himself when he calls back, because you were straight with him.
- **Friday, 10 PM.** The coach calls: the end's dad wants to see your campus tomorrow. This one stops your evening (a live moment, GR14).
- **Saturday, 10 AM, on campus.** The kid and his dad on an unofficial visit. You walk them through the D-line room and the weight room, and your strength coach shows the dad the plan for his son's frame. The dad asks what happens if you leave for a bigger job. You answer him yourself.
- **Sunday.** His tape confirms the get-off.
- **Monday, 5 AM.** The lead card reads "Auburn: Thursday. You: Tuesday." Columbus is Known. The Monday letter opens: "This week: two new names, Columbus Known, the end's visit done."

Played this way, the week took about 12 minutes. Handed to your coordinator, it takes one: you read the Monday letter, and only Friday's call stops you.

**LSU, the week of the spring game (mid-April).** No road here either, so your campus is the road. Friday is your clinic: 300 high school coaches in the team room and at practice. You talk third-down pressure, and the ones who run your kind of defense stay to ask more. Saturday is the spring game: 40 recruits in the stands on unofficial visits and the coaches in a suite. You have an hour between warm-ups and kickoff, and three families want you.

**Mercer (FCS), a Tuesday in late April.** The rule is FBS-only, so Mercer's head coach drives to Columbus himself. 7:40 AM: the front office's sign-in sheet shows Georgia's name from last week. In the coach's office, his Valdosta State pennant says where his ties run. He hands over his list (you're Known, so it's his real list, the same day: GR19). Down the hall in the weight room, a sophomore is squatting more than the seniors. Your eye caught it; a duller eye walks past. The counselor has his transcript. You ask the coach about him, and he's careful: "He grew three inches since Christmas. Nobody's been in for him." You leave with the list, a name nobody else has and a step toward Trusted. Skipped, it runs on his own skill and eye and gives one line: "Columbus: the coach's list (3 names), Georgia was in last week." Whether the sophomore makes that line is up to his eye.

## Your week

- **Monday:** the morning map, then the meeting at the map. Your coordinator (your director, round 1) drafts the week: each man's days and places drawn as routes, the calls to make, what you host and the film to watch. You change any of it. It's the week's one planner (question 1). Your staff's assignments and the film queue, built today as separate pieces, become rows in it.
- **Tuesday to Friday:** the road for your men, and your campus and your phone for you.
- **Every night:** the night calls. Each one is a talk with your man, who knows only what he saw (talk facts).
- **The weekend:** what you host (junior days in February and March, unofficial visits, the spring game, official visit weekends from late May, your camps in June) and Sunday's film.
- **Every week ends with something:** the Monday letter's first line says what the week bought (a name, a filled blank, a standing step, a visit set, a commitment or a warning), and round 1's goal lines follow. The recruiting desk's Monday digest (built) becomes this letter.

## The FBS head coach's spring

The rules keep an FBS head coach off the road in the spring contact period, and they have since 2008. FCS head coaches go. So you play the scenes you're at, and your men's visits come to you as their night calls. At FBS your spring levers are:

- the meeting at the map, since where your men go is the biggest call you make all spring;
- your campus: junior days, your clinic, open practices, the spring game and unofficial visits;
- your phone: a call from you counts with a high school coach by your name (question 7);
- your film.

**The 2027 rules (checked 2026-11-15, Cain asked).** Two changes are coming, and neither puts you on the spring road:

- **The new calendar is adopted.** The FBS Oversight Committee approved it on August 28, 2026, and FootballScoop reported on November 6 that the Division I Cabinet approved it, effective January 1, 2027. Spring practice and summer activities become two practice periods (21 practices, at most seven weeks together and five each), with at least nine summer weeks off, a 27-day preseason for 21 practices and a 10-day January portal window. The game has the portal window (`calendar/anchors.rs`, `january_window`), not the practice periods.
- **The recruiting model is unconfirmed.** The committee's July 2026 report proposed 300 off-campus recruiting-person days from January 1 to May 31, 50 fall evaluation days (from 33), and up to 17 off-campus recruiters (16 staff and the head coach), with the head coach's days not counting. It also keeps the head coach off campus in the spring contact period. It was on the Cabinet's October 6-7 agenda, and I couldn't find the result.

So the FBS head coach's spring stays the campus and the phone under either rule book. Sources: the committee's July report (ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com, `JULY2026D1FBSOC_Report.pdf`), FootballScoop (2026-07-23, 2026-08-28, 2026-11-06), Yahoo Sports and theScore on the 21 practices, CBS Sports on coaches weighing practices in place of spring games. The pages themselves wouldn't load here, so these are from search results.

## How long a week takes you

- Three to five scenes a week come to you (question 5): the ones your coordinator says matter, with his reason ("The end's dad asked for you"). Those are the scenes tied to your goals, a first meeting with someone who matters, a rival in the room, or a live moment (GR14). He plays the rest and you get their lines, and you can open any of them yourself.
- A played week takes about 10 minutes: the meeting at the map 2 or 3, each scene about a minute, and the night calls seconds each unless one stops you. A handed-off week takes about a minute: the Monday letter and any live moment.
- The offseason's 30 weeks then take about five hours played and half an hour handed off.
