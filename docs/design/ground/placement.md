# The favor economy (Ground, GR22: placing kids builds standing)

Every program has too much of one thing and too little of another, and the kids you can't take are how you trade between them. Help a kid find his home and two coaches pay you back for years, in what they tell you and how early, never in a signature.

## A week of it

**A playoff program, the last week of January** (the portal window has closed; February's signing day is ahead).

- **Monday 8 AM, the staff meeting.** The favor board opens beside the map:

  | Your haves | Their needs | Owed to you | You owe |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | The Texas safety you passed on in December, still unsigned | Samford: a linebacker | Georgia's corners coach: one | The Valdosta coach: one, for January's early tape |
  | Two walk-ons leaving after the window | Troy: a corner | The Columbus coach: a big one | |
  | Your backup corner, in the portal, not landed | Valdosta High: a home for his linebacker | | |

- **Monday 1 PM.** The Valdosta coach calls in his planning period: "My linebacker's got nothing but a walk-on spot at a Division II school. You know anybody?"
- **Monday 4 PM.** You call Samford's head coach: "Take a look at him. He can play for you. Watch his grades." His staff will look, and now you owe Samford one.
- **Monday 7 PM.** You call the Valdosta coach back with your words, worry included. At 8 PM he tells the boy's mother.
- **Wednesday 1 PM.** You spend Georgia's favor. At 10 PM its corners coach calls back about the corner Georgia lost in the window: "His knee isn't what the reports say." You pass. A rival takes him.
- **Thursday 5 AM.** Since last night: Samford's staff watched the linebacker's film. They want him on campus Saturday.
- **Saturday 8:40 PM.** Samford's coach texts: "We offered him tonight." A scholarship for a kid who had none is the biggest home there is. When he signs in February, your old debt to the Valdosta coach is paid. If he holds up at Samford's level by his second year, the Valdosta coach owes you one more, Samford owes you back for the look, and your word there grows.

**The same week at the other seats.** A high school coach calls the FCS staff that owes him about his one unsigned senior, and turns away a staff that pulled an offer from his school last year. An NFL head coach at the Senior Bowl in Mobile stands beside a college head coach whose receiver made his 53 after a year on the practice squad, and hears about the tight end's shoulder before the combine's medical checks.

## What each seat gets

Each seat has its own job in the market. Champions need homes for their no's and their leavers. The bottom tier has looks, places and early snaps. High school coaches hold the truth about their kids. NFL clubs hold tryouts, practice squad spots and snaps. Favors flow toward whoever gave the scarce thing, and information flows back.

**Championship program** (a Georgia, Ohio State or Alabama in a title year). Your no's become your best recruiting. Find homes for the kids you turn down and the 13 players you lose to the portal, and their high school coaches stay Trusted: they tell you the truth about their next five-star a year before anyone asks. Your recruiting coordinator places the routine ones by a rule you set ("every kid we pass on gets three calls"); you take the Trusted and Home schools yourself. Skip it and a school can shut you out: DeSoto (Texas) barred Washington State from its campus after it pulled Myron Turner's offer in 2013, and the ban was nearly four years old in 2017 ([Dallas News](https://dallasnews.com/high-school-sports/2017/01/19/what-happens-when-a-scholarship-offer-gets-pulled-how-it-might-affect-the-college-more-than-the-player)).

**Playoff program** (about 10-2). You buy the truth. One favor with Georgia's corners coach tells you which of its leavers is real, and that matters: 8 of the 13 who left Georgia's 2021 title team started elsewhere ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/sports/georgia-bulldogs/georgia-ohio-state-mine-transfer-portal-with-care/UF5PEKH7RNHX7LDQKR3L3YK4HA)). In the fall, favors with high school coaches buy the truth about their seniors after Friday's game: the work, the grades, the shoulder he plays through.

**Bottom-tier Division I** (a 2-10 Group of Five or FCS team with little money). You hold what the top lacks: looks, places and early snaps. Every look you give a champion's kid earns a favor from the richest staffs in the country, and those favors buy their eyes: tape and honest reads on about 10 kids a cycle in places you never visit. Your word grows by being right about kids others rated low, so in three or four years a Power Four staff takes your call like anyone's. Khalil Mack's only FBS offer was Buffalo, because an assistant who knew him from Liberty's recruitment, Robert Wimberly, moved there; he went fifth overall in 2014 ([SI](https://www.si.com/college/2013/11/19/khalil-mack-buffalo-bulls)).

**High school powerhouse.** Kids post their own offers now, so your currency is the truth and your word. Staffs that find homes for your lesser seniors owe you, and you choose whom to tell first about the kid nobody has seen yet. Oversell one kid and your five-star pays for it. Glenville's Ted Ginn Sr. sent at least 21 players to Ohio State from 2002 to 2017 ([Eleven Warriors](https://www.elevenwarriors.com/ohio-state-football/2022/06/131116/buckeye-pipelines-glenville-has-been-a-geyser-for-ohio-state-throughout-the-21st-century)), and after Cardale Jones signed he sent him to Fork Union for a semester to mature first ("It was all my idea", [SI](https://www.si.com/college/2015/01/08/cardale-jones-ohio-state-military-school)).

**Small high school.** You have a few seniors a year who can play at Division II, III or NAIA, and no eyes on you. One staff that trusts your word becomes a chain: your call to the FCS staff that believes you turns into looks from its Division II and NAIA friends (each step is that staff's own claim, and you owe it for the looks). You vouch for more than your roster: the kid at the 1A school down the road, the basketball player who never played football. Heritage Hall's Rod Warner wrote to 105 staffs, then called his friend Tommy McVay at Texas Tech for Wes Welker, who got the last place in the class when a signee backed out ([ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/college-football/story/_/id/29658528/texas-tech-director-football-operations-tommy-mcvay-dies-76)).

**NFL contender.** Tuesdays in season become a market: tryouts, the practice squad, waiver claims, with agents and other clubs who owe you. Your veteran cuts land with friends, and their agents owe you (at the August cutdown veterans are free agents at once; players with fewer than four seasons go on waivers, and after the trade deadline everyone does, [NFL Football Operations](https://operations.nfl.com/updates/the-players/nfl-waiver-system-overview)). College staffs you host at camp give your scouts the straight word on character and the trainer's word on injuries for late picks and undrafted signings. Few NFL coaches make those calls: Lane Kiffin said they don't call him, "with the exception of Belichick" ([PFT](https://profootballtalk.nbcsports.com/2021/04/08/lane-kiffin-bill-belichick-is-unique-among-nfl-coaches-in-researching-draft-prospects/)), and Nick Saban said: "There are 30 teams I never hear from" ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/college-football/news-all-call-when-nick-sabana-called-nfl-front-offices-major-flaw-bill-belichick-s-presence)).

**NFL rebuilding club.** Your currency is snaps. Promise real reps to three undrafted rookies, keep it, and next April two agents call you first with their best undrafted clients. In season the waiver wire and the practice squad market run every Tuesday. Money alone doesn't decide the undrafted rush: every club had the same $206,144 bonus pool in 2025 ([Arrowhead Pride](https://www.arrowheadpride.com/2025/4/26/24417837/chiefs-2025-undrafted-free-agent-tracker)), but guaranteed salary has no limit (the Packers gave Nazir Stackhouse $15,000 plus $150,000 guaranteed, [Acme Packing Company](https://www.acmepackingcompany.com/2025/7/17/24469193/green-bay-packers-news-nfl-draft-2025-undrafted-free-agents-pre-training-camp-nazir-stackhouse)), so clubs compete on guarantees and a path to the 53, and agents weigh the path.

**The goal, the risk, the reward.** The goal: Home with the people who control the talent you need. The risk: your word rides on a read you can't fully check, a favor spent now is gone when the five-star comes, and a kid you place may beat you. The reward: the call at his hour ("From now on you hear about my guys first") and your name first on the lead card.

## The year of it

The board is never empty, and its weeks follow the real calendar.
- **January:** the portal window (one window from 2025-26: January 2-16, then from 2026-27 ten days from the first business day of the year, [NBC Sports](https://www.nbcsports.com/college-football/news/ncaa-approves-a-single-january-transfer-window-in-college-football), [FootballScoop](https://www.footballscoop.com/2026/08/28/ncaa-approves-new-calendar-for-football-adjusts-january-portal-window); the game already does this, `calendar/anchors.rs`). Leavers need landings, and the truth about them is worth most.
- **February:** signing day's last places and the no's.
- **March and April:** spring practice cuts, late kids for the bottom tier, small schools' Division II, III and NAIA seniors, junior college kids finishing; NFL pro days.
- **May:** the undrafted rush and rookie tryouts.
- **June:** one-day camps, with juniors at your camp.
- **July:** clinics (Texas's THSCA coaching school draws over 16,000 coaches, administrators and exhibitors, [thsca.com](https://thsca.com/convention)) and college staffs visiting NFL camps.
- **August:** FBS rosters to 105 and the NFL cutdown to 53 (in 2026, 6 PM Eastern on Sunday, August 30, with claims at 1 PM Monday, [Chargers](https://www.chargers.com/news/2026-nfl-53-man-roster-cutdown-deadline)).
- **September to November:** Friday nights and the truth about seniors; the NFL's Tuesday personnel day every week (commonly said: Tuesday is the players' day off and the day clubs hold tryouts and make moves).
- **December:** the early signing period, coaching changes (a new job's open debts and grudges), and the season's claims settling.

## How it works

### Two books with each person
Beside the ladder (GR17), each person keeps two things about you, each with a why line (GR18).
- **Your word.** His record of your claims about kids ("4 of 5 held up"). It is never spent. It starts from your claims only, not your wins or your program, and grows with evidence: three held claims count for less than thirty (it levels off near six to eight settled claims). It decides how fast and how hard his staff looks at a kid you send, never what they conclude: his own staff's read makes every offer. Being right about a kid others rated low counts most; being right about the obvious kid counts little.
- **Favors.** What you owe each other, at most five either way (the built `balance`). They are personal: they belong to the man who made the call and go with him when he changes jobs (GR29). You see them in words, never as a price list: "He owes you one", "He owes you a big one", "You owe him one".

### What earns a favor (you gave something scarce)
- **A look.** When someone's call makes your staff look at a kid, he owes you one (two in a scarce week: signing day afternoon, the portal window's last days), whatever you decide.
- **A home found.** When a kid you helped place signs, his coach owes you by how hard that home was for that kid: two for a scholarship where he had none at that level or above, one for a step up from his best standing at its peak, nothing for a place any staff would give (open-roster junior colleges, most Division III and NAIA walk-on spots). His best standing counts any board where he sat above the line, yours included, so pulling an offer and then placing the kid earns nothing. A portal kid's gap is read when the window closes. One comes at signing and the rest at the settle; a quit or a cut in year one takes it back. Later homes from the same coach in the same year count one less.
- **What counts as a step up.** The sending coach's nature decides (GR23): fit, playing time and a degree for a loyal coach; the level that lifts his school for a self-serving one (GR21); and from February 2024, when NIL as a recruiting inducement stopped being barred, NIL money for a family.
- **A claim that holds.** Claims settle on the kid's merit at the level you claimed, from his level rating wherever he plays (`level_rating`): after year one for a transfer, after year two for a high school signee, after the season at the NFL. Everyone who heard the claim records it in your word, not just the staff that signed him. The staff that took him after your call owes you back one, or two if he starts.
- **First word.** At the high school seat, telling a staff first about a kid nobody has seen earns one.
- **Help.** Speaking at his own clinic, hosting his staff at your practice or camp, a tryout for his senior: one, once a year per person. Big conventions (THSCA) give warmth across an area, not favors.

### What a favor buys (you ask, in a call, at his hour)
- **One answer one level up.** A favor lifts one answer one level above your standing (GR19), behind those who already hold that level: at Stranger his real list, at Known the truth about one kid, at Trusted first call on one kid, behind his Home staffs. It never skips GR28's minimum times (no Trusted inside a year, no Home before a kid of his has done well with you).
- **A look at your kid.** One, or two in a scarce week.
- **Access.** His juniors at your camp. A scrimmage at the high school seat, a joint practice at the NFL (commonly said: head coaches set these up with friends). A word in your assistant's job search. A job on his staff for your man, only if your man clears his hiring bar (built `candidate_score`). Bigger asks need more owed.
- **How he answers.** He answers at his hour, in his voice, by his nature. A loyal coach pays what he owes. A self-serving one may dodge when a bigger program wants the same thing, and the dodge costs him standing with you. Asking for more than he owes costs warmth.
- **Never.** No favor buys a commitment, a signature, a transfer, a contract, or a coach's word to a family on your behalf (Ground's rule 3), and brokers can't be paid in favors for any of those either. A coach's honest opinion of you stays what it is in the base world; favors add nothing to a kid's choice. What you learn early lets you act early, and acting early is fair recruiting.

### What you owe
What you owe never fades. Within about a year he calls it in at his hour with his own ask ("Take my linebacker", a clinic talk, a graduate assistant spot for his nephew, with a warning when an ask breaks the era's rules). A no or a dodge counts as a broken promise (GR24). What he owes you fades slowly if you never ask.

### What it costs
Your word, your places (a walk-on spot, a scholarship, a practice squad spot), the favors you owe, and what a rival gains, since a kid you place may beat you. Each ask is a call, and calls use your week's people time the way they do today (`reach_out`, `talks::spend`). The day's sections themselves are never spent (`docs/design/day-sections.md`).

### Claims and worries
The built claim words ("worth a look", "can play for you", "a sure thing") work at every seat; a stronger claim gains more when it holds and costs more when it fails. A claim can carry a worry ("Watch his grades"), which the kid's own coach passes to the family that evening. A hidden worry that comes true costs your word (built at the high school seat, `folk/dialogue.rs`).

### The no you don't place
A pulled offer or a late no to a Trusted or Home school's kid costs standing sharply (GR24), unless you find him a home by signing day, which cuts the cost to a quarter. It costs in full if he turned down other offers for yours, or if it's your second pull at that school within three years. An early, honest no is free.

### Your leavers
When one of your players enters the portal, his high school coach watches where he lands (GR23). Help him land well, with a call to a friend whose depth chart has a real hole, and that school's standing holds; let him drift and it slips. Division I staffs can't contact another school's player until he is in the portal, so your call comes after he enters (tampering cases still happen: North Dakota, June 2026, [NCAA](https://www.ncaa.org/news/2026/6/5/media-center-tampering-violations-occurred-in-north-dakota-football-program.aspx)).

### People who last
- **Placed kids** become people in your book. Each tells you about his new team (its needs, its coach), and later, as a graduate assistant, a high school coach or a pro, starts at Known with you.
- **Brokers** (private trainers, 7-on-7 directors, NIL handlers, agents) place kids too, as a business, and win the thanks you pass up (commonly said).
- **Your book moves with you** (GR29). A high school coach who placed 30 kids reaches college with 30 staffs' records of his word. The program's grudges stay with the program: a new job hands you its open promises and grudges in the takeover calls.

### At the NFL
- The Tuesday personnel day in season is the weekly market: tryouts, practice squad moves, waiver claims, with agents and other clubs as people with standing.
- Veteran cuts placed with friends' clubs earn favors from the friend's club only when it was a real hole, and from the player's agent for the home found.
- The live moment at cutdown: Monday morning, before the 1 PM claims, a club ahead of your friend in the order calls about your cut. The truth costs your friend the player but keeps your word with both clubs, and a lie settles later like any claim.
- College staffs at your summer camp and tryouts for their seniors earn favors and your word with them; you spend them on the straight word before the draft.
- Your GM must say yes to each promise of reps, tryout and placed cut when he holds the final say. The final say is built (`pro/office.rs`, `Power`: the GM, the coach or the owner), but today it only weighs voices; the yes is new.

### Guards (from real life and fairness; not asked)
- No hole, no favor: a taker takes your kids only for real holes on his own board and his own depth chart. New: today a placement's bond makes the friend's program want the kid whatever its depth chart says, and `class_plan` only sizes signing classes.
- Stashing a kid at a junior college or prep school buys first call when he's ready, never the kid.
- One favor chain per kid per recruiting year, however many times he moves.
- Placements inside your own coaching tree count half.
- A warning before any ask the era forbids: jobs or benefits for a prospect's relatives (Bylaw 13.2.1.1, [inducement rules](https://arkansasrazorbacks.com/pdf/compliance/booster-regulations.pdf)), and from April 2017 FBS noncoaching hires tied to a prospect ([FootballScoop](https://footballscoop.com/news/ncaa-approves-iawp-rule-comprehensive-recruiting-changes)). A breach becomes a case.
- The AI's books follow the same rules, upkeep and fading as yours.

### A bad placement
At 10 PM in October, Troy's coach calls: the "can play for you" safety you sent him last year quit, and you never mentioned his temper. Your word with Troy drops (4 of 5 becomes 4 of 6), the rest of his high school coach's favor never comes, Troy's assistants carry the story to their friends, and his high school coach calls too: you picked the place.

### The AI
Every staff and broker keeps the same books by the same rules, seeded from the world's 30 years, and places by its nature: a straight coach vouches honestly, a self-serving one oversells. Built in part: each coach's hidden straightness and each source's agenda already decide how often a tip oversells (`folk/tips.rs`); natures as GR23 describes them are new. You see the AI's economy through signs, not numbers (GR2): the lead card's order and its why, a Trusted coach's word ("Alabama got my safety into Troy"), pipeline lines on the map (GR6) and warnings (GR25).

### The eras
- Before October 15, 2018, a player needed his school's permission to talk to other schools, so a release to a friend's program was itself a favor ([The Advocate](https://www.theadvocate.com/baton_rouge/sports/lsu/article_eb2a90da-aaed-11e9-8629-6370e59e6273.html)).
- While the 25-signee cap lasted (waived 2022, ended October 2023), grayshirts and blueshirts were places you could promise. LSU signed 27 in 2010 and asked Elliott Porter to grayshirt after he had moved into his dorm; he went to Kentucky ([Rocky Top Talk](https://www.rockytoptalk.com/2010/8/3/1603610/thanks-to-arithmetic-elliott)).
- From 2021 a first transfer plays at once (a second needed a waiver until a December 2023 court order and the April 2024 rule change, [NCAA](https://www.ncaa.org/news/2024/4/22/media-center-division-i-board-of-directors-ratifies-transfer-nil-rule-changes.aspx)). NIL opened July 1, 2021, but NIL as a recruiting inducement stayed barred until a February 23, 2024 injunction ([Duane Morris](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/sportslaw/2024/02/28/update-judge-sides-with-state-ags-on-nil-recruiting-ban/)).
- From 2025 the 105 roster limit makes FBS walk-on places scarce ([AP via theScore](https://thescore.com/ncaaf/news/3589388/walk-ons-are-on-the-decline-the-ones-remaining-lament-lost-opportunities-for-grinders-like-them)).
- Junior colleges: about 800 junior college players went to FBS in 2019, about 150 of them to Power Five schools ([SI](https://www.si.com/college/2019/02/21/juco-junior-college-programs-transfers-closings-prospects)); Power Five junior college signees fell from 112 (2019) to 45 (2021) as staffs looked to the portal first ([Talon Marks](https://www.talonmarks.com/sports/2024/01/24/nil-and-transfer-portal-challenges-junior-college-athletes-futures/)).
- NFL Europe (1991-2007): allocations earned extra camp spots; Jacksonville sent five players in 2007 and got five exemptions plus two bonus ones ([Jaguars](https://www.jaguars.com/news/jags-give-five-to-europa-5837522)).

## Why it's balanced

- **Champion.** It gains protection and the truth, not kids. An obvious castoff placed a step down earns nothing, open-roster homes earn nothing, and every pulled offer has to be cleaned up. Its helmet gets its calls answered first (GR20), never believed.
- **Playoff.** Every trade needs a real hole, and its favors buy the truth about leavers and seniors, not the players.
- **Bottom tier.** It gives the most looks, so it earns the most favors, and its word grows by being right about kids others rated low.
- **High school.** Overselling spends the word a five-star needs, and claims settle with every staff that heard them. A small school's few homes weigh more, and vouching for neighbors keeps its loop alive.
- **NFL.** Tryouts, practice squad spots and snaps are finite, the GM bounds the coach, and a staff's word only sharpens a read.
- **No snowball.** Favors buy at most one level up, behind those who hold it; what you owe never fades; places are capped by real roster limits; nothing touches a kid's choice.

**The proof.** The layer ships behind a switch, off until a 30-year trial on three new seeds, against the same seeds without it, shows: each tier gains on its own measure (champions keep feeder standing, playoff teams sign more leavers who start, the bottom tier signs more kids who outplay their ratings); the top 10 programs' share of Home ties and of top-300 signees doesn't rise; real flows hold by era (the junior college and portal numbers above; the FCS share of draft picks, about 7% in the 2010s and 1.5% to 4.3% in 2023-2026 counted by the school drafted from, [NCAA.com](https://www.ncaa.com/news/football/article/2026-04-25/fcs-players-taken-2026-nfl-draft)); and the same offers on the same dates give the same choices, so favors never move a kid.

## Questions for Cain (only what real life can't settle)

1. **How far a favor reaches.** One answer one level up, behind those who already hold that level, never faster than GR28's minimum times. My pick: yes. Reason: any more and favors replace the grind; any less and they're worth nothing.
2. **The ledger in words.** Favors show as "he owes you one" or "a big one" and your word as "4 of 5 held up", never as numbers or a price list. My pick: yes. Reason: it reads like a coach's memory, and a price list turns it into a shop.
3. **The sizes** (numbers nobody publishes). A look costs one (two in a scarce week); a home found pays two, one or nothing by how hard it was; a held claim pays back one (two if he starts); your word levels off near six to eight settled claims; claims settle after year one for transfers and year two for signees; a placed no costs a quarter; debts are called in within about a year. My pick: yes, tuned in the trial.
4. **More people on the ladder.** GR17 adds the people you trade with: other staffs at every seat, brokers, and agents at the NFL. My pick: yes. Reason: the economy runs through them, and without a level and a why they'd be a second system.
5. **The champion's coordinator.** At volume your recruiting coordinator places the routine no's by a rule you set, and you take the Trusted and Home schools yourself (you can always take any call). My pick: yes. Reason: real champions turn down hundreds, and one call each would bury you.
6. **Ship behind a switch.** The layer ships off until the 30-year trial passes the marks above. My pick: yes. Reason: you called it delicate, and this is the one place a small-builds-ship-directly rule should wait.

Everything else above follows real life or fairness, so I took it as the design and it isn't asked.

## How this was made

Real-life research (sources above) and a map of today's code, then three independent designs (a favor ledger with prices, "the vouch" where your word is the currency, and supply and need by tier), a judge (supply and need as the base, with the vouch's settlement and the ledger's spending side grafted on), one combined design, and two rounds of stress tests and fact and code checks. The fixes they forced: a vouch buys a look, never the decision; homes are priced by how hard they were, so open-roster homes earn nothing; claims settle with everyone who heard them, on merit at the level claimed; a favor never ranks you ahead of the people who hold the next level; what you owe never fades; and weekly loops for the NFL (the Tuesday market) and the playoff team (Friday nights).
