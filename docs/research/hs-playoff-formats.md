## What the states have in common

- **Who gets in.** Most states qualify teams by district or region finish (often the top 4) plus a computer rating. The ratings are RPI-style formulas (own record, opponents' record, opponents' opponents': GA, NC, UT, IA), points per win weighted by opponent size or wins (OH, MI, NJ, WV, NE, SD, ME), or MaxPreps rankings (FL, AZ). **[published, per row]**
- **How many get in.** It runs from everyone (Indiana's open tournament, Minnesota sections, Utah's small classes, Missouri district rounds) to small fields (Connecticut, Delaware, Vermont, Maine: 8 per class). The big states take roughly half the teams or more (Texas: top 4 of each district; Illinois 384 teams; Wisconsin 256). **[published]** Claude's reading: "about half, by district finish plus a rating" is the most common shape. **[estimated]**
- **Private schools.** Many states now separate or handicap private and choice schools: Louisiana select and non-select, Tennessee Division II, Alabama private classes, Georgia's private format, Illinois' 1.65 enrollment multiplier, Connecticut's play-up rule, New Jersey's non-public groups, Mississippi and South Carolina private schools in separate associations (MAIS, SCISA). **[published]**
- **Open divisions.** California, Florida (2026), Arizona, Hawaii, Nevada and Oregon (a 6A second tier) play an Open or top bracket so the strongest teams meet. **[published]**
- **Seasons end at different times.** Alaska starts its playoffs about Oct 10; Wyoming and the Dakotas finish in mid-November; Louisiana, Arkansas and Pennsylvania play into December. **[published]**
- **Changes keep coming.** Illinois (2026), Georgia (2026-27), Florida (2026), Nebraska (2026), Nevada (2026), Kentucky (2027) and Ohio (2025) all changed their formats recently. **[published]**

## Practice limits

- **Texas (UIL)**, **[published]** https://www.uiltexas.org/football/page/fall-football-practice-regulations:
  - Five acclimatization days: helmets only on days 1-2, shoulder pads from day 3, contact only from day 5, full contact after.
  - Two-a-days only before school starts, never on back-to-back days, at most 5 hours in total, and full contact in only one of the two.
  - At most 3 hours in one practice.
  - **90 minutes of full contact a week**, all season and in the playoffs.
  - **8 hours a week outside the school day** (a state law). The school's athletic period does not count toward it.
- **Across the country** (AP survey of all 50 states and DC, August 2019), **[published]** https://vnews.com/2019/08/30/ap-survey-most-states-limit-full-contact-for-hs-football-28125729/:
  - **43 states limit full-contact time in the season.** 8 leave it to coaches (Louisiana among them).
  - Nearly 40 limit preseason contact. More than 10 don't (South Carolina among them).
  - Michigan allows 30 minutes a week in season. New Jersey allows 15 minutes a week, and 6 hours in total in preseason, scrimmages included.
- **Other states** (NFHS, 2015-09-15), **[published]** https://nfhs.org/stories/states-adopt-plans-to-limit-contact-in-football:
  - 90 minutes a week: Iowa, Kansas, Georgia and Tennessee. Kansas also bans live action the day after a game.
  - 60 minutes a week: Ohio.
  - The NFHS task force recommends 2-3 contact days a week, 60-90 minutes in total.
- **California** (AB 2127, 2014), **[published]** https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/13-14/bill/asm/ab_2101-2150/ab_2127_bill_20140702_enrolled.htm: at most two full-contact practices a week, each up to 90 minutes, and no full contact in the offseason.
- **Gap:** no single up-to-date table of every state's current limits was found. The AP survey is from 2019.

**Means:** a high school team's week is short and capped. Texas allows about 8 hours outside school plus the daily athletic period. Full contact is limited almost everywhere, to between 15 and 90 minutes a week. So high school teams get far less hitting and install time than college or NFL teams. That should slow down how much of a playbook a high school team can learn, and limit how much physical toughness practice can build. **[estimated]**

## Proposals for Raichel (Claude's ideas, proposed, not confirmed)

1. **Use each state's real format** from this table, as data per state and class, not one national rule. That answers open question Q2. Formats change over time in real life, and the calendar spec allows only an agreed list of off-field changes, so whether high school formats can change during a career needs Raichel's call.
2. **Private-school rules per state** (separate brackets, multipliers, play-up rules), because they shape where powerhouse private schools win titles and how recruiting hotbeds look.
3. **Practice limits per state** (contact minutes, hours a week) feed section 6b. They cap how fast high school players learn a playbook and how much hitting they get, so high school development depends partly on the state.
4. **Fill the gaps** above from each association's handbook when the high school world is built.
