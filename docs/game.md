# The game (section 8, first playable): how it works

Built 2026-10-20/21 overnight (thread "Build the program", PR #75), from `docs/design/first-playable.md`, `docs/design/training.md` (6b) and `docs/design/screens.md` (section 10, on Showtime).

## How to play

- **On a Mac:** download `AFM_universal.dmg` from the public downloads page, https://github.com/cnbville/afm-worlds/releases/tag/app-latest (no GitHub sign-in needed), open it and drag AFM to Applications. The app isn't signed with an Apple developer ID, so the first time macOS asks: right-click AFM, choose Open, then Open again (or System Settings, Privacy and Security, Open Anyway). It is one universal app for Apple silicon (the M1) and Intel Macs. Every build on `main` is also on the AFM repository's releases page (sign-in needed, the repository is private).
- **Updates (Cain 2026-10-21):** the app updates itself. At start and every three hours it asks the downloads page (`latest.json` in the `app-latest` release) for a newer build, downloads and installs it in the background, and restarts by itself when no career is open; with a career open it asks to save first and offers Restart now. Each build is version 0.2.<run number>. Updates are signed: the "Mac app" workflow signs them with the `TAURI_SIGNING_PRIVATE_KEY` secret and puts the matching public key (the `TAURI_UPDATER_PUBKEY` variable) into the build, and the app checks every update against it. AFM must run from Applications (not from the open dmg) to update itself.
- **Setting it up once:** the AFM repository needs one secret, `WORLDS_TOKEN`: a fine-grained token that may write contents to `cnbville/afm-worlds` and secrets and variables to `cnbville/AFM`. The "Set up updates" workflow (run once by hand) then makes the signing key inside GitHub, stores it as the `TAURI_SIGNING_PRIVATE_KEY` secret and the public key as the `TAURI_UPDATER_PUBKEY` variable; the key never leaves GitHub. Without them builds still work but make no update.
- **In a browser** (any machine with Rust): `cargo run --release --bin afm_play`, then open http://127.0.0.1:7700. Arguments: port, the screens folder (`app/ui`), the game folder.
- **The game folder** holds `worlds/` (the library), `saves/` and `bugs/`. On a Mac it is `~/Library/Application Support/AFM`, elsewhere `~/.afm`; `AFM_HOME` overrides it.
- **Getting a world (Cain 2026-10-21: both ways):** the new career's World step lists the worlds on the worlds page, the public repository `cnbville/afm-worlds` (world files only, no game code); one click downloads a world into the library, joining its parts. The "Build a world" workflow (Actions tab, Run workflow, a seed and years) builds a world on GitHub's machines in about three hours and publishes it there; it needs the `WORLDS_TOKEN` secret, a token that may write to `cnbville/afm-worlds`. "Build a new world" builds one beside the game with its progress shown (`jobs.rs`; `afm_world <seed> <years> <folder>` does the same from the command line); a 30-year world takes hours and peaks at about 6.5 GB, tight on an 8 GB Mac.
- **Importing a world:** on the World step, "Import a world folder…" (or, in a browser, "Import from a path…"). An `.afmworld` file is copied into the library and opened; from then on it is listed under "The world library", and every career started from it begins on the same February morning with fresh randomness. Dropping the file into `worlds/` by hand works too. A world split into parts (`name.afmworld.001`, `.002`, ...) is imported by choosing the `.001` part, or the folder that holds the parts ("Choose a world folder…"): the game joins the parts back into one file.

## The parts

- `crates/afm-engine/src/save.rs`: the world file (format 1, agreed with the world-building thread): `AFMWORLD`, the version, a JSON header (seed, history years, start year, commit, written, world date), then the whole world as named MessagePack through zstd. New fields load as their default, so an older world file opens in a newer game; `World::after_load` fills in what a newer game adds.
- `crates/afm-engine/src/world/seat.rs`: your seat in the world. `start_career` (fresh randomness mixed from the career seed, you take the head coach's job and he goes to the market), your games kept whole (`SeatGame`, the last 40), your depth order (`seat_depth`, played by the engine before the staff's order), your training focus (`seat_focus`), your notes, your high school staff (`HsAssistant`), and plain reads for the screens (fixtures, records).
- `crates/afm-game`: the game. One `Game::call(cmd, args)` the screens use: `library`, `import_world`, `open_world`, `load`, `save`, `build_world`, `setup`, `jobs`, `start_career`, `status`, `continue`, `day`, `holiday`, `home`, `inbox`, `news`, `calendar`, `team`, `roster`, `depth`, `set_depth`, `training`, `set_focus`, `recruiting`, `contracts`, `schedule`, `standings`, `staff`, `coach`, `player`, `games`, `game`, `search`, `you`, `note_add`, `note_delete`, `bug`, and for the pages before the game `towns`, `similar`, `card`, `rename_save`, `delete_save`, `reveal`. `reads.rs` turns true ratings into your staff's reads, `views.rs` builds every screen's data, `setup.rs` the setup and the library, `files.rs` the files, `cards.rs` the world and save cards.
- `app/ui`: the screens, one page in plain HTML and JS. The pages before the game (opening, Load game, new career) are `pregame.js` and `pregame.css`, in their own look; everything from taking the job on is Showtime (`showtime.css`, Archivo fonts). A frosted left sidebar, the top bar with back and forward, search (⌘K), the date, what's next and Continue (space bar).
- `app/src-tauri`: the desktop app on Tauri 2. One command, `call`, runs the game on a worker thread so the window never freezes. Not in the Cargo workspace; built by `.github/workflows/mac-app.yml`.

## What a career has now

- **The pages before the game (Cain 2026-10-21, from the mock-ups at https://claude.ai/artifact/FrYwnUMKnBs1L31EEYPude):** dark graphite with one amber accent, the stadium photo (`app/ui/art/stadium.jpg`) behind the opening and every page title, full width, a fixed footer so Next is always in the window. They leave Showtime; Showtime starts once you take the job.
  - **Opening:** the stadium, Continue (your last save's card: team, line, record, what's next, the date, when it was saved), New career, Load game, Settings (version, game folder, check for updates), Quit in the Mac app. "Around football" shows the latest big headlines of that world.
  - **Load game:** every save with its team, date in the game, season, world and when it was saved; the save's file beside it; Load, Rename, Delete (asks first; only files in the saves folder) and "Open a save file…".
  - **World and save cards (`cards.rs`):** each world or save file carries a card in its header (the world's story: the dynasty, the rival, everyone else, the NFL, high school; national titles and Super Bowls by team; your team on a save), so the pages read them without loading the world. A library world written before cards gets one beside it (`name.afmworld.card.json`) the first time it opens.
  - **New career in six steps:** World (the library, worlds to download, import, build; its story; names are fixed when a world is built), You (name, date of birth, heritage, hometown with its schools, high school, college and years, degree, family and children with their school year, looks, an optional photo and a "Copy AI prompt" for it), Background (11 roads, placed from your profile), Ratings (19, below), Style (five choices of five, each with real coaches at its ends, and who calls the plays), Job (every head coaching job at any NFL club, college program or high school, with filters, search, sortable columns, and a job file: record, prestige, stadium, NIL budget, titles, the coach you replace, your boss, the expectation, the calendar, program grades).
  - **Your 19 ratings** on the 1-20 scale every coach uses, from your road's pool in whole points: teaching (8 groups), game day (scheme, game management, play calling, in-game adaptation), people (motivation, discipline, relationships), building the roster (recruiting, evaluation, network, negotiating). The page shows how the world reads you and three coaches in the world on your road.
  - **Who calls the plays** (plays and tactics topic 6.1: the coordinator, or the head coach himself, like Sean McVay): your coordinators, the offense, the defense or both. It starts on the side you came up teaching (a quarterbacks man calls the offense, a secondary man the defense, a special teams man hands both off). At college and NFL jobs the side you don't call stays with its coordinator; at a high school you call both sides (the school's assistants don't call plays yet).
  - **The four new ratings** (only yours; AI coaches keep their blends): play calling and in-game adaptation replace the AI's blend (0.6 scheme, 0.4 game management) on the side you call, shifting the caller's reading, situations and creativity; evaluation is your staff's eye for your own reads; negotiating replaces your general manager's skill in reading an agent's bluff at your NFL club. They grow each year like your other ratings (by doing the job) and fade late in a career.
- **Continue** runs day by day and stops on a decision, a huge moment anywhere (a regular-season game is huge only at its own level: a college rivalry game doesn't stop an NFL or high school career), a big one at your level or for your team, and **your own game day**, which opens that game's page. It also stops **the morning of** your game day and of your level's decision days (NFL: the tag deadline, the new league year, the draft, cutdown day, the trade deadline; college: the early signing day, signing day, the portal opening), before the day is played; the note says which morning. The top bar names your next game.
- **The main menu** (sidebar) goes back to the start screen. Closing a career that is open asks first and says how long since you saved; Back never leaves the game.
- **Questions in the game's own dialogs:** the Mac app's window (WKWebView through Tauri) has no `prompt` or `confirm`, so saving, a bug report, closing a career and building a world ask in the page itself. Saving and a bug report show their progress, and errors come through as plain words.
- **Records:** your team's record, your own record (career and at this job) and every staff record come from the season's results. Only a head coach's job has a record: an assistant's job, and a job that ended before the world's kept records begin, show a dash. A coach who takes a college job in February doesn't inherit last season's bowl game. From the new football year's first day (the day after the Super Bowl) to the next kickoff, home and the team page show last season's record as "9-8 in 2025".
- **Squads** leave out a player who walked away from football or whose playing days ended, though he stays on the school's list until the roster is rebuilt. A pro season with no club reads "Unsigned" on the career tab.
- **Inbox:** what is about you (your program, club or school, and you), your decisions, huge moments, the big ones you care about, the final of each of your games and your college staff's own messages, newest first. News has the same "yours" rule (an NFL club's own stories included; the NFL's stories are labelled NFL, not College).
- **Depth chart:** the order the engine plays next game (your order first, then the staff's), with the injured and the suspended listed after the starters, not as starters; an NFL club's list leaves out its practice squad.
- **NFL screens:** the schedule shows each final score and the kickoff time ("1:00 ET"); the team page keeps a book of seasons (record, head coach, how far it went) from this world's next season on, and its Super Bowls. Contracts count the cap as the league does: before the season only the top 51 contracts (the rule of 51) plus dead money, with last year's carryover added to the room; players in their last year carry a "Free agent soon" tag.
- **College jobs:** the job list holds every program that plays this year and can dress a team (22 players or more), and searching by state name works ("Texas").
- **Saves** are named after the career ("Name, Team"), with the world date and when you saved. **Build a world on this Mac** asks first, with the time and memory it takes, and has a Stop button.
- **Screens:** home, inbox, news (everything or yours), calendar (your games, your level's fixed dates and the big moments ahead), squad (unit, class and injured filters, sortable, grouped by position), depth chart, training, recruiting (a college's offers, visits, commitments and signings; a high school's players with college interest), contracts (an NFL club's books against the cap), schedule, game pages (box score and play by play with each call and its reason), standings, staff, coach pages, team pages, player pages (overview, attributes, career, tendencies, recruiting, your notes), search, your profile, save, a bug pack.
- **What you see is your staff's read:** your own players' ratings as ranges whose width comes from the coach who reads them; other teams' players show only what is public. Potential appears only as words. Hidden coach ability is never shown, except your own ratings.
- **High school seat:** your school's games always run on the engine (even in a stand-in season) with full game pages; the schedule, your classification's standings, and a staff of teacher-coaches sized by enrollment who teach your players (below).
- **Training (6b topic 5, part):** a main focus per player. The focus grows up to half as fast again (1.5 times its share at full coachability) and his other skills of the same kind pay for it. Measured (`a_training_focus_grows_its_skill_and_costs_the_rest_a_little`, 400 players): the focus grows 1.29 times as fast and the year as a whole moves 1.2%, inside choice 2's 15%.

## Final build lane 1 (2026-10-22)

- **Ages come from birthdays.** Every age on a screen (squads, player pages, coaches, staff, your profile, the job lists) is counted from the person's date of birth on today's date, so a player turns 18 on his birthday, not at the end of a season. The player page shows the date ("Born 14 March 2009 (17)"). Some seniors are 18 all year and a few turn 19: about 1 boy in 10 was held back a grade (`docs/world-model.md`, "Final build lane 1").
- **Every name in a line is a link.** Inbox, news, Home and calendar lines link each player, coach, college, high school and NFL club they name, and your game results open their game page. A game you didn't keep opens the home team's schedule.
- **A player's public face:** the player page shows his followers, the ads and deals reported for him a year, and your staff's word for how he comes across on camera. His Aura itself is never shown.

## Memory (step 1)

Ratings and tendencies are stored as 4-byte floats instead of 8, and the people list keeps a third spare instead of doubling. On a 4-year quick world: a person went from 2,408 to 1,752 bytes; heap 5.60 to 4.59 GB, resident 4.09 to 3.53 GB, peak 6.08 to 4.99 GB. 
The 30-year world for first saves (seed 2026, `afm/worlds/` in the project folder, 1.98 GB in five parts), measured in `afm_play` on a 4-core Linux machine on 2026-10-21:

| Step | Time | Resident memory |
|---|---|---|
| Import (join the five parts into the library) | 76 s | |
| Open | 51 s (73 s on the old code) | 3.32 GB (3.84 GB on the old code) |
| Start a career at Iowa | 0.2 s | 3.32 GB |
| Continue from 9 February to the first game (stops on 24 April and 2, 4 and 6 September) | 148 s | 5.10 GB, peak 5.17 GB |
| Save | 74 s | 5.10 GB |

## Proposed, not confirmed

- Staff read widths: the range around the true rating narrows as the reading coach's eye rises (`reads.rs`).
- The 11 backgrounds' reputations, pools and spreads (`setup.rs`), with the four new ratings' leans (a college assistant's pool is 220, as in the mock-up).
- How the four new ratings work in play (above) and how fast they grow and fade; who calls the plays starting on the side you came up teaching.
- The job file's expectation lines (`college_expectation`, `nfl_expectation`, `hs_expectation`), and high school classes named "6A" to "1A" (Ohio, Michigan, Wisconsin and California: "Division 1" and on).
- The style choices' five steps sit at 2, 6, 10, 14 and 18 on the 1-20 scale.
- The opening's headlines are the latest big moments of the world's calendar (last season's, early in a football year).
- Which decision days get a morning stop (the NFL's five and college's three above, `World::morning_stop`), and a sack with no rusher on the quarterback credited to the man who tackled him.
- High school staffs: two assistants plus one per 400 students (3 to 15), ability around what the school's resources buy, a stipend of about $2,500-8,000, 85% teach (weighted toward PE and history); a group is taught by its own coach, or by the coordinator of its side at 1.5 points less.
- The training focus's cost: each other skill of the same kind gives up 3.5% at full coachability; how much of a plan sticks rises with coachability (60% to 100%).
- Time of possession on game pages is taken from the snap clock (the engine's own total counts only live-ball seconds); a kickoff's seconds go to the receiving team, so the two add to the game's length.
- A bug pack saves the world as it is when you report, not from the morning of that day.

## Doubts

- Memory in season is 5.1 GB, over the agreed 4 GB budget (opening is under it at 3.3 GB). The season's stores (game logs, the year's records) grow from the first day; compacting them is the next memory step. An 8 GB M1 runs it, with little room for other apps.
- Continue stops the morning of your game, then plays the whole day in one step and opens the game page; there is no stop at kickoff itself.

- Storing ratings as 4-byte floats changes results per seed slightly (rounding), so a seed no longer rebuilds a pre-build world exactly. Accepted because the section 8 build changes the world anyway.
- Season records are still 8-byte; compacting old seasons (topic 8) isn't done.
- Your school's games run on the engine in a stand-in season while its opponents' other games don't; their records are mixed (the engine's scoring is close to the stand-in's odds).
- Engine stats: a throwaway is no longer a target for any receiver, and a sack with no rusher on the quarterback (he went down to a tackle) goes to the tackler, so the box score's sacks match the play by play.
