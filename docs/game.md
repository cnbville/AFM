# The game (section 8, first playable): how it works

Built 2026-10-20/21 overnight (thread "Build the program", PR #75), from `docs/design/first-playable.md`, `docs/design/training.md` (6b) and `docs/design/screens.md` (section 10, on Showtime).

## How to play

- **On a Mac:** download `AFM_universal.dmg` from the public downloads page, https://github.com/cnbville/afm-worlds/releases/tag/app-latest (no GitHub sign-in needed), open it and drag AFM to Applications. The app isn't signed with an Apple developer ID, so the first time macOS asks: right-click AFM, choose Open, then Open again (or System Settings, Privacy and Security, Open Anyway). It is one universal app for Apple silicon (the M1) and Intel Macs. Every build on `main` is also on the AFM repository's releases page (sign-in needed, the repository is private).
- **Updates (Cain 2026-10-21):** the app updates itself. At start and every three hours it asks the downloads page (`latest.json` in the `app-latest` release) for a newer build, downloads and installs it in the background, and restarts by itself when no career is open; with a career open it asks to save first and offers Restart now. Each build is version 0.2.<run number>. Updates are signed: the "Mac app" workflow signs them with the `TAURI_SIGNING_PRIVATE_KEY` secret and puts the matching public key (the `TAURI_UPDATER_PUBKEY` variable) into the build, and the app checks every update against it. AFM must run from Applications (not from the open dmg) to update itself.
- **Setting it up once:** the AFM repository needs one secret, `WORLDS_TOKEN`: a fine-grained token that may write contents to `cnbville/afm-worlds` and secrets and variables to `cnbville/AFM`. The "Set up updates" workflow (run once by hand) then makes the signing key inside GitHub, stores it as the `TAURI_SIGNING_PRIVATE_KEY` secret and the public key as the `TAURI_UPDATER_PUBKEY` variable; the key never leaves GitHub. Without them builds still work but make no update.
- **In a browser** (any machine with Rust): `cargo run --release --bin afm_play`, then open http://127.0.0.1:7700. Arguments: port, the screens folder (`app/ui`), the game folder.
- **The game folder** holds `worlds/` (the library), `saves/` and `bugs/`. On a Mac it is `~/Library/Application Support/AFM`, elsewhere `~/.afm`; `AFM_HOME` overrides it.
- **Getting a world (Cain 2026-10-21: both ways):** the new career's World step lists the worlds on the worlds page, the public repository `cnbville/afm-worlds` (world files only, no game code); one click downloads a world into the library, joining its parts. The "Build a world" workflow (Actions tab, Run workflow, a seed and years) builds a world on GitHub's machines in about three hours and publishes it there; it needs the `WORLDS_TOKEN` secret, a token that may write to `cnbville/afm-worlds`. "Build a new world" builds one beside the game with its progress shown (`jobs.rs`; `afm_world <seed> <years> <folder>` does the same from the command line); a 30-year world takes hours and peaks at about 6.5 GB, tight on an 8 GB Mac.
- **Importing a world:** on the World step, "Import a world folder…" (or, in a browser, "Import from a path…"). An `.afmworld` file is copied into the library and opened; from then on it is listed under "The world library", and every career started from it begins on the same February morning with fresh randomness. Dropping the file into `worlds/` by hand works too. A world split into parts (`name.afmworld.001`, `.002`, ...) is imported by choosing the `.001` part, or the folder that holds the parts ("Choose a world folder…"): the game joins the parts back into one file.

## The parts

- `crates/afm-engine/src/save.rs`: the world file (format 1, agreed with the world-building thread): `AFMWORLD`, the version, a JSON header (seed, history years, start year, commit, written, world date), then the whole world as named MessagePack through zstd. New fields load as their default, so an older world file opens in a newer game; `World::after_load` fills in what a newer game adds.
- `crates/afm-engine/src/world/seat.rs`: your seat in the world. `start_career` (fresh randomness mixed from the career seed, you take the head coach's job and he goes to the market), your games kept whole (`SeatGame`, the last 40), your depth order (`seat_depth`, played by the engine before the staff's order), your training focus (`seat_focus`), your notes, your high school staff (`HsAssistant`), and plain reads for the screens (fixtures, records).
- `crates/afm-game`: the game. One `Game::call(cmd, args)` the screens use: `library`, `import_world`, `open_world`, `load`, `save`, `build_world`, `setup`, `jobs`, `start_career`, `status`, `continue`, `day`, `holiday`, `home`, `inbox`, `news`, `calendar`, `team`, `roster`, `depth`, `set_depth`, `training`, `set_focus`, `recruiting`, `contracts`, `schedule`, `standings`, `staff`, `coach`, `player`, `games`, `game`, `search`, `you`, `note_add`, `note_delete`, `bug`, and for the pages before the game `towns`, `similar`, `card`, `rename_save`, `delete_save`, `reveal`. `reads.rs` turns true ratings into your staff's reads, `views.rs` builds every screen's data, `setup.rs` the setup and the library, `files.rs` the files, `cards.rs` the world and save cards.
- `app/ui`: the screens, one page in plain HTML and JS. The pages before the game (opening, Load game, new career) are `pregame.js` and `pregame.css`, in their own look; everything from taking the job on is Showtime (`showtime.css`, Archivo fonts). A frosted left sidebar, the top bar with back and forward, search (⌘K), the date, what's next and Continue (space bar).
- `app/src-tauri`: the desktop app on Tauri 2. One command, `call`, runs the game on a worker thread so the window never freezes. Not in the Cargo workspace; built by `.github/workflows/mac-app.yml`.

## What a career has now

- **The pages before the game (Cain 2026-10-21, from the mock-ups at https://claude.ai/artifact/FrYwnUMKnBs1L31EEYPude):** dark graphite with one amber accent, the stadium photo (`app/ui/art/stadium.jpg`) behind the opening and every page title, full width, a fixed footer so Next is always in the window. They leave Showtime; Showtime starts once you take the job.
  - **Opening:** the stadium, Continue (your last save's card: team, line, record, what's next, the date, when it was saved), New career, Load game, Settings (version, game folder, check for updates), Quit in the Mac app. "Around football" shows the latest big headlines of that world.
  - **Load game:** every save with its team, date in the game, season, world and when it was saved; the save's file beside it; Load, Rename, Delete (asks first; only files in the saves folder) and "Open a save file…".
  - **World and save cards (`cards.rs`):** each world or save file carries a card in its header (the world's story: the dynasty, the rival, everyone else, the NFL, high school; national titles and Super Bowls by team; your team on a save), so the pages read them without loading the world. A library world written before cards gets one beside it (`name.afmworld.card.json`) the first time it opens.
  - **New career in six steps:** World (the library, worlds to download, import, build; its story; names are fixed when a world is built), You (name, date of birth, heritage, hometown with its schools, high school, college and years, degree, family and children with their school year, looks, an optional photo and a "Copy AI prompt" for it), Background (11 roads, placed from your profile), Ratings (19, below), Style (five choices of five, each with real coaches at its ends, and who calls the plays), Job (every head coaching job at any NFL club, college program or high school, with filters, search, sortable columns, and a job file: record, prestige, stadium, NIL budget, titles, the coach you replace, your boss, the expectation, the calendar, program grades).
  - **Your 19 ratings** on the 1-20 scale every coach uses, from your road's pool in whole points: teaching (8 groups), game day (scheme, game management, play calling, in-game adaptation), people (motivation, discipline, relationships), building the roster (recruiting, evaluation, network, negotiating). The page shows how the world reads you and three coaches in the world on your road.
  - **Who calls the plays** (plays and tactics topic 6.1: the coordinator, or the head coach himself, like Sean McVay): your coordinators, the offense, the defense or both. It starts on the side you came up teaching (a quarterbacks man calls the offense, a secondary man the defense, a special teams man hands both off). At college and NFL jobs the side you don't call stays with its coordinator; at a high school you call both sides (the school's assistants don't call plays yet).
  - **The four new ratings** (only yours; AI coaches keep their blends): play calling and in-game adaptation replace the AI's blend (0.6 scheme, 0.4 game management) on the side you call, shifting the caller's reading, situations and creativity; evaluation is your staff's eye for your own reads; negotiating replaces your general manager's skill in reading an agent's bluff at your NFL club. They grow each year like your other ratings (by doing the job) and fade late in a career.
- **Continue** runs day by day and stops on a decision, a huge moment anywhere (a regular-season game is huge only at its own level: a college rivalry game doesn't stop an NFL or high school career), a big one at your level or for your team, and **your own game day**, which opens that game's page. It also stops **the morning of** your game day and of your level's decision days (NFL: the tag deadline, the new league year, the draft, cutdown day, the trade deadline; college: the early signing day, signing day, the portal opening), before the day is played; the note says which morning. The top bar names your next game.
- **The main menu** (sidebar) goes back to the start screen. Closing a career that is open asks first and says how long since you saved; Back never leaves the game.
- **Questions in the game's own dialogs:** the Mac app's window (WKWebView through Tauri) has no `prompt` or `confirm`, so saving, a bug report, closing a career and building a world ask in the page itself. Saving and a bug report show their progress, and errors come through as plain words.
- **Records:** your team's record, your own record (career and at this job) and every staff record come from the season's results. Only a head coach's job has a record: an assistant's job, and a job that ended before the world's kept records begin, show a dash. A coach who takes a college job in February doesn't inherit last season's bowl game. From the new football year's first day (the day after the Super Bowl) to the next kickoff, home and the team page show last season's record as "9-8 in 2025".
- **Squads** leave out a player who walked away from football or whose playing days ended, though he stays on the school's list until the roster is rebuilt. A pro season with no club reads "Unsigned" on the career tab.
- **Inbox:** what is about you (your program, club or school, and you), your decisions, huge moments, the big ones you care about, the final of each of your games and your college staff's own messages, newest first. News has the same "yours" rule (an NFL club's own stories included; the NFL's stories are labelled NFL, not College).
- **Depth chart:** the order the engine plays next game (your order first, then the staff's), with the injured and the suspended listed after the starters, not as starters; an NFL club's list leaves out its practice squad.
- **NFL screens:** the schedule shows each final score and the kickoff time ("1:00 ET"); the team page keeps a book of seasons (record, head coach, how far it went) from this world's next season on, and its Super Bowls. Contracts count the cap as the league does: before the season only the top 51 contracts (the rule of 51) plus dead money, with last year's carryover added to the room; players in their last year carry a "Free agent soon" tag.
- **College jobs:** the job list holds every program that plays this year and can dress a team (22 players or more), and searching by state name works ("Texas").
- **Saves** are named after the career ("Name, Team"), with the world date and when you saved. **Build a world on this Mac** asks first, with the time and memory it takes, and has a Stop button.
- **Screens:** home, inbox, news (everything or yours), calendar (your games, your level's fixed dates and the big moments ahead), squad (unit, class and injured filters, sortable, grouped by position), depth chart, training, recruiting (a college's offers, visits, commitments and signings; a high school's players with college interest), contracts (an NFL club's books against the cap), schedule, game day (the opponent report, the film room, the plan, live play calling) and game pages (key plays, box score, advanced, drive chart, play by play, snaps, charts, officials), standings, staff, coach pages, team pages, player pages (overview, attributes, career, tendencies, recruiting, your notes), search, your profile, save, a bug pack.
- **What you see is your staff's read:** your own players' ratings as ranges whose width comes from the coach who reads them; other teams' players show only what is public. Potential appears only as words. Hidden coach ability is never shown, except your own ratings.
- **Dynamic Links on the player page:** the Overview tab lists the physical gifts your staff sees, strongest first, with how strong each looks, the numbers it is named by and where it helps (`reads::links`). Your own players are measured; pros and combine invites have public numbers; anyone else is a film guess; a boy still growing is read by what his frame projects to, in words only. Friday nights: the busiest Friday of the small world (about 300 games) runs in 3.1 s on 4 cores (`friday_check`), memory flat through the season.
- **High school seat:** every high school game in a live save runs on the engine, yours with full game pages (the others keep only each player's season line, as the history does); the schedule, your classification's standings, and a staff of teacher-coaches sized by enrollment who teach your players (below).
- **Training (6b topic 5, part):** a main focus per player. The focus grows up to half as fast again (1.5 times its share at full coachability) and his other skills of the same kind pay for it. Measured (`a_training_focus_grows_its_skill_and_costs_the_rest_a_little`, 400 players): the focus grows 1.29 times as fast and the year as a whole moves 1.2%, inside choice 2's 15%.

## Final build lane 1 (2026-10-22)

- **Ages come from birthdays.** Every age on a screen (squads, player pages, coaches, staff, your profile, the job lists) is counted from the person's date of birth on today's date, so a player turns 18 on his birthday, not at the end of a season. The player page shows the date ("Born 14 March 2009 (17)"). Some seniors are 18 all year and a few turn 19: about 1 boy in 10 was held back a grade (`docs/world-model.md`, "Final build lane 1").
- **Every name in a line is a link.** Inbox, news, Home and calendar lines link each player, coach, college, high school and NFL club they name, and your game results open their game page. A game you didn't keep opens the home team's schedule.
- **A player's public face:** the player page shows his followers, the ads and deals reported for him a year, and your staff's word for how he comes across on camera. His Aura itself is never shown.

## Game day (section 9, final build lane 2)

Built 2026-10-22 from `docs/design/game-day.md` (all 12 topics, Cain's answers to questions 1-6). Commands in `crates/afm-game/src/lane_gameday.rs` (with `lane_gameday/film.rs` for the tapes and `lane_gameday/page.rs` for the game page); screens in `app/ui/screens/gameday.js` and `.css`; the engine side in `docs/match-engine.md`, "Live games, officials and the toss".

- **Continue stops the morning of your game and opens the game day screen.** Continue with a game under way asks first, then your staff calls the rest. "Let the staff coach it" hands the whole game over.
- **The game day screen** (`gameday`): the matchup in a room lit by both teams, the records, the game's name and why it matters, the weather on game morning with the kicking line toward each end from warmups, and four tabs.
  - **Report:** your staff's report on the opponent from its last four tapes this season: run and pass by down and distance, by personnel, favourite calls by situation, big plays, coverages by down, pressure and run defense, the head coach's 4th-down and timeout habits, special teams, matchups and who is out. Every line gives its sample and how sure the staff is. The staff tags with errors by its film study (a sharp quality-control staff misses about 3%, a thin one about 20%) and tags more snaps the better it is. Your own finds from the tapes you studied sit above it, under your name.
  - **Film room:** every tape of the opponent this season (last six). Studying one takes about 3 hours of the week's film time (12 hours for a college or NFL head coach, 6 at a high school; less in a short week) and gives that tape's three strongest hints at once, each with its count and the textbook answer. The same habit on several tapes becomes one stronger hint. One click adds a hint to the plan: a corner to throw at, a receiver to bracket, a spy, kicking away from a returner, draws and screens against a rusher who wins upfield with speed, or a tell your players key from the snaps that show it (`Memory::study`, your staff reading those snaps as they really were). Taking a hint out undoes what it set unless another tape's hint holds it. A hint the plan has no answer for (a rusher who wins with power, say) is marked "For your eyes": it tells you something, and the battles decide it.
  - **Game plan:** the matchups (throw at their corner, bracket their receiver, spy their QB, kick away from their returner, more draws and screens against their rush) and your game rules: a 4th-down chart (staff, the numbers, aggressive, conservative, all from the true win chance of each choice), a two-point chart (staff, Dick Vermeil's chart in the 4th quarter, always kick) and the tempo (normal, no-huddle, milk the clock). The plan and the film you studied hold whether you call the game or your staff does.
  - **Calling:** which snaps stop for you (your situations: 3rd down, 4th down, red zone, goal line, two-minute drill and the decisions; every snap; none) and the time for a call (realism: 25 seconds, then your play caller's call goes in; learning: no clock); your play calling this season against your staff's (snaps, EPA a play, success rate).
- **The live game** (`live`): the scoreboard with timeouts, challenges, the ball and your win chance; the win-chance line so far; the field with the line of scrimmage, the line to gain and the drive. Each stop shows the situation, the personnel they sent on, the analytics staff's numbers on 4th down ("go 61%, punt 57%"; a high school has no analyst, just the book's conversion rate), your play caller's call with his reason and how it has worked today, his next choices, and the full sheet (any personnel, backfield, set, motion and play; off the week's sheet is less practiced). The coin toss (receive, defer, kick, take the wind; in overtime the level's own choices), extra point or two, onside or deep, challenge or not (your replay assistant says throw it or don't) and halftime (each unit's half, what works and what doesn't, each coordinator's adjustment with its reason as one click, the trainers' report) stop when they are yours. The sideline: timeouts, the tempo, a word to a coordinator (throw at, bracket, spy, kick away, draws and screens). Spots read from your side of the ball ("at their 41"), and the board says "Coin toss", "Kickoff", "Try" or "Halftime" when that is what's waiting. Watch (W) lets your staff call the snaps at key-play or every-snap pace with a "Step in" at any one; "Staff to the end" finishes it. The feed shows every snap newest first, the snaps since your last call rising in one by one, your calls tagged beside your staff's, the officials' close calls and injuries.
- **Keys on the live screen:** Enter goes with the staff, 1-9 picks a card, G/P/K go, punt or kick on 4th down, K/G kick or go for two after a touchdown, F the full sheet, T a timeout, N takes the next snap, W watches, E hands the rest to the staff, Esc steps in or closes the sheet. Space never plays the day from the live screen.
- **The final whistle:** the result, your calls against your staff's, the plays that decided it by their swing in your win chance, game balls, the trainers and the officials; then the day finishes and the game page opens.
- **A game saved mid-way** resumes at the same snap: the save keeps the game's seed and every answer you gave.
- **The game page** (`game_page`, every kept game of yours): the scoreboard in a room lit by both teams, the score by quarter, the win-chance line with the big swings marked (each a link to its snap), game balls, a "Go to" line, and tabs: Key plays (the swings, scores and turnovers with each call and its reason; your calls against your staff's), Box score (team stats and the scoring summary: each score with its try, the drive and the score after it; passing clean and under pressure with the depth of target, yards before contact, air yards, yards after the catch and separation, pressures, kickers and punters, returners), Advanced (EPA a play, success rate, explosive plays, early-down pass rate, red zone, pressure, depth of target, time to throw, yards before contact, field position; by half; every 4th down against the model with what it cost), Drive chart (every drive drawn on the field; a click shows its plays), Play by play (every snap with both calls, the pre-snap look and the why, EPA and the win chance after it; filters by team, key plays, scores and your calls), Snaps (by personnel and against each defensive package, and every player's snaps on offense, defense and special teams), Charts (the pass chart, runs by gap, EPA by drive and by quarter, the win-chance line) and Officials (each team's flags and yards, every flag with its player, and every close call: whether it was right, who reviewed it and what came of it). Every player in a play links to his page. Other lanes add tabs through `window.gamePageTabs` (the Watch tab).
- **For the 2D and 3D views:** `live_frames {play}` gives the tracking of a play of your live game (the plays since your last call are kept); the film room plays finished games and the live game from their seeds ("The film room" below).

### Decided in the final build (Claude's pick)

- **Saving mid-game is supported:** the save keeps the live game (its seed and your answers), and loading resumes at the same snap. Weighed against blocking a save during a game; the design asks for it (topic 12).
- **The week's work counts when you hand the game off:** the plan, the film you studied and your game rules apply to a game your staff coaches, as they would in real life. A game with none of it plays exactly as the morning built it (the identity test).
- **Film time:** 12 hours a week for a college or NFL head coach, 6 at a high school, scaled by the days since your last game (half a week to 1.4 weeks); a tape takes 3 hours. Weighed a flat count of tapes; time follows people topic 5's budget.
- **Three hints a tape**, merged by habit across tapes (the design's proposal); at most two of them "for your eyes" notes, so a tape whose strongest finds are all lost battles still shows the best one the plan can answer (lane 7, found when a test world's only tape showed three).
- **The staff's tagging error** (3% to 22% of tags wrong by film study) and its tagged share (55% to 100%); the report's "sure" words come from the tagged sample (sure at 40 snaps, fairly sure at 15).
- **The 4th-down charts** use the engine's own win chance (`live::win_prob`) on its expected points; aggressive goes within two points of win chance, conservative needs four. College and high school overtime have no punts, so the staff decides there.
- **The two-point chart** is Dick Vermeil's, in the 4th quarter only.
- **The realism clock** stops for halftime and the toss (no play clock runs there); when it runs out the play caller's call goes in, with a note.
- **Watching** paces key plays (scores, turnovers, sacks, gains of 20 or more, 4th downs, reviews, injuries) at about 1.7 seconds and the rest at a glance; "every snap" pace shows each for about a second.
- **The game page's success rule and key plays** are the confirmed ones (40/60/100%; swings of 6 points of win chance or more, scores, turnovers, kicks, long gains, reviews).
- **Packages on the Snaps tab** count defensive backs on the field (4 base, 5 nickel, 6 dime, 7 quarter; 8 linemen and linebackers goal line). Kneels, spikes and tries don't count as personnel calls there; every player's snap count still has them.
- **Draws and screens** is a plan line of its own (staff, more, as usual) and the answer to a speed rusher on tape: it sets the offense's lean against an upfield rush (`plan.vs.draws`, at least 0.7) for the game.

## Your front office (final build, lane 10)

Every choice the AI made for your team is yours now, on the league's real rules, and goes through the same market code the AI clubs use (`world/front_office/`, the game's `lane_frontoffice/`, the screens `app/ui/screens/fo-*.js`).

- **Who decides:** each duty is yours or your staff's (Front office screen). Contracts, tags, free agency, trades and the cutdown start with you; the cap manager's restructures, waiver claims and the practice squad start with your staff. A duty you hand over is decided by the person hired for it, the AI's way; acting on one takes it back. Your club's AI code asks `front_office::yours` and skips what you keep.
- **Talks with agents (Negotiation):** years, new money a year, the guarantee, the signing bonus (part of the guarantee), void years (from 2016), the shape (even, rising, back-loaded, or a year-one base at the minimum), incentives, a no-trade clause and a starting job. The live preview lays the deal out year by year exactly as it would be signed (base, bonus, roster and workout bonuses, the cap number, what's guaranteed, your room after it) and warns when year one doesn't fit. The agent answers with the market's own score of your offer (`market::score`, the player's hidden ideal and goals): a deal, a counter with his ask, an offer that stands against the other clubs', or the end of talks when lowballs wear his patience out (a week's cold shoulder in free agency, a month for your own player). He may claim other interest: real when an AI club's own offer beats yours, a bluff otherwise by his honesty, and your negotiating gives you a read of which it is. Your own players' wants you know; a free agent's you learn on a visit or after a few rounds.
- **The calendar of a league year:** the tag window (22 days before the league year to the deadline 8 days before it), one tag a club a year from 2011 (a franchise and a transition tag before), the exclusive tag priced from this year's top five; tenders for restricted (first, second, original round or right of first refusal) and exclusive-rights players before the league year, taken back after it; the two-day negotiating window (talks and verbal deals, nothing signed, no visits) and free agency from the league year; offer sheets on other clubs' restricted players, five days to match yours; fifth-year options for the 2011 class on, undecided options declined at the deadline; the trade window from the league year to the deadline. Reminders reach your inbox the morning they matter, and the deadlines stop Continue.
- **The cap sheet:** five league years of every contract's cap number (hover for base, bonus spread, roster bonus and guarantees; void years in italics), what a release costs and saves today, a post-June 1 designation (two a year from 2011) with its split, restructures (with void years from 2016, and never past the real high cap hit), dead money charge by charge, carryover, the rule of 51 until the first game, and your picks. Your room is the league's: the cap less what counts. The 5% your AI cap managers keep for the season is shown as their advice, not a limit.
- **Trades:** build a trade with any club (players and picks over three drafts, compensatory picks from 2017), ask their GM or make it. He answers by his own chart and read of your players (`trades::player_points`, `pick_value`, his mode and urgency), needs to gain by a margin (more if you fleeced him before), and counters with the cheapest of your picks that closes the gap. A no-trade clause is waived only for a stronger club. A conditional pick moves up a round if the player plays, starts or makes the Pro Bowl, valued by the chance he does, settled after the season. Calls from AI buyers wait two days for your answer; a player on your block is shopped like the AI's surplus; "Shop him" shows each club's best pick package. The seller keeps the bonus, as the league has it.
- **Roster moves:** the cut to 53 by position in your staff's order, with your call (53, squad, cut) standing and the staff filling every place you leave open; the practice squad by the era's eligibility and veteran places, four protected a week from 2020, signing players to the squad and up to the 53; the waiver wire, claimed in the league's order (worst record first), a claim taking over his contract. The roster limit is 90 from the day after the Super Bowl to the cutdown, 53 in the season. Players off the street can sign any day; players on the market wait for the league year.
- **Commands:** `fo_office`, `fo_set_owner`, `fo_cap`, `fo_expiring`, `fo_talk`, `fo_offer`, `fo_preview`, `fo_tag`, `fo_tender`, `fo_rescind`, `fo_option`, `fo_sheet`, `fo_free_agents`, `fo_visit`, `fo_restructure`, `fo_release`, `fo_trades`, `fo_trade_team`, `fo_trade_eval`, `fo_trade_propose`, `fo_shop`, `fo_block`, `fo_call`, `fo_cutdown`, `fo_plan`, `fo_squad`, `fo_protect`, `fo_sign_squad`, `fo_sign_up`, `fo_wire`, `fo_claim`.
- **Tests** (`src/tests/front_office.rs`): only your club and what you keep is yours; your offer is weighed by the market's own score (a lowball gets the ask back, the ask signs); one tag a club a year at the tag's price, none after the deadline; a trade needs the other GM to gain on his own chart and moves the picks; your cut plan stands and the staff fills the rest to the limit; a release leaves its bonus on your cap; the negotiating window agrees terms but signs nothing.

### Decided in the final build (Claude's pick): the NFL desk

- Default owners: you keep contracts, tags, free agency, trades and the cutdown (the head coach and GM seat); the cap manager, waivers and the squad start with your staff (the jobs real GMs delegate day to day). Weighed against everything yours from day one: too many daily calls for a calm game.
- A visit leans a free agent your way by up to 3% of his score, by your relationships. Weighed: none (visits would mean nothing) against the AI's larger home pull.
- The agent opens 2% to 8% over the player's ideal (the greedier, the higher) with 3 points more guarantee; lowballs under 70% and 55% of his score cost a quarter and nearly half of his patience, the hot-tempered faster.
- Your room is the league's (the cap less what counts) and the AI's 5% season reserve is advice only: the league stops a club only at the cap.
- The other GM needs a gain of 40 chart points (more after you fleeced him); his counter is the cheapest one or two of your picks on your own chart that close the gap.
- A conditional pick moves up one round (first-round picks can't), on games played, starts or a Pro Bowl, valued at the GM's chance it happens.
- The waiver claim period holds a player on the wire until the next day, as the league's 4 p.m. claiming deadline does.
- Street free agents (released, unsigned) sign any day, as real reserve/future and street signings do; players on the open market (the market's own line, consensus 66) wait for the league year, as the AI's market does.
- Staff hiring and firing and coordinators' proposals are lane 6's (its brief owns staff people, hiring and firing, and proposals); this lane doesn't duplicate them.
- **Your staff tells you what it did for you.** When the cap manager restructures or releases a cap casualty on your club, one inbox line names each move with the room it made, the dead money and the June 1 split. Before the league year it says why (room for the draft class and the market) and where to take the duty back; on the first game's day it says the rule of 51 has ended. Weighed against silence, which left the cap sheet changed with no word of why.
- **A verbal agreement is worded by its phase.** In the negotiating window it signs when the league year opens, on that date; after it, the deal signs on the market's own day unless a clearly better offer sways him. One sentence for both read wrong in either phase.
- **Trade lines are written before anything moves**, so the wire names the club that gave each piece, and picks read in words ("the Bears' 2026 fourth-round pick", "a compensatory pick"). An own pick reads "a 2026 fourth-round pick".
- **Morning stops only for your own deadlines:** the long-term deadline when you tagged someone this year and he is still unsigned, the fifth-year option day (from 2014) when a first-round pick from three drafts ago is undecided, and the waiver claim and practice squad days when those duties are yours. Stopping on every date of the league year was weighed and rejected as too many stops; stopping on none let a deadline pass unseen.
- **The cutdown ends in one line:** how many were cut and kept on the squad, your own calls against the staff's, the three best players let go (linked), and who goes on waivers (under four seasons) against who is a free agent at once. The cutdown screen shows each player's draft slot ("2024 R3 · #81" or "Undrafted").
- **Seasons count once.** The season just played accrues at the Super Bowl, so the desk and the AI's re-sign talks never add one more (restricted players had read as unrestricted). A deal replaced before the league year books its void years' bonus as dead money, and an extension keeps the old bonus in the years it was spread over. Tags and tenders can be withdrawn until the league year; a void-year bonus is checked before a tag, tender or re-sign takes its room.
- **Market cells say what's next:** a released player reads "Released · talks from" his date, your own players "Yours until the league year", anyone else "No deal yet". A blank cell read as a bug.

### The high school seat

Final build lane 10 (`world/front_office/school.rs`, the game's `lane_frontoffice/school.rs`, the screens in `app/ui/screens/fo-school.js`). Everything here is your school's alone: AI schools keep the built model, and every draw is keyed by the career, so no other draw in the world moves. Commands: `school_office`, `school_squads`, `school_area`, `set_squad`, `reset_squads`, `squads_owner`, `squad_suggestion`, `booster_wish`; screens School office, Squads, and School and feeders.

- **Squads.** A school fields a JV team from about 450 students and a freshman team from about 1,100, each only with 22 players to dress; a smaller school plays everyone on varsity. Your staff makes the lists the way real staffs do: every senior and junior on varsity (a junior below the three-deep goes to JV), a sophomore in the two-deep and a freshman who starts move up, the rest play JV and freshman ball, varsity at most 72. A team short of 22 is folded into the one above, except that a short JV first takes the freshmen in (one sub-varsity team, as most schools of that size run), so the varsity doesn't swell past 72. On the Squads screen you set them yourself or leave them with the staff. Seniors can't play JV and only freshmen play on the freshman team (most state associations' rules).
- **JV and freshman games.** Each week's JV and freshman games are played the Thursday before the varsity's Friday, against the same school, each at the other site, on the engine with 10-minute quarters and the running clock. They have box scores, best lines and season lines on the Squads screen; nothing else in the world reads them, so the memory gate holds (a season keeps about 20 small games).
- **Moving up.** Your staff watches the tape: after two games, a JV or freshman player whose read reaches the varsity's last man in the two-deep at his spot is ready. With the squads in your hands that is a suggestion (Move him, Leave him); with the staff's, they move him. After the last regular-season Thursday the best JV sophomores and juniors dress for the varsity's last game and the playoffs.
- **Reps.** The staff's squads change nobody's development, so your school grows its players as any other does. A player you keep off varsity who would start there gets a JV starter's reps instead of a varsity starter's.
- **The people you answer to.** The principal, the athletic director, the superintendent (the head of school at a private school) and the booster club president are the same persons the meeting room, the letters and their own pages know (`folk::cast`, lane 6): one name, age, years in the job and temper each, and one trust in you that the meetings and this desk both move. The office shows what each weighs (wins, grades, the numbers coming out for football, behavior), how patient each is, and trust only in words ("Firmly behind you" to "Wants a change"); each name opens the person's page. A local man (grew up in the town, went to the school) and your presence start a little ahead. When a term ends, the new person starts with an open mind.
- **Goals.** The principal sets the season's goals in the start-of-year meeting before school starts (lane 6's meeting, where you can argue them down or take on more): wins, the playoffs at a school that expects them, players kept eligible, staying out of trouble and, with a rival, beating it. The week school starts the athletic director adds the program's numbers (players out for football) and, at a school that expects deep runs, the booster club its playoff wins. The office lists them all, each with who set it, how you dealt and where it stands ("6 of 8", "In the field", "Won 28-21"). After your last game the athletic director and the booster club judge every goal by what each weighs, through their patience, in their own words, and the superintendent follows the principal; the principal gives hers or his in the season review meeting. In January (the carousel's day) the school decides on its coach: when the principal and the AD have both lost faith, after two seasons' grace, the coaching stipend isn't renewed. You keep teaching there, and the job market is your way back.
- **The booster club.** A year's money from the spring membership drive, the golf tournament in June, program ads and stadium banners in August, concessions at each home game and the December banquet, sized by the school's size, the area's money and the last two seasons' winning (about $5K at a small rural school, $60-120K at a big suburban one, several hundred thousand at a powerhouse). Out: pregame meals every game, charter buses for long road trips and every playoff trip, helmet reconditioning in May, new gear in July, the film subscription, and the next project on your wish list when the money is there at the end of June. Seven projects, from speed and agility equipment ($7,500) to an artificial turf practice field ($450,000), each adding a little to the school's resources (the number every high school's development reads). The office shows the books, money in and out, and the wish list you order.
- **Your teaching job.** A subject from your degree, five of a seven-period day (four at a school of 1,500 or more), a teacher's salary from the state's average pay (NEA 2023-24, growing 3% a year) plus a coaching stipend by state and school size. At a big public school in Texas the head coach is the campus athletic coordinator instead, on a coordinator's salary that covers both. Your week's hours: about 38 in the building plus planning and grading, and coaching from a dozen hours in the offseason program to 35 or more in season.
- **The school and its feeder area.** Enrollment, classification and playoff district, mascot, the program's standing, weight room and staff, the area's money and safety, the transfer rule; the feeder middle schools and youth teams with their coaches, boys, share of your freshmen, last season, the freshmen coming up and who on your roster came from each; how many players moved in or transferred; the school's book of seasons, its head coaches, and its players in college and the NFL.

### Decided in the final build (Claude's pick): the high school seat and setup

- Teacher pay from the NEA's state averages; the campus athletic coordinator only at big public Texas schools.
- Assistant seats only at college (coordinators and position coaches) and the NFL (coordinators): the staffs the world keeps; high school assistants stay staff, not a seat.
- The head coach lets a 3-point gap go; past it his chance to overrule rises to 90% at a 9-point gap, more with a temper: assistants get their way on close calls, not on obvious ones.
- Offers wait three days (five at a high school) and you can hold three at once: real windows, and the AI's searches don't stall on you.
- A free start lets it be known you want jobs at the level you picked (a high school job near home): the market screen changes it any day.
- Aura from your road and people ratings rather than a slider: it stays hidden, as every presence is.
- You never retire through the AI's health draws, and the carousel never retires you: your career ends when you end it.
- Fictional names change colleges, conferences and NFL nicknames and keep towns, club cities and bowls: the world's geography stays real, as FM's fictional mode keeps its countries.
- A new head coach keeps an assistant who knows him far more often (45% plus up to 50%): coaching trees.
- A job you turned down or left waits 60 days before it comes to you again.
- A new head coach's people start between "Watching how it goes" and "Pleased with you", never "Losing patience" before a game: the man they just hired gets the benefit of the doubt, and the goals move it from there.
- Out of work at college, interest starts at FCS and up: a coach in his prime doesn't open with Division II and NAIA jobs, and three such offers on the first day read wrong in play. One setting on the market screen lowers it.

- **One set of school people.** Lane 6 and this lane both drew your principal, athletic director and feeder coaches; the school desk now reads lane 6's persons (names, ages, terms, gender, patience) and keeps one trust each in lane 6's store, so the office, the meeting room and the people pages agree. The principal's goals are the meeting's; this desk adds only what the principal doesn't set (the AD's numbers, the boosters' playoff wins) and no longer posts goals of its own. Weighed against keeping two goal lists with two bosses' names, which read as a bug in play. Lane 6's feeder coaches are one per 600 students up to four, the feeder schools the area page lists, and the youth league row names its president.
- **Your school made the playoffs** is known when the field is seeded, so a first-round loss still counts as making it (the record alone missed it). The principal's playoff goal reads the same at your school; anyone else's still stands in with seven wins.
- **Squad suggestions expire.** The staff's "move him up" ideas last 14 days; a call-up for the last game and the playoffs lasts until the season ends, and every suggestion clears when it is over. Each suggestion names one player per position, the best read first, and skips a position that already has a varsity suggestion waiting. Before, October's ideas still showed in January, and three players were named for one place.
- **The school and feeders page** puts the school, its seasons and its head coaches in one column and the feeder schools in the other, so neither column runs empty. Below them, everyone from the school still playing is in one sortable table (name, position, class, where he is now, linked, and his level), grouped NFL, free agents, the draft pool, then college by division, newest class first. ### Decided in the final build (Claude's pick): the college desk

- **Who owns what at first:** the board, visits and the portal are yours, and NIL is your staff's. A new head coach recruits himself from day one; the collective's money is its own job until you take it.
- **Your order decides who can commit when the class fills.** A signing day is one batch, so it can't stop to ask. Commitments go first, then your order. An offer past the class's places shows "Past the class". I weighed this against asking mid-stage, which the world's matching can't do.
- **The coordinator fills open spots** on a signing day when the switch is on (the default), and each offer carries his reason. Off, only your offers go out. A real coordinator works the board's leftovers.
- **Walk-ons stay with your staff.** A walk-on place is a roster call made from the leftover pool, not a recruiting offer.
- **Your own time:** up to 35 targets, about a class and a half. They get the same relationship weight an AI head coach's own targets get.
- **His interest is words from the signing day's own appeal:** utility, his feel for the school, contacts, an official visit, an early offer, and the hold of a commitment. Never a number.
- **A visit shows his feel for the school in words**, the same fixed feel his signing day weighs.
- **Visits:**
  - An official starts on a Friday, one per school and five per recruit. Seniors take them from September 1, and from 2018 juniors from April 1 to June 30. In the fall an official is built around a home game. There is no program-wide cap: the old FBS two-year limit isn't modelled, and the AI has none either.
  - Unofficials are on Saturdays outside dead periods, and in the fall on home game days.
  - A home visit is for seniors in a contact period, once a cycle, never on a Sunday, and only after an offer.
  - Drop-ins are on Fridays in an evaluation period, for juniors and seniors.
  - He says yes or no by how your program ranks among his schools, his officials left, his family's money against the miles for an unofficial, and how firm a commitment elsewhere is.
- **Tampering:** a quiet word before he enters adds 0.05 to your appeal at his decision. That is a quarter of a childhood lean. It also makes him a little more likely to enter. One call in eight becomes news, and the NCAA never punishes it, as it rarely proves one.
- **Talks to keep a player** sway him by your relationships rating, on the same formula as an AI head coach's sway: 1 - 0.03 x (relationships - 10), between 0.75 and 1.25. One talk per player a year.
- **Portal:** you are free of the AI's habit of ten adds a year, but bound by the roster limit and the level's rules. An offer needs him in the portal; before that it is a quiet word.
- **NIL:** your deals replace the collective's split on July 1. A player with eligibility left whose usual share is $25K or more, and who gets under half of it, grows restless: his lean is x1.35 + 0.01, for the reason "his NIL deal fell short of his worth". A recruit's promise replaces the collective's usual offer in his signing-day weighing, booster cash included. A promise is capped at his position's real market top, and a portal offer at 2.5 times that.
- **Pulling an offer** from a committed recruit ends the commitment. It cools that high school's pipeline (x0.85) and is news for a 3-star or better.
- **A duty with your staff refuses your own moves on it** instead of letting them sit unread, so the screen never shows a move that does nothing.
- **Your staff's read of a recruit on your board:** a half-width of 3 + (20 - the reader's eye) x 0.2. Off the board it is a stranger's 7. Potential words come only for recruits on your board.
- **Who your staff knows in Find recruits:** every recruit the services rank, plus the unranked in your state, your footprint (0.3 and up) and your pipeline schools. For junior colleges, every unsigned JUCO player with a season played.
- **The public class rank** follows the composite services' formula, the same one the signing-day news uses: stars to 99, 92, 85, 78 and 70, with each recruit weighted down the class (sigma 10). It counts commitments and signees and ranks within your division.
- **Your class keeps its plan at each position.** On a signing day your board fills a position up to its plan (or to the commitments you already hold there, if more), not to every offer you made there. An offer that would overfill a position shows it isn't committable yet. Before, a board with five offers at receiver signed all five.
- **Signing-day lines go best names first.** The day's line lists your signees and the ones you lost by stars, with each player and school linked.
- **The portal is only for players with a season left.** A player whose eligibility ends with the season just played can't enter (no "0 left" entrants), on any path: the main rule, a program dropped, a dismissal or a teammate leaving. On January 2 eligibility is settled before the January window opens, not after. "Yrs" on the Entrants tab is the seasons he could play from next season on (his class is in its tooltip), and "Decides" shows the date with the days in its tooltip; division and class columns start hidden so the table fits. In a test world this cut the year's entrants from 6,505 to 5,646.
- **NIL reads fame.** The NIL table and a player's file show his followers, his brand (what his name is worth a year, from fame and Aura) and, hidden by default, how he comes across on camera in your staff's words. Aura itself stays hidden, as every presence is.
- **Portal pushes in plain words:** a rotation player leaves for "no starting job waiting elsewhere" when that's the pull, and a competitor's push says "his competitiveness"; a content one stays "content where he is".

## Memory (final build lane 1)

In-season memory is under the agreed 4 GB: 3.81 GB at every stop from the first September stop to the first game, against 5.09 GB before. Results per seed are the same (how it was checked is below). Measured with `memory_season` (`crates/afm-game/src/bin/memory_season.rs`: opens a world file as the game does, starts a career at Iowa and presses Continue to the first game, then weighs each store) on the 30-year world for first saves, on a 4-core Linux machine, 2026-10-22:

| Stop | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Open | 3.36 GB | 2.99 GB |
| 10 February | 3.38 GB | 2.89 GB |
| 24 April | 3.50 GB | 2.91 GB |
| 2, 4 and 5 September | 5.09 GB | 3.81 GB |
| Highest at any moment | 5.09 GB | 4.00 GB |

What changed (how each works is in `docs/world-model.md`, "Memory compaction"):

- Season rows grow by exactly one row and are 136 bytes, not 176: 0.99 to 0.44 GB in September.
- The stat book keeps only each row's non-zero numbers: 0.60 to 0.28 GB.
- Last year's college season drops its games, rosters and lines once the new one starts: 0.09 GB.
- The game hands freed pages back to the system after a load and after each Continue (`memory.rs`): about 0.2 GB in season.

Same results: a small world (seed 2026, scale 0.05, the history and a season to come, `memory_check 2026 3 1 0.05`) built from main and from this build gives the same fingerprint, a hash of every person's season rows, ratings, stage and school, every alumnus, coach and news line (`memory_check` prints it). World files keep their shape: the new code opens the old world file, and writes the same bytes for every changed field (`tests/compact.rs`).

## The screens' core (final build, lane 9)

The shell and the core screens of section 10, on Showtime. The game side is `crates/afm-game/src/lane_core.rs` with `lane_core/` (`desk.rs` the inbox and your desk, `mail.rs` who writes what, `links.rs` the link markup, `pages.rs` the pages' data, `tables.rs` the big tables, `people.rs` the named people around you). The world side is `crates/afm-engine/src/world/desk.rs` (kept in the save: mail, notes, my players, watches, contacts, big moments). The page side is `app/ui/screens/core.js` and `core.css`, with the shell in `app.js`.

- **Mail is written each morning** for the days the clock just ran (`lane_core::after_days`, after Continue, a day, a holiday, a load). It is built from the calendar's events and from people's own records, because the calendar posts only the loud recruiting moves. At most the last 30 days are filed, and anything older than 30 days clears unless saved, so the desk stays small (about 100 letters).
- **Letterheads by sender:** your school or club (crest in its colours), the conference or league office (seal), the national paper and the local paper (masthead, clips in columns), an email (header rows), a staff memo (typed). Each letter rises off a paper stack over the blurred inbox; Newer and Older (or ← →) turn the page.
- **Who writes:** the athletic director or general manager or principal (welcome, staff changes, a letter after three straight losses with "Answer by"), the conference office (realignment in your conference, 10h1), the compliance office or league office (windows and deadlines at your level), personnel (each commitment, the week's offers and visits, signing days, the portal), communications (postgame notes with the box, morning clips of your team and your rivals, 10h5), the trainer (the NFL's Wednesday injury report), the chief of staff (Monday's "Your people" memo, 10h3), the national paper's front page (huge news at your own level). A high school seat gets colleges' offer emails for its players from the position coach who offered, and the player's own commit email.
- **Answers do what they say:** a call or a note to a player raises his mood (and his trust in you, if you are his coach), "add to my players" adds them, and every answer is logged on the player's "History with you" tab.
- **Inbox screen:** a one-line list under day headings, unread bold with a dot and the count in the sidebar, All/Unread/Read, the keyword menu (Your job, Your players, Recruiting, Portal, Injuries, Money, Spring practice, Draft and NFL, Staff, Your people, Media, League), full-text search top right, stars and the Saved mails button, the Notes panel, Mark all read, "Clears in N d" in a letter's last ten days.
- **Lower divisions (10h2):** in December and April a college seat gets watch lists of standout players below its own division (the portal season).
- **Home:** the rotating hero (8 seconds, team colours, dots and arrows, holds on hover, stops under reduced motion), storyline posters with a drawn scene per kind, the next seven days, your boss's latest word, your team in words, tracked players with how the read moved, Around football filtered to what concerns you, the newest mail.
- **Linking (L1-L8):** every line the engine writes carries link markup (`[[p:id|name]]`, teams, coaches, games, days, screens) that `rich()` turns into links; hover cards on any name; shift-click opens the page beside the screen, ⌘-click its own window; right-click a name for a menu; a "Go to" line under each heading; the trail of where you came from in the top bar; tabs kept in the route so Back returns to them.
- **The player page:** a hero with his jersey number, the face slot (Lane 12 fills it), his position history on hover and his level rating ("76 among FBS players"); tabs Overview (your staff's read, the five mental attributes that matter at his position, measurables with where each was taken, where else he could play), Attributes (a group menu), Reports, Stats (seasons, game logs and home/away splits from your kept games), Career, History with you, Tendencies, Recruiting, Contract, Notes (edit in place). Other lanes add tabs through `PLAYER_TABS` (Lane 11's Development tab sits after Stats).
- **Tables (S8):** click to sort, shift-click a second sort, Columns, saved views, the filter builder, "On my list" and "Has my note" chips, compare up to five, CSV (into the game folder's `exports`), arrow keys and Enter. Leaderboards and the player finder are paged by the game: it sorts every row and the page draws only what is in view. Group heads over the columns span only the columns shown, so hiding one keeps them in line.
- **My players (S5):** add or remove anywhere; the list shows your read now against when you added him. A search in the finder can be saved as a watch; every Monday personnel writes who is new.
- **Search (S6):** ⌘K finds players, coaches, teams and schools, towns (opens the finder on that hometown), your kept games, records, screens, your notes and your mail.
- **Also:** news feeds (beat writers, national, recruiting, your level, fans) with how sure each line is and story chains; the calendar as a month with your games and scores; standings with playoff brackets; the coach page (jobs, coaching tree, players he developed, notes); your profile (contract, tree, the job market); the team page; History (champions, record books, NFL records, Halls of Fame, awards, the coaching carousel, drafts); the big moments (a commitment, a signing, a title, your hiring) full screen; holiday mode; Settings (theme, text size, reduced motion, hover cards and their delay, the keys).

### Decided in the final build (Claude's pick)

- Mail is composed when the clock moves and stored in the save, not rebuilt on each look: letters keep their wording and answers once read. Weighed against rebuilding from events each time, which would drop answered state.
- Made-up names for the papers ("The National Football Daily"; a local masthead per town) and for staff the world doesn't keep (communications, personnel, compliance, the chief of staff, a commissioner), seeded so they stay the same.
- What each answer does is small and honest (mood +0.03 to +0.08, trust +0.01 to +0.03, only toward your own coach), so talking can't outweigh playing time.
- The front page carries only huge news at your own level (and the world's); other levels wait on the News screen (10h5).
- "Your rivals" for clips: a college's named rivalries, an NFL club's division, a high school's district group.
- Every game of yours makes the morning clips, with the result first.
- ⌘-click in the Mac app opens the page wide beside the screen (the app has one window); in a browser it opens a real window.
- A player with a note stays on "My players" until the note is deleted.
- Watches run on Mondays and report only players the search hadn't found before.
- The level rating counts only what your staff can read.
- A high school bracket shows your own road through the playoffs: the engine keeps who is still alive, not each round's pairings.
- Jersey numbers follow position rules (a quarterback 1-19, a lineman 50-79 and so on), seeded per player, until the world keeps real numbers.
- Measurables show where each was taken (his school's listed roster, his team's spring measurements, the combine); arms, hands and wingspan in eighths of an inch as the combine writes them; drills only for players who were tested.

## The film room: 2D and 3D game views (final build, lane 13)

Later extras topics 2 and 3 (X2, X3) and the four film-room tools (X8), from `docs/design/later-extras.md` and `docs/design/screens.md` topic 12. Commands in `crates/afm-game/src/lane_views.rs` (`lane_views/film.rs` builds the film, `numbers.rs` the jersey numbers, `venue.rs` the stadium, light and weather); screens in `app/ui/screens/views.js` (the 2D drawing), `views-watch.js` (the film room) and `views.css`; the 3D view in `app/ui/screens/views/views3d.js` on three.js r149 (vendored, MIT, `THREE-LICENSE`). The engine side is in `docs/match-engine.md`, "Frames for the views".

- **A game is kept as what decided it, not as its tracking.** Each kept game of yours (the last 40) carries its replay setup: both teams as they dressed, the rules, the weather, the seed and, for a game you called live, every answer you gave (about 50 KB packed). Watching plays it again with frames on, so the film is the same game snap for snap, and only the game on screen has frames in memory. A game played on an earlier version of the match engine can't play back (the film would be a different game), and its Watch tab says so in plain words.
- **The Watch tab** (game page): the film on the left, a side panel on the right. Under the film, play and pause, the snap before and after, a tenth of a second back and forward, the scrubber with the snap, the throw, the catch, flags and your drawings marked, the clock, the speed (quarter, half, normal, double), the 2D/3D switch and full screen. On the film: the score bug, a caption with the play as the play-by-play tells it, a card between snaps (down and distance, the call and the result), Next Gen tiles after the snap (time to throw, air yards, separation at the catch, the carrier's top speed in mph, men in the box; chips for a blitz, play action, pressure) and the picked man's card. Every "Watch" link on the game page (Key plays, the play-by-play's ▶) opens that snap here.
- **The 2D view** (X3): the field from above, in the stadium's own colours, with dots by default, each with his number in his team's colours (numbers by each level's real rules, `numbers.rs`), the ball, the line of scrimmage, the line to gain, route trails and the separation at the catch. Figures sized by each body (height, weight, shoulders) are a setting. Four cameras: All-22, tight on the ball, end zone and free (drag, wheel).
- **The 3D coaches' view** (X2), on since Cain okayed the clip (2026-10-23); "3D coaches' view" in the View panel turns it off and leaves the 2D view only. Bodies from the player model (height and weight set the build), helmets with the team's stripe, jersey numbers front and back; the stadium generated from the real venue (an NFL club's real stadium, roof and seats; a college's from its capacity as a bowl, horseshoe or two sides; a high school's bleachers, press box and light poles), the crowd as its seats times attendance, a video board, goalposts and pylons; light from the kickoff hour and date (day, dusk, night under the lights, a dome), the day's weather (rain, snow, wind, fog); the level's officials where their mechanics put them and the chain crew with the chains. Cameras: coaches' end zone (1), All-22 sideline (2), broadcast (3) and free (4). A big play (a score, a turnover, a sack, 20+ yards) replays from the end zone at half speed after it. One switch between 3D and 2D, the button or V, even mid-play; the film room opens in the view you used last.
- **Quality for the two Macs:** auto picks High on an Apple M chip (60 fps, shadows, the full crowd, sideline players) and Low on Intel graphics (30 fps, no shadows, a third of the crowd, no sideline players); Medium sits between, and each is a setting. A view that can't hold its frame rate steps itself down once. The frame rates are each tier's aim; nobody has measured them on a Mac yet (the build machine renders in software, at about 4 fps).
- **The four film-room tools** (X8): **drawing** on a paused frame (arrow, line, circle, freehand, five colours; undo with Z; saved with the game, shown on the scrubber and listed in the Draw panel; they fade while the film plays); **cut-ups** (the Cut-ups panel filters the game's snaps by side, down, distance, field zone, clock, personnel, formation, play type, coverage, rush and result, any mix of them, and plays the cut-up in order; a drawing's snaps are a cut-up too); **isolate one player** (click a man, then Isolate or I: the camera follows him and the rest dim; his card shows his line this game); **snap grades** by your position coaches (the Grades panel: this snap's grade for each of your men on the field, plus, 0 or minus with the reasons, from the engine's own record of the play: blocks won and lost, busts, missed tackles, drops, penalties, sacks, pressures; the game sheet by position group with each man's snaps, plus/zero/minus, grade, busts and loafs, his top notes and a link to his snaps).
- **Live, during game day:** the live screen's field gets a "Film" box beside the field chart. Each snap since your last call plays in 2D as it happens (or in 3D with the setting on), and Watch mode waits for the film before the next snap; at key-play pace only the key plays play, the others pass on the field chart. R (or the Film button) opens the game so far in the full film room.
- **Keys in the film room:** Space or K plays and pauses, ←/→ a tenth of a second (Shift for the snap before or after; ↑/↓ in full screen), [ and ] the speed, 1-4 the camera, V 2D/3D, F full screen, D draw, Z undo, I isolate, R replay the snap, P folds the side panel away for a wider film, Esc steps back (tool, isolate, pick, full screen). Space doesn't Continue while the film has the keys.

### Decided in the final build (Claude's pick)

- **The first time the film room opens** it opens in 3D (after that, in the view you used last); the game day box follows the same view.
- **The tracking tiles** (time to throw, separation, the carrier's speed) build once and then change only their numbers, four times a second of film for the live ones. The carrier's tile waits until he runs with it: a quarterback dropping back isn't carrying it yet.
- **Ends of the field:** the home team attacks the right end in the 1st and 3rd quarters (and overtime), as the coin toss's ends usually fall; the engine plays every snap toward one end.
- **Kicks:** a kickoff or punt with a return plays from the engine's tracking of the return. One settled without a return (a touchback, a fair catch, a punt downed, an onside kick, a block), a field goal and a try are drawn from the play's own record (the kicker, the distance, the hang, the returner, where it ended), with both units in their real spots: the kickoff coverage in lanes across the width (from 2024 the NFL's dynamic kickoff, both lines set five yards apart until the ball lands), the punt team's gunners, shield and line against the return's jammers and rushers, a returner who kneels in the end zone or signals the fair catch.
- **The midfield mark:** the home team's monogram, as real fields carry one: an NFL club's city (KC, NY, SF, a lone C for Chicago), a college's own acronym (UCLA, LSU) or block letter (M for Michigan, O for Ohio State), a high school's initials.
- **Jersey numbers:** the numbers each team wears on its roster pages and jersey lists (the stories' numbers: each era's rules, veterans choosing first, retired numbers out of circulation), so a man wears the same number in the film as on his page. A man the team no longer lists (gone since the game) gets one by each level's real rules (NFL since 2021, the 1973 rules before, 0 from 2023; college and high school any number but the interior line in 50-79) from the numbers left, the same every time (his id picks it). A college or high school roster bigger than its numbers doubles up the way real ones do: an offensive and a defensive player share one, never two men on one side of the ball (lane 8's merge, 2026-10-23: the film had given out its own numbers, and a 107-man roster put eight men in 99).
- **The venue:** an NFL club's real stadium (seats, roof, surface); a college's stadium shaped by its real capacity (80,000 and more a bowl; 45,000 a bowl or a horseshoe; 15,000 a horseshoe or two sides; smaller, bleachers), turf at about six in ten; a high school's home bleachers sized by its enrollment, resources and standing (400 to 14,000 seats). The light comes from the game's own kickoff time on the calendar (the NFL's and college's slots, in local time) against that month's sunset; Friday nights at 7 under the lights at a high school.
- **A loaf:** a free man of yours (running, standing, in coverage, rushing) 6 to 30 yards from a carrier going 4+ m/s who moves under 1.2 m/s for 1.5 seconds. It costs 0.8 of a grade and goes on the sheet.
- **Snap grades:** the play's own grade (the engine's) plus blocks won (0.15 pass, 0.35 run) and lost (0.4, 0.7 with pressure), beating a block (0.4), a bust (1), a missed tackle (0.6), a forced fumble (+1), a fumble (1.5) and a loaf; above +0.25 is a plus, below -0.25 a minus. A man's game grade is the share of his snaps that were plus or zero.
- **Officials:** the level's crew (seven in the NFL; eight in major college from 2023, the center judge added; five at a high school) at their mechanics' spots, moving with the play, and a flag thrown by the official nearest the foul; the chain crew on the far sideline, the rods at the series' start and the line to gain, the box at the ball.
- **Bodies move by their action** (stance, run by speed, block, rush, cover and backpedal, the throw, the catch, the carry, the tackle and the fall), drawn from the frame's action and facing; no motion capture.
- **The live film waits:** during Watch the next snap waits for the film of the last one; key-play pace skips the film for the other snaps.
- **Old films:** a kept game that no longer plays back the same (an engine change) shows a plain message rather than a different game.

## Money and the other leagues (final build, lane 4)

  - **Teams:** every club with its seasons.
  - **Rules:** the year's rule book in plain words.
  - **Draft (the CFL only):** each year's picks with their U Sports schools.
- **An other-league club's page** (`league_team`, also through any team link): its staff, roster (nationals marked under the CFL), seasons, games and upcoming schedule, record and pay.
- **Player pages:** a player's other-league seasons sit in his Stats with his college and NFL lines, sorted by year. "Now" names his club when he plays in another league. A Canadian's hometown and school come from his U Sports program.

#### Claude's picks (proposed, not confirmed)

- The services' underclass updates on 21 January, 12 May, 14 July and 20 October. A first sophomore list reaches 60% of a full class's 5- and 4-stars, 25% of its 3-stars and no 2-stars. After the sophomore spring and summer it reaches 100%, 90%, 50% and 10%. A junior class carries all its 5- and 4-stars, 70% of its 3-stars and 25% of its 2-stars until the signing stages take over at its first commitment wave (20 March of the junior year).
- Who the services look at for an underclass update: the top 8% of the class by film, plus every kid with stars, camp stars, all-state, an offer or a commitment. Classes under 10 kids are skipped.
- The composite bands (5 stars from .9834, 4 from .89, 3 from .797, 2 from .70) and each service's lean (a lasting misread of about 0.012-0.04 on the composite scale, a little of each update's own, plus its known bias: big programs' kids, viral speed, size or production).
- The insiders: picks on the 4- and 5-stars and on 3-stars with six or more offers, in the two classes signing next. A first pick needs the leader 0.08 ahead (0.025 within 75 days of the decision), a change needs 0.06. Confidence runs 50% plus 550 × the gap, up to 95%. Top lists come out within 90 days of the decision (10, 8 or 5 schools by how many recruit him), cut to 5 or 3 in the final month. When his stock moves and new offers come, a pick on a school out of his top three is moved like a fresh pick or taken back, and a list his new favorite isn't on (or that has mostly fallen away) is dropped: he has reopened and names a new group when his decision nears again. Insiders keep six years of records.
- The search's "how sure": a fair read has a spread of 6 rating points or less, a narrow one 3.5 or less.
- Verified measurements: your own camp's, or a showcase's for a kid with 3 stars or more (or camp stars) whose family can travel (a 25% chance a year plus 55% times the family's money). An NFL prospect's are verified after the combine, or after his pro day (spread over the pro day weeks by school). Self-reported marks run as the film shows them.
- Other sports' public marks (track 100 m, 200 m, hurdles and shot put, basketball points, wrestling wins) follow his athletic tools with some spread, like a meet or box score.
- Schedule strength for a high schooler comes from his school's classification against the biggest in his state and the state's football culture (1-10). For a college player it's his last season's opponents.
- A recruiting profile's GPA reads 0.1-0.25 high until your academic staff has his transcript. Core courses behind the NCAA's 16: none from a 2.6 GPA; below 2.2 two short seven times in ten; otherwise one short about half the time. Academic risk words: high when he doesn't qualify, some when he qualifies but is behind or under 2.6, none otherwise.
- The frame read: room to add = his grown frame weight plus 6 lb a year until 21, plus part of the gap to his position's combine weight, minus his weight now, read with a spread from 12 lb down to 3 lb by the best judge's eye. The words: filled out (under 5 lb), some room (5 to 15), room to grow (15 to 30) and lots of room (30 and up).
- Your camp: an invite for a high school kid on your board or with a file, within 400 miles, until the third Saturday of June. NIL value from the NIL era on, priced the way the public valuations are: $3,000 times e to the power 17 × (composite − .70), so about $3,000 at .70, $16,000 at the 3-star line, $76,000 at the 4-star line and $370,000 at the 5-star line (by stars when he has no composite); times his position (quarterback 1.8; receiver, back and edge 1.1; corner 1.0; tackle 0.85; interior line 0.75; specialists 0.4; the rest 0.9); plus $1.20 a follower, rounded to $1,000.
- "You vs the services": your staff's ceiling tier against his stars, a tier apart or more. "You vs the desks" (NFL): 12 places or a third of your rank from the consensus.
- The chart's cap of 600 players, Compare's limit of five, and 50 rows a page.
- The Overview fits a 1440-pixel window (a 13-inch MacBook's) without scrolling sideways: every fact in the mock-up stays, but the school and hometown sit under his name, his class under his position, the move beside his national rank and his weight under his height; long headers wrap; "You vs the services" (and the NFL's "You vs the desks") reads Higher, Lower or Level (the full words on hover); the board button reads "+ Board".

## Doubts

- Memory in season is 3.8 GB, under the agreed 4 GB, with a brief high of 4.0 GB in the summer (see "Memory (final build lane 1)"). Measured on Linux; a Mac's allocator keeps and returns memory differently, so the Mac figure may differ a little.
- Continuing the same career twice from the same world file gives the same season: scores, players, news and the depth chart match stop for stop (checked 2026-10-22 on college, NFL and high school seats). An earlier note here said it didn't; the memory check's fingerprint printed each box score's player lines in hash map order, which changes run to run. The fingerprint now sorts them by player id.
- Game day (lane 2): the first game of a season had no opponent film; since the first post-release update it has last season's (above). In one browser game an FCS team drew 16 accepted flags against Alabama: the engine's discipline against a much better level may run hot (not measured).

- The services' stars churn more than real lists between updates (lane 7's browser check, seed 7 at scale 0.05): of the class of 2029's 38 3-stars and up in April of its sophomore year, 16 were down to 2 stars or off the list by August (two updates); in the class of 2028, rated over the same months by the signing stages' summer waves (older than lane 7), 74 of its 124 April 3-stars and up were down to 2 stars or off the list, and its April 5-star was a 3-star. Each look drifts halfway toward a fresh read of his ceiling (noise about 7 points at 16) and his ceiling itself moves as he grows, and the star tiers are thin at the top. Real services rarely move a kid two stars between updates. A stickier read, or a smaller step for a kid already ranked, would be closer to life; left for Raichel's call (not changed).
- A scouting file counts as seen from its first look, before any report is written (`lane_scouting::scout_file`, `seen`; the reports' lag is `file::read`'s `today` filter): a prospect whose only looks are last week's all-star reports lists them, reads no rating and shows the services' word for his overall. Seen in lane 7's review checks (the scouting test now asks for ratings from a file near the top of the board); older than lane 7, not changed.
- Storing ratings as 4-byte floats changes results per seed slightly (rounding), so a seed no longer rebuilds a pre-build world exactly. Accepted because the section 8 build changes the world anyway.
- Season records keep 8-byte values (an exact-size list of 136-byte rows since the final build); making the values 4-byte would move results per seed, so it waits for a build that may move them.
- Engine stats: a throwaway is no longer a target for any receiver, and a sack with no rusher on the quarterback (he went down to a tackle) goes to the tackler, so the box score's sacks match the play by play.
