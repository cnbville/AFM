Live talks are typed lines to your players, recruits and staff, answered in character by Claude Haiku 5.5, on top of the chips and the line bank, which always still work. The rule under all of it: the AI only reads your words and writes theirs; the game decides every result. The design is `afm/live-talks/proposal.md` in the project folder; this page says what the code does.

## Quick answers

- Which model do live talks use? Claude Haiku 5.5, `claude-haiku-5-5`, thinking off, structured output when a schema is given, the fixed rules marked for the cache (`crates/afm-ai/src/client.rs`, `MODEL`, `Request::body`).
- Where is the API key kept? In memory on `Game.ai`, and on a Mac also in the login Keychain (service "AFM"). Never in the world, a save, a world file, a log, the game folder or a screen (`crates/afm-game/src/live_talk/key.rs`, `Ai`; `app/src-tauri/src/keychain.rs`).
- Who reads the key from the Keychain? The app, once at start, and it hands the key to the game. The app saves it when `ai_key_set` works and removes it on `ai_key_clear`, with the game unlocked, since macOS may stop to ask the person; `kept` reads "keychain" only after a save worked (`app/src-tauri/src/main.rs`, `main`, `follow_keychain`, `keychain_status`).
- Does a key survive an app update? Not checked yet. The app is ad hoc signed, so macOS may ask for the Mac password again after an update. It is the first check on Cain's Mac.
- What does the browser build use? `AFM_AI_KEY` for a key, a name of the game's own. `AFM_AI_FAKE=1` plays every call on the fake instead, with no key and no cost (`crates/afm-game/src/bin/afm_play.rs`, `live_talk_from_env`). The desktop app never reads either.
- How long can a call take? 6 seconds in all, connecting and reading the answer included; 30 seconds when the call has a schema (the typed-question reader), because the API compiles a schema the first time it sees it and a typed line timed out at 6 s on 2026-11-14 (`SCHEMA_TIMEOUT`). A redirect is never followed, so the key goes only to the address the client was built with (`crates/afm-ai/src/client.rs`, `TIMEOUT`, `SCHEMA_TIMEOUT`, `Real::build`).
- What counts as a failure? No key, no network, a timeout, an API error, a refusal, the credit or the key's own limit gone, the game's cap reached, 32 questions already open, or an answer that can't be used (cut off, empty, not the JSON asked for). All of them mean "play on the chips" (`crates/afm-ai/src/client.rs`, `Failure`).
- What is the cap? $50 a month, by calendar month in UTC, counted from each reply's `usage` at list price. At the cap every call fails before it is sent (`crates/afm-ai/src/spend.rs`, `CAP_NANO`; `crates/afm-ai/src/lib.rs`, `Client::call`).
- Where is the spend kept? `ai-spend.json` in the game folder, written to `.partial` and renamed. The fake's spend goes to `ai-spend-fake.json` so a test run never counts against the real month (`spend.rs`, `Spend`; `live_talk/key.rs`, `use_fake`).
- Is a refusal paid for? Yes: any reply with `usage` is counted, a refusal and a cut-off reply included, before it is turned into a failure (`crates/afm-ai/src/lib.rs`, `Client::call`).
- Can the game call a model with no price in the table? No. It fails before anything is sent, so the cap can't miss a cost (`spend.rs`, `Price::of`).
- Does a call run inside a command? No. A command returns a ticket at once, a worker thread makes the call, and `ai_poll {ticket}` hands the result back under the game lock (`live_talk/tickets.rs`, `start`, `poll`).
- What happens after a failure that will repeat (the API refuses the key, the credit is gone)? The boxes stay hidden (`usable` is false) until a new key or a good Test (`live_talk/key.rs`, `note_failure`, `why_not`).
- Does a reply change? Only with a usable key: a scene that takes a typed line gets `say`, and a poll's nested reply gets it too (`live_talk/mod.rs`, `after`; `say/mod.rs`, `flag`).
- Can a typed line do something a chip can't? No. It lands on one of the scene's own options through the chip's code, and the bank picks its line with the same seeds (`world/folk/scene.rs`, `say`, `land`).
- Can a typed line fire a coach without asking? No. A pick that can't be undone comes back as one reading to confirm, and `scene::say` refuses it unconfirmed (`world/folk/finals.rs`; `scene.rs`, `asks_first`).
- What if the AI fails mid-talk? The box hides and the chips go on; if the writer fails, the bank's line shows (`live_talk/say/mod.rs`, `reader_step`, `writer_step`).
- Can I type in a one-on-one? Yes (lane 9). Your line lands on a move on the table in the tone you said it, or turns the talk to another of his points as its chip does and lands the move there. A promise asks first. A fact he can check as you say it is checked, and a false one costs what a move against the facts costs (`live_talk/say/one_on_one.rs`; `world/folk/talks.rs`, `one_on_one_say`, `typed_turn`, `typed_against`).
- Is "you're my starter" typed to your player checked against the depth chart? No: players never see the chart. It is heard as a promise about what he'll see and handed to the seam lane P1 fills (`world/folk/claims/truth.rs`, `standing_promise`; `talks.rs`, `typed_promise`).
- Can I type a team talk before kickoff or at halftime? Yes, through `talk_typed`, which reads your line into the room's own move, tone, subject and who and runs the chip's command. A pregame talk can be changed until the team is on the field; the halftime talk always shows its reading first and is said once (`live_talk/say/rooms.rs`).
- Is a lie judged? Yes, on the day he can see it, at a broken promise's cost scaled by his belief (`world/folk/claims/truth.rs`, `check_lies`, `judge`). Only lane 10 files lies so far: a NIL number a recruit can check only at signing (`claims/check/recruit.rs`).
- What if I tell a recruit something false he can check on the spot (titles, last season, draft picks, who comes back at his spot)? It is never saved. The point adds nothing to your case, the oversell costs 0.006 off his lean and your name on the trail drops 0.02 (`claims/check/recruit.rs`, `landed`, `CAUGHT_TRAIL`).
- Can I promise a recruit something by typing it? Yes: the line lands on the promise it means (a start, a role, his position, an early chance or no redshirt; a starting job or a real role for a transfer), asked first, the same promise as the chip (`world/folk/pitch.rs`, `typed_promise`; `live_talk/say/recruit.rs`, `heard`).

## Lane 1, the pipe

### The commands

- `ai_status`: what the settings card shows. `key_set`, `key_ending` ("...abcd", never more), `kept` ("keychain" or "memory", when a key is set), `fake`, `usable` (a box may show), `month` ("2026-11"), `spent` (dollars), `cap` (50), `cap_reached`, `calls` (this month), and `note` (one plain line when something is wrong: a lasting failure, the cap, the last failure, or an unreadable spend file) (`live_talk/key.rs`, `status`).
- `ai_key_set {key}`: checks the shape (`sk-ant-`, letters, digits, `-` and `_`, 20 to 300 characters, trimmed first) and keeps the key in memory. It answers with the status. In the app the reply may also carry `keychain_note`, a plain line, when the Keychain would not take the key (`crates/afm-ai/src/client.rs`, `Key::parse`; `app/src-tauri/src/main.rs`, `follow_keychain`).
- `ai_key_clear`: forgets the key, and the app removes it from the Keychain.
- `ai_test`: one call the size of a typed line (the scene reader's rules and schema with a short card, 400 tokens at most), to see that the key is accepted and to show the seconds a typed line takes; it also warms that reader's schema at the API (it was a 16-token plain call that answered in 0.8 s while typed lines timed out at 6 s, 2026-11-14). It answers `{"ticket": n}`; the poll's `done` carries `text` ("The key works."), `seconds`, `cost` and a fresh `status`. It goes through even after a lasting failure, since a good test is how that is cleared (`live_talk/mod.rs`, `test`).
- `ai_poll {ticket}`: "wait" until the call is back, then the ticket's answer in the contract's shapes. A ticket is answered once; an unknown or old one answers `off` with "That answer is gone." (`live_talk/tickets.rs`, `poll`).
- `ai_demo {state?}`: on the fake only. With no state it lists the states and the flags a reply carries (the contract's `say`, `ask`, `facts`, `sources`). With a state (`wait`, `unsure`, `done`, `none`, `off`, `cap`, `table`, `text`, `reload`, `checks`, `ask_first`) it gives a ticket that answers "wait" twice and then that state's answer (`live_talk/demo.rs`).

### Tickets

- A ticket is a worker thread, a slot for its result and a next step. `start` refuses (before anything is sent) when there is no client, the cap is reached, a lasting failure stands (unless forced), or 32 tickets are open (`tickets.rs`, `start`, `MAX_OPEN`).
- The next step runs under the game lock when `ai_poll` finds the result. It returns `Step::Done(answer)` or `Step::Call(request, next)`, which starts a second call on the same ticket. A typed line is the reader, the game's step, then the writer; the core fills those steps (`tickets.rs`, `Step`, `Next`).
- A panic in a call becomes an API failure, so a ticket never waits for ever. A ticket is dropped 3 minutes after its call started (a second call in the chain starts the count again); a call times out in 6 seconds (30 with a schema), so only a ticket nobody came back for is ever dropped (`tickets.rs`, `spawn`, `TTL`, `prune`).
- Tickets live on `Game.ai` and are many at once; `Game.job` holds one job and refuses a second, which is why this is its own map.

### The client

- `Model` has one call. The real client is on `ureq` and sends one POST to `https://api.anthropic.com/v1/messages` with the headers `x-api-key` and `anthropic-version: 2023-06-01`. The base URL is written out because thread machines set `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` (`crates/afm-ai/src/client.rs`, `Real`, `BASE_URL`, `VERSION`).
- The body: model, `max_tokens`, `thinking: {"type": "disabled"}` (Sonnet 5.5, which answers that with a 400, gets `{"type": "between_tools"}`; no lane uses Sonnet yet), the rules as the system text (marked `cache_control: ephemeral`), the card and line as the user turn, and `output_config.format` with the schema when there is one (`client.rs`, `Request::body`).
- HTTP errors become failures: 401 and 403 "The key was not accepted"; 429 and 5xx "busy"; a message that names the credit balance or a usage limit is "credit gone"; the key is taken out of any message (`client.rs`, `status_failure`, `Key::scrub`). Any other API error shows the API's own words on screen, "The AI answered with an error (400: ...)", so a call that fails only on the real API can be read from the page (`client.rs`, `api_note`). A call that never got an answer is a timeout if it timed out, else no network.
- A reply that came back but can't be used (a refusal, cut off at the token limit, empty, or not the JSON asked for) is a failure after it is counted (`client.rs`, `Reply::classify`).
- The fake plays recorded replies by the request's label ("test", "reader", "reader_first", "writer"), queued replies and failures first. Its recordings `test`, `reader` and `reader_first` are real Haiku 5.5 replies, kept from lane 1's real test on 2026-11-13; `writer` is written by hand from the proposal's card until a lane records a real one (`crates/afm-ai/recordings/`). It keeps every request it was asked. `AFM_AI_FAKE=1` gives it 0.7 s of latency so a screen can show its waiting state (`crates/afm-ai/src/fake.rs`).
- With the fake in use the boxes are on without a key (`usable` is true). Without it a key is needed.

### Spend

Prices are per million tokens. Money is counted in billionths of a dollar.

| Model | Input | Cache read | Write, 5 min | Write, 1 hour | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude Haiku 5.5, prompt up to 100,000 tokens | $0.10 | $0.01 | $0.125 | $0.20 | $0.50 |
| Claude Haiku 5.5, prompt over 100,000 tokens | $0.50 | $0.05 | $0.625 | $1.00 | $2.50 |
| Claude Sonnet 5.5 (build-side tools only) | $2 | $0.10 | $2.50 | $4 | $10 |

(`crates/afm-ai/src/spend.rs`, `Price::of`, `Price::cost`, `LONG_PROMPT`.) Source: the claude-api skill's model table and its Haiku 5.5 notes, read 2026-11-13. The cache writes are 1.25 times and 2 times the input price. A prompt is the fresh input plus the cache read plus the cache writes.

- The book keeps the last 24 months. A file that can't be read is set aside as `ai-spend.bad` and counting starts again from zero, with a note (`spend.rs`, `Spend::open`, `KEEP_MONTHS`).
- A call that fails with no reply (a timeout, no network) is not counted: nothing says what it cost.

## Lane 2, the boxes

Built on 2026-11-13 (lane 2, screens only: `app/ui/screens/ai.js`, `say.js`, `ask.js`, `ai.css`, and one call at each place). The frames are lane 0's, built as drawn (pick 19). No Rust changed, no API call is made by these files: a box only sends the command its reply's flag names, then asks `ai_poll`.

### What a place does

- One call puts a box on a screen: `aiSayBox(reply, key, options)` for the talk box, `aiAskBox(reply.ask, kinds, key, options)` for the ask box, `aiFacts(reply, key, options)` for the fact chips, `aiScoutPanel(file)` for the scout file, `aiSettingsPanel()` for the settings card. A box shows only when the reply carries its flag (`say`, an `ask` entry of the place's kind, `facts`, `sources`), or the demo switch is on and the place's own preset stands in. No flag, no box and no gap (`ai.js`, `aiFlag`; `ask.js`, `aiAskItems`).
- The box's state is kept by a key per talk or page (`ai.js`, `aiBox`), so a screen that draws again finds it as it was. It is not saved, and a typed line is never kept anywhere but the game's own log.
- The places: the scene (`people.js`, `screens.people_scene`, key `scene:<id>`) and the face to face one-on-one (`talk.js`, `tkFaceToFace`, same key); the pregame and halftime room (`talk.js`, `tkChoose`, key `talk:<moment>`); the contract talk (`fo-nfl.js`, `fo_talk`, key `fo:<id>`) and the trade call (`fo_trades`, key `fo-trade`); the NIL promise on a recruit's file (`fo-college.js`, `fcActions`, key `nil:<id>`); the stat finder (`trends.js`, key `statfind`); Find recruits and Find prospects (`find.js`, keys `find:college`, `find:nfl`); the squad bar (`quickfilter.js`, `qfBar`, now `qfBar(scope, groups, rows, shown, ask)`, called with `x.ask` from `app.js`, `training.js` and `squadboards.js`); the next opponent (`desk.js`, `screens.nextopp`); the scout file's Summary tab (`scouting.js`, `scFileSummary` plus `aiScoutPanel`); the leads mail (`core.js`, `showLetter`, `letterHtml`); the settings card (`core.js`, `screens.settings`, before the Logos panel, and `pregame.js`, the settings dialog).

### The states of a box

Ready and typing (a counter out of the flag's `limit`, default 400; Enter sends, Shift+Enter is a new line; the button wakes with text), wait (the words stay, the button waits, one line says who is reading), unsure (two or more readings to pick from, "Say it this way" or "Say it another way"; one reading, which a step that can't be undone always has, is a confirm card with "Do it" and "Not that"), none (the note in bold, "Your words are still in the box"), off (one note in place of the strip, once; after that no box), done. A pick sends the box's `cmd` again with `{ticket, pick}`; the answer may be a new ticket, an answer in the poll's shapes, or the page's normal reply, and the box takes any of them (`say.js`, `aiPicked`; `ai.js`, `aiSettle`).

Done, for a talk box: the box clears; "Heard as" and the marks go under your line in the talk's log (matched by the text you typed, or read from `heard` and `marks` on the log's own line when the core puts them there: `ai.js`, `aiYours`); the page draws again from its own reply, or runs the place's `apply` (a contract talk takes `reply.answer` as the agent's answer, as an offer's reply does: `fo-nfl.js`, `foAiApply`). On a page with no log of its own, or in the demo, the box shows your line, how it was heard and `reply.text` under it.

Done, for an ask box: a `query` is handed to the page's own filters and the page runs the search (the stat finder, Find and the squad bar take it, in `ask.js`, `aiQueryStat`, `aiQueryFind`, `aiQuerySquad`), with the set fields lit gold and a note under the strip; without such a page (the opponent card) the chips, the `line` and the `table` show in the box; `text` shows as an answer card by the scout, the source or "Scouting report"; `reload` draws the page again.

### Added fields (proposed, not confirmed; all optional, so lanes 3 to 10 may ignore them)

- A `query` entry is `{field, value}` with optional `op`, `v2`, `label` and `words` (what the chip says). The stat finder takes `field` as one of `find, level, division, state, from, to, pos, class, rs, age_min, age_max, min_len` and a condition as `{field: "cond", stat, op, value}`; Find takes `pos, class, level, place, q, sure, unseen` and any field of its catalog (`op` one of `ge, le, between, is`); the squad bar takes `pos`, `q` and any field of its catalog (`op` one of `<, ≤, =, ≥, >`).
- A mark may carry `when` ("Saturday": "Checked Saturday"). A reading may carry `why` (the line on the confirm card, "It goes on his record, and the room sees it"; else "It can't be taken back once it is done.").
- An `ask` entry may carry `label`, `placeholder`, `caption`, `name`, `role`, `by`, `byNote`, `wait` (the wait line; the leads mail's default is "<first name> is picking the best few and writing why.") and `rerun {cmd, args}`: without `rerun` the opponent card's chips are read-only (the frame draws them with a ×), since only the AI can read a new question.
- A report's `text` may hold headings as lines that start with `## `.
- The leads mail's `letter` reply carries `ask` with the `auto` entry; the page makes the call once as the mail opens and reads the mail again on `reload`.

### The demo switch

Off by default, and only while `ai_status.fake` is true (`AFM_AI_FAKE=1`): the browser build on a real key never meets it. Settings has a Demo row (or add `?aidemo` to the address). While it is on, every place shows its box against its preset flag, every call goes to `ai_demo` with the state the bar at the foot of the window names, a fact chip or the sources row only marks itself, and the leads preset runs on any memo (`ai.js`, `aiDevOn`, `aiDevPaint`, `aiCall`).

### The settings card

`ai_status`, `ai_key_set` (the key field is a password field; Enter saves, also inside the pregame dialog), `ai_key_clear`, `ai_test` (the card says "Works. A call the size of a typed line, 1.2 s." or the refusal's line) and `ai_checks` (rows with a bar and a tick at the bar to clear; amber only under the tick; cost and seconds) (`ai.js`, `aiCardHtml`, `aiCardAct`). With no key the card also says the game plays on the chips. With a key and the cap reached it says so with the day the cap resets.

### Sizes (proposed, not confirmed)

The text field grows up to 140 px; the poll asks every 450 ms (a poll stops when its box is no longer the one kept for its key); the ask box's send button says "Ask" (an `ask` entry with a `button` is a one-button box that says it, "Write the report", then "Write it again"); the demo bar floats at the bottom right and a page keeps 96 px under its last row while it is on; the confirm card's default line, the "off" note's tail ("The chips work as before." and the filters', bar's and file's versions) and every box's own words are lane 0's where the frame has them and mine where it has not.

## Lane 3, the core

Built 2026-11-13. A typed line lands on one of a scene's own options through the same code as the chip, so the line bank still picks its line with the same seeds and the world after is the chip's. Your words go on your line for that pick, never on a second line.

### A typed line, step by step

- `people_say {id, text}` checks the meeting (open, not over, a kind that takes a typed line, at most 400 characters), builds the reader's card from the scene's own options and returns a ticket. No call runs in the command (`crates/afm-game/src/live_talk/say/mod.rs`, `people_say`).
- The reader (label `scene_reader`, Haiku 5.5, structured output, at most 400 tokens) answers with an option id (o1 to o12, or none), a close second when unsure, a tone, a promise as read, the facts stated, the people named and its fit: clear, unsure or not a line. The rules and the schema are fixed for every scene, so the cache holds (`say/mod.rs`, `READER_RULES`, `reader_schema`).
- The reader also says, in at most 20 plain words, what your line said that the option doesn't: your plan, your numbers, the situations you named ("Aggressive offense, a huge number of kicks, kicking from the 50-yard line"). It is display and the writer's card only: the game decides nothing from it. It is kept only when it passes the reply filter against your own words (no number you didn't say) and is at most 160 characters (`say/mod.rs`, `beyond`, `BEYOND_CHARS`). "Heard as" shows it after the option and the tone, "... firm. Also heard: ..." (`heard_full`), on the done answer and on each reading of an unsure one. The writer's card carries it as "What the coach said beyond that, as he heard it", and the writer's rules tell him to react to your specifics the way a real player would, with the game's answer deciding how he takes them (`card.rs`, `Card::beyond`; `WRITER_RULES`).
- The reader's rules count a promise said without the word: telling a player what he will get (the job, the ball, carries, targets, the kicks, the snaps, a green light, a package) commits you to it, even as "I want" or "I'm planning" (`READER_RULES`). A recruit's promise then lands on one of his five (lane 10, below). Checked live on 2026-11-14 with Haiku 5.5: the kicker line "I'm foreseeing a huge amount of kicks for you. ## Live talks v3, lane N3: deals and requests

Built 2026-11-15 on the shared contract (`docs/design/live-talks-persona.md`, sections 8.2, 8.3, 11, questions 3 and 4). - What can a deal hold? Usage terms (`Offer.terms`, `usage::Usage`) and `terms::Give`: a scholarship (his, or for a brother or friend), money a year, years, guarantees, a start, a real role, a place, a position, no redshirt, a jersey, early enrolment, time away, a tutor, a staff job, a council seat, your word you'll stay, a pick, a favour, money outside the rules. The ear names them by `GIVE_KEYS` and a term by the usage reader's labels (`live_talk/persona`, `EarGive`, `EarTerm`; a chip or double is never a term).
- How does he weigh it? `persona::deals::Deals` (`Valuing`), against his best option (`best`), from the systems that already price him (`deals.rs`, `weigh_in`):
  - A pro: his market price (`people::pay_and_worth`) x (1 + 0.12 x greed z + 0.05 x ego z - 0.06 x loyalty z when he's yours). Your money plus 0.15 x his security weight x guarantees a year plus 0.1 x his price x the other wants met. At his ask or over: yes. Under 0.75 of it: a lowball. Between: a counter at his ask (`pro`).
  - A recruit (a high schooler, or a portal player you recruit): your appeal on his signing-day sums (`recruiting::appeal`, your money in it) plus your other gives as case points (0.02 x how much his camp cares x what it meets, all together at most `case::CASE_TOP`), against his best other school (`best_school`: his offers and his commitment). Over it by 0.005: yes. Money under half his best school's when money matters to his camp: a lowball. Else his counter is the money that would top it (found by halving, up to `ncaa::position_nil_max`), or what he wants most that you didn't give (`recruit`).
  - Your own college player: his lean to the portal. Each want the deal meets pulls it by 0.5 x the want's rank weight (1, 0.6, 0.35, 0.15) x what it meets x belief, the portal meeting's rule made general (`promise::portal_pull`); together at most three quarters off (`cut`). Under 0.25 (the line `talks.rs` reads as a leaver): yes. A man who isn't leaving takes anything he wants (`own`).
  - Anyone else: what the deal meets of his wants (lane N1's `Wanting`) against 0.4 if he has somewhere to go, 0 if not (`other`).
- What does each give meet? `meets`: a start what it adds over his role (`promise::adds`), a real role the same for snaps, money your number over his bar (his price, his usual NIL offer), years over 5, a usage term 0.35 of his role and 0.15 of his career, and so on. A promise is believed as P1 believes it (`promise::belief_own`; a recruit's `belief_out`, a term's `usage::likely`); a thing done at once in full.
- His counter? `counter_for`: his heaviest want the deal meets under 0.6, as a thing the game can do: money, a start (or his position's usual usage term when he starts, `usual_term`), years and guarantees, time away, a tutor, your word, a staff job, a favour, a council seat. Your own player's counter is offered only when it would keep him.
- Taking time? A recruit whose family decides takes 3 days (a handler 5); a pro with an agent 2 (`takes_time`). A deadline inside 7 days makes him answer now, and moves his bar: a pro's ask 4% x ego z, a recruit's 0.005 appeal points x ego z (`pressing`, `deadline_factor`, `DEADLINE_APPEAL`). His counter's money always closes the gap: your first number only raises it. An answer he owes is kept (`memory::Owed`) and weighed again on its day (`due`).
- Anchors? Your first number with him in a season (`memory::Anchor`, kept by `offered`) pulls his ask a tenth of the way to it, at most a tenth of his ask (`anchored`).
- What does a lowball or asking again cost? `cost`: trust -(0.02 + 0.02 x ego z + 0.01 x temper z); each ask after a no, patience -0.15 x (1 + 0.5 x temper z). N4 applies them.
- What does agreeing do? `agree`, after your confirm: your own college player's lean falls by the deal's cut; a start, a role, a position or your word logs a P1 promise, and each usage term a usage promise (`talks::promise`, `set_terms`); time away (`life::grant_leave`) and a council seat happen at once; the deal is kept with your words (`memory::Agreed`). Money, a scholarship, a contract and a pick come back in `left` for their facet lane (N5a recruiting, N5b negotiating).
- Will he do what you ask? `persona::asks::Asks` (`Acting`): a score from 0.5 + 0.5 x (trust in you - 0.5) and what the ask weighs for him; 0.5 or more yes, 0.35 or more he will for something (a role, a council seat, your word), else no (`score`, `price`). A mentor ask: +0.2 x leadership z, +0.12 x professionalism z, -0.1 x ego z, and -0.25 x (1 + ego z) when the kid is a threat to his job (same spot, he starts, the kid within 5 points); never for an older man. Talking to a teammate: leadership and their friendship. Recruiting a friend: his mood (a happy man sells his school). Telling you things: less the closer he is to the man.
- What runs when he agrees? `run`: a mentor ask makes a mentor tie (`ties::mentor`, replacing another; the yearly mentor step then moves the kid's professionalism, down too). A teammate talk or calming one lands with odds 0.35 + 0.25 x leadership z + 0.2 x friendship + 0.15 x the mate's coachability z - 0.15 (0.25 to calm him) x his temper z - 0.1 x his ego z, +0.05 on the council: landed, his mood +0.06 x (1 + leadership z) (the locker room) and their friendship +6; not, his mood -0.04 and the friendship -8 (`ties::strain`), and the report back is honest by professionalism, loyalty and trust. Recruiting a friend or hosting a recruit is a real point in your case's people group by today's pitch rule with the player as the man making it (`case::RATE`; proof from how well he knows him, a step down when he's unhappy; a host's night goes wrong 0.15 - 0.1 x professionalism z of the time, the point floor). Keep quiet marks your secrets with him (`passes_on`: 0.35 - 0.2 x loyalty z - 0.15 x professionalism z + 0.1 x ego z - 0.5 x (trust - 0.5)). Tell me keeps him as a watcher (`memory::Watch`; `tells_you`, a keyed draw: 0.3 + 0.4 x trust - friendship x (0.5 + 0.3 x loyalty z)). A position coach told to watch or work with a man gets your built line, "tell me if he's slipping" or "push him". Every other ask is weighed but run by its facet lane: `run` says so and changes nothing.
- What asks you to confirm? `asks::confirm`: an offer, a decision, and an ask that spends your money, time, staff or a man's health, or changes his role or the plan (`costs_you`). A promise counts when you say it; a favour of goodwill runs when he agrees. `Confirm::words` is the card's line.

Without a key they play on their chips. - The draft side's talks (`draft::scene::start`, game command `draft_talk {with, id, k}`): his family (`family`, a phone call to his parents, once a year, only while he's a college player or declared), your director of security (`security`, in your office), one of your college scouts (`scout`, `k` his place), one of your pro scouts (`pro_scout`), the draft room (`room`, only on the clock), another club's GM (`gm`, `k` the club), and the draft-day call to a man you took or passed on (`call`). Each has its own pick list (codes 801-879) and opens with a line of its own.
- The interview clock (`draft::room`, ready for when the rooms play as v3 talks; today they keep lane 6's path): a typed question in a timed room spends its minutes on the interview topic it's about (a case or rule break is the incident topic; his life, wants, money, NIL and grades the family topic; character, effort, mood, goals and football the football topic; showing film the worst tape), so the file reads it as the picked topic would (`dialogue::spend_topic`). Staying on a topic is a follow-up: it asks the worries your scouts flagged on it and peels his script (below). At zero minutes the talk ends and the room is filed as the chips file it (`dialogue::combine_close`). A follow-up is now allowed after every topic but the whiteboard.
- The whiteboard (`draft::whiteboard`; the whiteboard chip, or the request `whiteboard` in a v3 talk): a prospect in your room goes to the board for 6 minutes of an 18-minute room (scaled to the room, 3 to 20). He gets 0-6 of six real assignments at his spot right (`assignments`: a QB's protection call and hot read, a corner's pattern match, a long snapper's protection...), from 0.6 Football IQ and 0.4 play recognition plus noise. What he had, what he missed and how he put it across (communication bands at 50 and 70) are said. Each session (and the picked whiteboard topic's minutes) reads Football IQ (spread 4), play recognition (5) and communication (6) into your file, tighter with more minutes and with your eye for his spot (0.6 your evaluation, 0.4 your teaching of his group), and the file's overall now is worked out again (`whiteboard::file`, called by `readers::read`).
- Coached answers (`draft::coached`): a declared prospect is prepped for the stock questions: 0.35, plus 0.25 in the consensus top 50, 0.15 at a testing camp, 0.1 x his family's money, 0.05 x professionalism z (0 to 0.85); a recruit on a visit 0.05 + 0.15 x money. The script covers the family, incident and football topics fully, the worst tape half and the whiteboard not at all. A coached topic's traits read up to 1.5 trait points better than the truth (prep x cover), halved for each follow-up on that topic that day (`shade`, in `own::interview_traits`). Your read of people sometimes hears it: "It sounds rehearsed" (odds prep x cover x (0.2 + 0.6 x your sharpness), when prep x cover is 0.2 or more).
- He remembers you (`draft::remember`): how you treated a prospect moves his trust in you, kept by his key (`Mine.trust`), as lane N1 keeps anyone who isn't yours. In the undrafted rush your club's offer weighs his word of you: 1.0 roster place of path per point over 0.5 (`rush_pull`, in `pro::scouts::rush`; 0 for every other club and for a man you never talked to). Once he joins you, his trust in you as his head coach starts half the way toward his word of you, once (Mondays, `week`). A man you had in for a visit or a private workout who went within 40 picks after one of yours to someone else, or went undrafted, loses 0.03 x (1 + ego z) of his word of you (`passed_on`). The draft-day call: welcoming the man you took adds 0.04, telling the man you passed on that it was close 0.03.
- A fix in lane N4's reader: a talk with a player you don't coach read and moved his trust in his own head coach. It now reads and moves his trust in you (`talk::trust_of` and `apply`, through `makeup::trust` and `makeup::coached_by_you`).
- His family (`scene::family_source`): his parents answer on work habits, coaching, character and what drives him as a source in your file (`own::FAMILY_SOURCE`, eye 13, a home visit's kind; they defend him, as a family does), and on his health from what they know (lane N1).
- Your scouting people (`draft::scouts`): your scouts, pro scouts and director of security answer you straight (1.5 minutes a question): only from their own looks in your file, never the truth (a range and his best and worst attribute; his character from their trait reads; the doctors' grade). Security with no background run says so; after it, the incident record, any school suspension or dismissal, and a risk call from the professionalism and temper reads.
- Sending them (request `find_out` or `scout` with a topic, or the picks): a college scout goes to the school (7 days) on character and life, cross-checks him (10) on football, or makes calls (3) on his health; security runs the full background (5); a pro scout gets him on film (3). On its day it becomes his look in your file under his own name (`dept::add`) and his answer comes as a letter ("What you asked about ...").
- The draft room's debate (`draft::debate`, game command `draft_debate`): on the clock, the top 4 men left on your board who are worth the pick each get a case: the reader who reads him highest argues for him from his own read (a man of conviction 15 or more "pounds the table", 6 or less "hedges"), and the lowest says why not when he reads him 2.5 or more overall points lower. The file's flags are said out loud: a character worry (temper read 13 or more, professionalism or work ethic 7 or less) and the doctors' grade. You hear them out, take a man (it turns in the card, `live::pick`; typed, it asks first) or hand it to your staff (`live::auto_pick`).
- Another club's GM (`draft::smoke::gm_says`): asked who he likes, he names a man his club had in for a visit, or, when he's lying, a consensus name they didn't visit. He lies 0.25 + 0.2 x his overconfidence - 0.25 x (his trust in you - 0.5) of the time (0.1 to 0.7), the same answer all week.
- Reading smoke (`draft::smoke::read`, game command `smoke_read {team, id}`, and the war room's leak list): your staff reads a club's interest from what it can see, never whether it was real. It starts at 0.75 for a leak in the press and 0.55 for a GM on the phone, weighed with that club's record over the last 6 drafts you watched (worth 4 drafts of prior); then x3 when your people saw a visit (x0.5 when they saw no real work; they see it 0.35 + 0.4 x your director's network / 20 of the time), x1.3 when they need his spot (x0.8 when they're set), and x1.2 when he fits their slot (x0.6 gone before, x0.7 a reach). "Looks real" at 0.65 or more, "Smells like smoke" at 0.4 or less, else "Could be either", each with its reasons. The war room's leak list no longer shows the truth.
- Planting smoke (request `plant`, or the GM call's picks): with a GM, only his club hears it (`predraft::Leak.to`; `leak_pull` skips a leak meant for another club); with an agent, it gets around the league. After the draft (`smoke::settle`), each club's leaks and what its GM told you are scored against what it did with its pick in hand, for next year's read, and a GM you fed smoke loses 0.03 of his trust in you.
- What's saved (`claims::Live.persona.draft`, `draft::Book`): questions out, whiteboard sessions, presses, GMs' answers, planted smoke, each club's record, the men who thought you'd take them, and who has joined you (400 rows each). Old saves load and write the same.
- Tests: `crates/afm-engine/src/tests/persona_draft.rs`.
- Not built here: typing into Ground's calls (waits for Ground), NFL scouts asking you about your own college players (built as SK19 "your word").
