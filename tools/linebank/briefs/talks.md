# This family: one-on-ones in your office

You, the head coach, call a player into your office (high school: the coaches' office after school; college: your office in the football building; the NFL: your office at the facility) about one subject you picked. The talk is a few short exchanges, and every line you write is his.

- The opening is two parts said as one line: his mood part (`talk.open.sour`, `good` or `flat`), then the subject part (`talk.open.<subject>`), for example "Coach. You wanted me. Is this about the depth chart?" Mood parts never name or hint at a subject (no chart, snaps, money, family, school, the record, the portal, the team's form). Subject parts never assume a mood (he may or may not have a grievance) and never start with a greeting.
- When he asked for the meeting himself to say he means to leave (`talk.open.portal.*`), his opening says why he came, in his mood.
- A reveal (`talk.reveal.*`) is what's really bothering him, once he trusts you: first person, honest, often vulnerable. A deflect is the polite or guarded way he won't open up.
- A reply (`talk.reply.<move>.<outcome>`) is his answer to your move (ask, tell him straight, praise, challenge, criticize, promise, warn, listen, hand it to the leadership council or give him time at home) by how it landed: good, flat or bad. Replies never know the subject, so they never name one. A close (`talk.close.*`) is an optional last sentence after the reply.
- The friend parts are about a close friend who left the team, `{gone}`; `{friend}` is a friend still on the team.

## Who he is

- High school players are 14 to 18, college players 18 to 23, NFL players 21 to 38. Write each level's real life: a high school junior with offers and a curfew, a college redshirt with study hall and an NIL deal, an NFL vet with a contract year and kids at home (never named).
- A bad outcome is honest disappointment, hurt, frustration or a closed door, still in a tone a player uses with his head coach. High school and college players stay respectful in your office: no sarcasm, brush-offs, ultimatums or entitled demands. A line that talks back ("Are we done?", "That's cute.", "Good luck replacing me.", "Put my name at the top and let's move on.") carries `brash`, which only an NFL ego or temper, or a college player with both who doesn't trust you yet, ever has. Never pair `brash` with `hs`, and keep brash lines to about one in eight of a bad outcome's lines.
- Position, stage and personality tags are where the real detail lives: a center talks about calls and the snap, a corner about press and his eyes, a kicker about operation time; a walk-on about earning a scholarship, a psquad player about the 53, a firstround pick about expectations.

## Mix

Roughly: a third untagged; a fifth one level tag (spread over hs, college and nfl); a sixth level plus a position or stage; a fifth one personality tag (spread over all fifteen); a few region lines; a few `young` lines with light slang ("lowkey", "real talk"), never overdone. Mood parts and closes lean on untagged and personality; subject parts and reveals on level, stage and position.
