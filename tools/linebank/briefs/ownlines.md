# This family: your own lines (what the head coach says, the moves you pick)

You are the head coach, and every line you write is YOURS, said to the person across from you: a player in your office (high school, college or the NFL), or a recruit and his family, or a transfer. The game shows each line as a chip the player of the game picks; the line is a move (ask, tell him straight, praise, challenge, criticize, promise, warn, hand him something, listen) or one of your four answers to his question (straight, soften, promise, dodge), or your pitch to a recruit.

## How you sound

- A head coach talking to his own people: direct, specific and human, never a speech and never a lecture. One or two short sentences. Calls him by name only where the part lists `{name}`; never by nickname.
- Respect first. You can be hard, but you are never cruel, never swear and never threaten his family. A criticize move is firm and fair: it says what you saw and what must change, it is never an insult to who he is.
- The tone of the move is part of the line: ask and hand are warm, straight is calm, challenge is firm, criticize is hard, promise is steady and sure, warn is firm and plain, listen is quiet (it says little: it invites him to talk).
- Each line must carry the move's real action, because the game does that action when it is picked: a promise line promises (and says what, in your own words, as the part says), a warn line warns, a hand line gives him something, a listen line says little and hands him the floor. Never put an action in a line that its move doesn't do: a praise line does not promise anything, an ask line makes no demand, a straight line asserts only facts the part lists.
- Level matters. Say what is true at the level of the tag you use: a high school coach talks to a teenager he sees every day; a college coach to a young man with class, NIL, a scholarship and the portal; an NFL coach to a pro with a contract, an agent, a club and a roster spot. Untagged lines must fit all three. Level words follow the general rules (tag `hs`, `college` or `nfl` where a line names something one level has).
- Players never see the depth chart. Never write "the chart", "depth chart", "you're listed", "you're first string on the chart", "where you stand on the chart". Say what he can see: the reps, the snaps, who's playing, what the room looks like, what you saw in practice.
- Never assert what the world may contradict. Only the facts the part lists may state a specific thing (a name, a game, a count, a day, a charge). Never write a day or date of your own. Never name a charge, a punishment or a rule unless its fact is listed. Never state his record, grades or health beyond what the part says.
- Hidden numbers never reach a line: no ratings, no potential, no trust or mood numbers, no "I rate you".
- Never say what a move costs or does inside the game ("this lowers your mood", "that gives me trust").

## Versions and facts

- A line that uses a fact in `{braces}` is said only when the game has that fact, so give most parts both lines that use a fact and lines that don't. A part's branches (the states listed) are separate situations: a line written for one state carries that state's check (`~ why=injury`) and says nothing that is false in the others; a line without a check must be true in every state of the part.
- The part says what today's wording is. Yours must be new and varied, not rewordings of today's line, and not rewordings of each other. Cover the plain ground a real coach would cover: what he'd ask, what he'd say straight, what he'd promise and how he'd phrase it.

## What the parts are

- `talk.point.you.<point>.<move>`: your move on one of his talking points (an arrest, a lost job, a big game, a week of practice...).
- `talk.point.answer.<point>.<straight|soften|promise|dodge>`: your answer to the question he asked you. Straight tells the truth plainly. Soften is kinder than the full truth, but says nothing false. Promise is a promise. Dodge is a polite non-answer that doesn't say he asked something wrong.
- `talk.you.<subject>.<move>`: your move when you call him in about one of his subjects (his role, his touches, his money, his behavior, his life, the team, his future, a friend who left).
- `recruit.pitch.<point>.<tier>`: your pitch to a recruit or a transfer for one thing your program has. The tier is how your proof reads against the other schools on his list (elite, strong, fair, weak). Elite and strong: confident and specific (but never a number the part doesn't list). Fair: honest and warm, not overselling. Weak: you only say what is true and you do not oversell; a recruit can tell. `recruit.pitch.straight.<group>`: when you have no proof in a group (playing time, money, the school, close to home, the people, the league, winning), you are honest that others on his list can offer more there.
- A recruit's side is never written here: you speak to him.
