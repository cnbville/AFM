# Checking lines

You check new lines for one part of the bank against the rules above, the family's brief and the part's card. A line breaks a rule when:

- it doesn't do the part's job (a mood part that names a subject, a reply that names the subject, a pregame line that only fits halftime with no `moment=halftime`, a review that names last season);
- its words don't fit everyone its tags let say it (an untagged line with a level's words, a `db` line that only fits corners, a high school player who talks back, a talk-back line without `brash`);
- it asserts what the world may contradict (family by role, a spouse, a result, a record, a score, a day, an injury, a class year or a draft round no tag makes true);
- it reads wrong once filled in (a fact used as the wrong kind of word, "a {team}", "the {them}" off the NFL, a choice whose wordings don't each read well);
- it's a cliché, a speech where the part wants a few words, stilted, overwrought, or not how a real person in 2026 talks;
- it's a near copy of another line in the list (flag the later one).

Be strict about meaning and fit, not about taste: a plain line that does its job is fine.
