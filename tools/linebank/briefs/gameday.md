# This family: game day

Before the game and at halftime you talk to your team in the locker room, and on the sideline you have a word with a player after a mistake. A paper may quote an opposing player before the game.

- Your talk (`gameday.open.<tone>`, `gameday.<move>`, the optional `gameday.close.<tone>`) is three parts said as one: an opening in your tone, your point, a send-off. Each part must read on its own and join the others. The speaker is you, the head coach: your words to a room of high school kids, college players or NFL pros. You have no spouse or family in the bank, and no traits but your tone (fiery, warm, firm or calm) and level.
- When-checks keep a line to its moment. `moment=pregame` or `moment=halftime`; at halftime `lead=up`, `down` or `level` (the score's margin); `last=win`, `loss` or `none` (last week's game, none in a season's first); `rival=yes` (a rivalry game); and on your point `subject=` us, them, plan, stakes, seniors or one (one player you single out, `{player}`: often a starter who is out hurt, so a line about him never says whether he plays). An untagged line fits every moment, so it can't assume you're ahead, that it's halftime, or that last week went well.
- `gameday.captain` is your line handing the room to a captain, `{captain}`. The captain's own words (`gameday.captain.open`, `own`, `us`, `job`, `break`) are a player talking to his teammates: his own voice (a quiet lineman, a loud receiver, a young quarterback), short, real, never a speech. They follow one another, so each stays inside its own job.
- On the sideline (`gameday.word.*`) you talk to one player, `{player}`, right after a mistake; `mistake=` names it when the line needs it. Ten seconds, between plays, in the tone the part says.
- `gameday.quote` is an opposing player with an ego, quoted in the paper about one of your units: confident, a little disrespectful, quotable, never vulgar, never a guarantee of a score.

## Voice

Locker room speech is short and physical: breath, noise, the next snap. Halftime is about adjustments and the second half; pregame about the plan, the week's work, each other. Fiery is loud and edged, warm is close and personal, firm is clear and demanding, calm is steady and low. High school talks lean on this town, the seniors and Friday nights (never a made-up name); college talks the program, the conference and the rival; NFL talks are professionals at work, the job and the week. Tag the level when the words name one. No movie speeches, no inches, no "leave it all on the field", no promises of a win.
